The row itself is the button. One click, straight into the screen that sets this. No sub-menu, no tab to find first.
The name is plain language. Not the name of the database table, and not the name of the menu tab it happens to live under today.
The evidence line is the honest part. It is counted from what is actually configured, and it tells you what is left without opening anything.
Why not just Complete / Incomplete
Your mock-up had three states, which is the right instinct — but a bare Incomplete tells someone to go looking, and they may open a screen that turns out to be nearly done. Because the system can already count what is configured, the chip can say which part is missing at no extra cost.
There is one thing the evidence cannot answer: whether a person is willing to say this is ready. That is a separate act, and it is what unlocks anything downstream. The three artboards below are three ways to ask for it.
The five states
Four of these are counted by the system and need nobody to maintain them. Only Ready can also depend on a person having signed off — which is the decision the three artboards below are about.
Ready Everything this item needs is set, and where a sign-off is required it has been given. Example: 34 redaction rules, each with a citation, confirmed by R. Ellis on 14 Aug.
In progress Someone has started and part of it is set. The evidence line names the gap. Example: provider set, no test message sent.
Not started Nothing set. Where the system ships with a working default, the evidence line says so — running on defaults is a real answer, not a blank.
Needs attention Was finished, and something has changed since: a proposed rule change is waiting, a rate now exceeds a state limit, a connection that was working has started failing. This state is the reason the page stays useful after go-live instead of becoming a page nobody opens again. Note the difference from In progress — that one has never been finished; this one has come undone.
Waiting Something else has to happen first. The row is dimmed and carries a Why button that explains it in a sentence.
What the Why button opens
Your taxonomy-and-connectors example, drawn two ways. The difference is whether a dependency is a wall or a warning.
Treatment A — locked
Record types and categories is waiting
This step uses your connected record systems to suggest the record types your city actually holds. Until at least one system is connected and has finished a scan, there is nothing for it to read.
Waiting on: Where the records live — Technical Setup, set up by your System Administrator. Two of three systems are connected; Laserfiche has not finished its first scan.
Trade-off. Nobody wastes time in a screen that cannot work yet. But record types can also be typed in by hand, so this blocks a person who does not want the suggestions at all — and in a city with no connectors, it blocks them permanently.
Treatment B — open, with a warning
One part of this step is not available yet
You can add record types by hand right now. What you cannot do yet is have the system suggest them from your own records — that needs a connected record system that has finished a scan.
Waiting on: Where the records live — Technical Setup, set up by your System Administrator.
Trade-off. Nobody is stuck, and a small city with no connectors can still finish setup. But the lane no longer reads top-to-bottom as a sequence, and someone can do work by hand that the scan would have done for them in a minute.
