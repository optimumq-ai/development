Texas law already answers all six questions behind this switch — what the response clock does while a deposit is unpaid, how long the requestor has to pay, and the revised-estimate rules (§ 552.263(e)–(f) · § 552.2615(b)–(c)). The switch decides whether the system acts on them: off, staff see flags and the clock is never touched — a request sitting on an unpaid deposit keeps burning its response window, and the city looks late for the requestor's inaction.
