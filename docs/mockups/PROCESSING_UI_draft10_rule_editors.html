Design annotations

  1. KEVIN 7/29 Each readiness-index row opens a section screen — content · city knobs (Draft 6's confirm cards embedded as a zone, not a separate screen) · provenance · pending proposals. One surface per domain; the 15 renderers share the shell.
  2. Ownership is the permission groups, displayed and enforced: Legal Rules (exemption · redaction · deadline · clock_matrix — Senior Legal owns, Director may propose, SysAdmin technical-only) · Fee Configuration (fee · payment · fee_waiver · approval_modules · ledger) · Workflow & Taxonomy (intake · branches · disposition · clarification · eligibility) · template_import read-only. Mapping is drafted — open question 1.
  3. THE RULE Two kinds of content, two edit paths, one visual grammar. Statute-derived facts: navy solid edge + citation; editing requires citation + note and always creates a proposal. City-policy knobs: dashed amber + suggested-default chip; the owner sets and confirms freely (Draft 6 flow). A reader never wonders which kind they face — that confusion is how a city “edits the law.”
  4. Knobs keep the Draft-6 contract: unconfirmed ⚠ blocks attest; confirming the default is still a decision, recorded as one; the suggestion chip stays visible after confirming.
  5. Wiring is shown, honestly: wired = redaction rules / engine consume this content; content-only = a correct, queryable fact nothing consumes yet — the branch-profile vocabulary applied to content, so nobody mistakes documentation for enforcement.
  6. Read-only states carry their reason — the Team-Supervisor banner restates the do-the-work vs set-the-rules line where it bites, instead of a mute disabled screen.
  7. Every content edit is a proposal — one audit path, no exceptions. The owner's own proposal may be applied in the same act; a Director-may edit on a Legal domain routes to Senior Legal (open question 2). Applying recomputes the content hash → an attested section drifts and demands re-attestation. The drift machinery Draft 6 renders now has its cause.
  8. Deadlines & Clocks is the section where the editor earns its keep: the named-timer table renders kinds in the ClockChip grammar so an operational target can never masquerade as a statutory deadline — even here, in admin-land, where the mistake would be born.
  9. RULE A Kind is not editable prose — it is the compliance taxonomy (response · agency_action · requestor_window · operational_target). Changing a kind is a statute-assertion edit (proposal + citation); the target row is the city's knob.
  10. The editor refuses what the engine would refuse — the WS1–WS3 police rules (response band 1–45 bd, kind required, no primary denial deadline, policed domains never accept guessed enums, unroutable roles refused) run at compose time, with worded refusals, not just red borders.
  11. Empty is honest: a never-imported domain says “No content — import a state template or add the first rule,” never an alarm, never a fabricated default (the rule-b posture, applied to configuration).
