The gate speaks only in quoted numbers. Quoted share = gross × (total ÷ gross subtotal) — one ratio, both directions, no per-vehicle rules (§5.10.2) — fixed at estimate acceptance and never revised mid-flight. Each ready record ships when the running funds balance (paid − quoted shares of records already shipped) covers its quoted share; actual costs accumulate silently for the end. A denied or never-shipped record's share never consumes funds — a denial frees money for its siblings. The row still explains itself to a requestor: “your $150 item was billed $120 because the request total was capped by aggregate pricing.”

The last record settles the request 13

When item 4 becomes the last record ready, every sibling is terminal and its own actuals exist — so the aggregate adjustment is finally computable. Actuals run through the same engine (caps and all) → the adjusted final invoice or refund, exactly as for a non-MRR request:
Balance due → the last record is held until the adjusted invoice is paid — final delivery against final payment, the regime deposits were designed for. Collectible overage is capped by the 20% updated-statement rule (§552.2615): if the mid-flight watchdog never sent an updated statement, the excess is forfeited, and this screen refuses to bill it.
Refund or zero → the last record releases immediately and the refund issues (ORO Finance) — the method never holds a finished record from a requestor who has overpaid.

Design annotations

  1. One screen, one code path. For a single-item request the allocation table is one row where charged = total and the screen reads as a plain statement — the §5.9 no-op identity, visually. Opened from the MRR hub, the request header, or Cash Drawer's receipt line.
  2. The release rule is state config, shown by name. TX: pay_in_full — once charges are paid, anything ready releases. WA (per_installment, the only real one): each installment prices at its own cost and releases on its own coverage — with the sharp statutory edge (an unclaimed installment can close the whole request) modeled and labeled, and the model-rules rewrite flagged for re-check before hard-coding.
  3. RULE C adjacent The statement is evented, never recomputed — same discipline as the class-A ledger: a figure a demand rests on must be reconstructable. Every line carries its actor badge: requestor acts (approval), person acts (credit approval, revised-notice send), system computations (allocation, coverage) with their basis.
  4. Verify ≠ Approve, again. The approval line is the requestor's — purple badge, same family as the AG ruling in Draft 4: an external actor's decision, recorded here. Staff verification never appears as “approved”.
  5. DECIDED §5.10.2 Generalized prorata is the allocation — and it is FROZEN at estimate acceptance. One ratio (total ÷ gross subtotal) scales every item, savings and surcharges alike, no per-vehicle rulebook — and each row carries the requestor-explainable sentence. Gross stays visible beside the quoted share so the delta is never hidden. A page-weighted or vehicle-aware allocation was considered and declined: half the vehicles are surcharges or step functions, and with the gate frozen on quoted numbers, allocation precision no longer affects release.
  6. §5.9 A row is only ever held for its OWN share. Item 1 released while the request was unpaid overall; item 3 is held for the $42 that covers it. The table states the sibling rule on the row where it bites, because this is the line staff will be tempted to “fix” at the counter.
  7. The refund/credit path (new design, closing Draft 2's flag): recompute → evented credit cited to its cause → net against balance while one exists → refund only when credits exceed balance, issued by ORO Finance with method recorded. Credits fold into the next communication (revised notice), never a standalone letter — the one-notice principle applied to money going the other way.
  8. The running funds balance IS cumulative FIFO — paid minus quoted shares of records already shipped, stated on the screen because it is flagged in-code as a prerequisite for the money axis moving to the parent: three $20 items can never all release against $50 paid, and the UI asserts the rule so the build can't quietly ship the per-row version.
  9. Reconciliation: auto-draft, human send. The draft computes itself when the last billable task finalizes (measured labor over quoted quantities); the revised notice send stays a person's act, and the labor delta rides the same communication as any credits.
  10. Authority is visible, not hidden. The RM sees refund state with the button disabled and labeled for ORO Finance — the same worked/decided split as everywhere else: seeing money is the manager's job, moving it out of the city is Finance's.
  11. Other jurisdictions in one line each: OH — actual-cost lines only, no labor anywhere in gross or charged; anonymous requests show no cross-request chip (“does not apply”, rule e). IL — the fee-forfeiture guardrail surfaces here as a warning banner when a response is late: charges may no longer be collectible — money the screen must refuse to demand, not merely note.
  12. Not duplicated here: taking payment (Cash Drawer), estimate line-item editing (Draft 2's screen), the waiver decision (Drafts 1–2), disposition (next draft). This screen is the money's single source of truth, not a second home for any of those acts.
  13. KEVIN 7/28 The MRR settlement method. Quoted pro-rata shares govern every release; actuals never touch the gate; variances accumulate silently. When the last record becomes ready (all siblings terminal, its own actuals in), the aggregate adjustment runs once — the same adjusted invoice/refund as a non-MRR — and the last record is held for that payment, or releases immediately when the adjustment nets to refund or zero. Mid-flight, the only running number is the parent-level 20% overage watchdog feeding the updated-statement (reissue) machinery, which also caps what the final invoice may collect. Honest footnote: a purist §5.9 reading would hold the last record only for its own adjusted share — final-delivery-against-final-payment is the standard regime deposits were designed around, the invoice issues the moment the record is ready, and the 20% rule bounds the exposure, so the method is documented and adopted.

Flagged, not designed here: the refund execution mechanics (card reversal vs check request — city finance-system integration); WA per-installment pricing UI; the org-wide revenue/receivables report.
