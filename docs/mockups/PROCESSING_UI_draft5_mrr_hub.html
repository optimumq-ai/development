Design annotations

  1. KEVIN 7/28 MRR Management is the task. Department routing is always ORO, triggered by MRR status — the classifier's department call plays no part in routing the parent. Assignment among eligible ORO Associates uses the same smart-routing / pool machinery as non-MRR tasks (specialization auto-assign, else claim from pool); each associate holds only their own MRRs. Lands in a My Tasks MRR grouping and stays assigned until every child record is terminal. Replaces the never-built mrr_processing key (new name, new entry, with its spawner this time).
  2. The manager's own child-task assignments appear as their own My Tasks groups — taking a search yourself puts “MRR Search — assigned to me” on your list like anyone else's. Everything worked has a task; nothing is invisible.
  3. KEVIN 7/28 The overview is scoped to the manager's own MRRs. With several eligible associates, each sees only theirs. The all-office view (manager column, read-only) belongs to oversight authority — ORO Supervisor / Director — not to this screen.
  4. Master record view: summary on top, one bar per child below — statutory clock (master-only, navy), tolled days, payment, and the estimate-readiness meter in the summary; each child as a single scannable bar.
  5. Requestor-level checks live on the master card (no intake-review stop for MRR): the same eligibility / ledger / waiver chips as Drafts 1–2, expandable.
  6. KEVIN 7/28 Estimate readiness drives one button. The master card counts “Estimate data: n of m complete”; at m of m, Generate estimate activates and highlights — one estimate for the master record through the standard engine. Staff Verify; the requestor approves (two words, two actors — approval is a statutory trigger in some states).
  7. The bars carry exactly what you said: per-activity status (Queued · In Process · Complete, plus honest Not started / Not required), Assigned to, and tags (Vague, Denial designated → Legal Review, External…). Click a bar to open the child record.
  8. KEVIN 7/28 Child-level defect + denial designation. Mark Vague at the child level (clarification goes out through the RM — one voice); Designate denial → submit for Legal Review sends the item to Draft 4's legal task with grounds attached — designation is not a denial; legal decides, Draft 3 composes the letter.
  9. External contributor: estimate-gathering assigned by email to a non-user via a secure, expiring, single-use link; link state shows on the bar. (Carried from the standing §14.4 design — the AV-specialist case.)
  10. One request, one voice: Contact-requestor exists only on the MRR screens; every assignee-facing task says “email the Request Manager”.
  11. KEVIN 7/28 MRR tasks are NOT flow tasks. mrr_search, mrr_estimate, mrr_redaction (redaction is new to the catalog) are hand-assigned to any person — no team filter, no smart routing, no stage advance. Completing one updates the bar; the manager, not the engine, moves the work along. This is the structural distinction from the non-MRR estimate/search/redaction task types, kept as separate task-type keys so the two can never blur.
  12. The assignee inset makes the distinction visible where it matters: the person doing the work sees a normal-looking task; the difference is what completing it does (updates the bar; advances nothing).

Columbus, OH in one line: the master card's clock strip shows the honest “no statutory deadline” treatment, waiver chip absent (explicit false), ledger chip “anonymous — does not apply” when unanchored; the bars and orchestration are jurisdiction-independent.  Superseded from 5a: the suggest-and-confirm team-routing emphasis and the “Kevin's shape (2026-07-16)” attribution in SPEC_parent_child_lifecycle.md §14.3 — flagged for correction when this design is ratified.
