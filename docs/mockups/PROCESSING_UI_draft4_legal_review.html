Design annotations

  1. One task type, office-level. legal_review is team-agnostic (routing built): tasks from every fulfillment team pool to Senior Legal / Legal Associate by task subset. The same screen serves both stages that spawn it — exemption_review and ag_review — with the work surface swapping by stage and branch profile. legal_redaction stays on the redaction screen (separate task family).
  2. The duty-clock column is the queue's sort key. These are the only tasks whose deadlines carry automatic statutory consequences (§552.302 presumed-public), so the queue leads with the duty and its exposure — not with age or team.
  3. RULE A A tolled clock is shown tolled, with its reason. The response clock renders in a paused treatment (“tolled while the AG holds it · resumes on the ruling”, reason ag_ruling_pending) — never silently hidden, never still counting. The engine already tolls and resumes; the UI's job is to say so.
  4. Not every date is the city's duty. The AG's own 45-business-day ruling window (§552.306) renders in a fourth, informational treatment — dashed, “the AG's clock” — distinct from statutory duties, city targets, and requestor windows. It ages expectations, not obligations.
  5. What-was-asserted / records-at-issue carries each record with its ground and citation — the same per-record spine as Denial compose, arriving pre-filled from the assertion (TX) or the escalation (OH).
  6. The band is a statutory tracker, not a to-do list. Each step is a §552.301 duty with its own date, evidence (letter on file), and history row; the briefing step carries the statute's required-contents checklist (§552.301(e), requestor copy (e-1)). Steps that are met show met, and when — a cleared exposure says so rather than disappearing.
  7. Third-party notice is its own branch-gated panel (§552.305, capability third_party_notice): where active, identifying a third party adds their notice + their right to brief the AG to the band. Null-capability cities render the panel; only explicit false hides it.
  8. RULE C The ruling is an external decision, recorded — not made — here. New badge in the decided-by vocabulary: Decided by · Office of the Attorney General with ruling number and letter attached (same pattern as the OH vexatious list being the court's). Sustained → clock resumes + Denial compose reopens citing the ruling; overruled → clock resumes + records return to redaction → delivery. Both recorded by a person, decided by the AG.
  9. Release is never blocked. Withdraw-assertion-and-release is always available and needs no ruling, no gate, no signature — the asymmetry (blocking needs process, releasing doesn't) runs the whole product and holds here.
  10. RULE B Ohio's tab is the gating proof. No AG column, no referral chips, no band panel — ag_review is in unavailableStages, so the band doesn't exist rather than being hidden per-panel. The legal task becomes the decision point itself: uphold → Draft 3's compose (grounds carried in), release → back to redaction. Unresearched cities (null) render the TX-shaped screen minus state-specific copy.
  11. Escalation provenance (who escalated, why, from which task — which is paused waiting) opens the OH screen, the same way “Path here” opens the estimate: the reviewer's first question is why it's on their desk.
  12. Answer-without-deciding. “Return to redaction with guidance” closes the legal task with advice instead of a determination — most escalations are questions, not denials, and forcing a formal decision on each would manufacture denials. The decision path stays one click away when it is warranted.
