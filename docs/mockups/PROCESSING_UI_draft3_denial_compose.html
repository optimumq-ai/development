Design annotations

  1. A compose surface, not a routed task. Opened from legal review, redaction, intake review (OH deny-too-vague), or the estimate screen's clarification path; Save draft & return keeps the task as home. Denial-shaped advisories arrive here as prefilled drafts: the MA unpaid-balance advisory, a vexatious flag, or an abandonment closure in a via-denial state pre-select the ground with citation — system · raised it, a person · decides it. Auto-denial stays out of scope by decision.
  2. Per-record disposition is the spine. Withhold in full / release with redactions / release in full, each carrying its ground. The letter must account for every withheld record and redaction; released records continue to delivery unaffected. (Open question: how partial denials map onto the child's eight terminal dispositions.)
  3. RULE B adjacent The reason library is per-state config, cited, fail-closed. Binds to the existing decision_reasons substrate (categorized, most-used-first) + the exemption domain. A ground the state lacks is absent, not greyed: OH lists vagueness (with its invite-to-revise duty attached); TX does not list it at all. An unresearched ground ships absent — same rule as overbroad_is_denial_ground in the record-search spec: an unresearched denial ground is legal exposure.
  4. Library texts are versioned and usage-counted (v3, used 12×) so counsel can tune the standard paragraph once and every future denial improves. Editing the letter never edits the library.
  5. Required elements are a per-state checklist that gates Send. Ohio: explanation with legal authority (B)(3), written denial, per-redaction notice (B)(1), remedy language. An unresolved element disables Send and says why in words — same gate pattern as WAIVER_UNDECIDED and the record-search Found gate. Selecting the vagueness ground adds its own element (inform how records are maintained + invite revision, (B)(2)).
  6. The letter is assembled, then owned by a person — generated from library texts and the disposition list, fully editable, re-generated if grounds change (with edits flagged for re-review rather than silently discarded).
  7. RULE C Decided-by, again. The record reads “Denied in part by A. Whitmore · Senior Legal” — with grounds, citations, and the withheld-record trail (Vaughn-style) attached. Never “denied by the system,” even when the draft arrived prefilled.
  8. Send delivers by the request's channel (email / printable postal letter — the clarification outreach pattern), writes the history row, and returns to the originating task.
  9. Authority routing. Senior Legal owns exemption denials (role model §5). A composer without authority drafts everything and gets Route for signature instead of Send — the draft lands on the authorized role's list. Whether OH's procedural vagueness denial needs legal sign-off or stays with the intake reviewer is an open question for Kevin.
  10. RULE A + C The Texas gate. In a pre-clearance state the compose screen's real job is refusing to be a denial screen: exemption grounds route to the AG (10-bd duty clock, § 552.302 presumed-public exposure rendered as a warning on that clock — never its own countdown), with the previous-determination exception checked and shown. The unlawful path isn't disabled — it doesn't exist.
  11. The AG band itself lives on Legal Review (extend item, drafted separately): briefing package, requestor notice, ruling tracker, and the reopen-to-compose-citing-the-ruling flow sketched in the ghost panel.
  12. Illinois, not tabbed but binding: a burden (unduly-burdensome) denial ground carries the prerequisite conference to narrow, offered first (clarification_duty: required_before_burden_denial, seeded, still uncalled) — on this screen that's a required element that gates Send exactly like OH's remedy language, with the estimate attached as the burden evidence. And the forfeiture rule renders as a hard stop: a response past the deadline cannot be denied as unduly burdensome at all — the ground greys out with the reason.
