Design annotations

  1. RULE D The checklist is computed, never asserted. Knob counts from the imported template (confirmed: false + suggested_default), proposals from the review queue, findings from configIntegrity's invariants, attestation from section hashes. The gate summary is the same truth attest() enforces — the screen just makes it visible before someone hits the wall.
  2. Dev mode is the last pill, deliberately. Enforcement is System-Administrator-only and separate from configuration ownership; the summary makes “turn it off” legible as the final, informed act rather than a switch found in a settings page.
  3. Owner ≠ attester, and the mismatch is flagged. Content ownership follows the permission groups (Director: Workflow/Fee; Senior Legal: Legal Rules); but today's ATTEST role is System Admin | Director only. Drafted with Senior Legal attesting the legal sections — this requires widening the attest role, an open question below.
  4. “ACTIVE + unconfirmed parameter” outranks everything. A live program running on an unconfirmed default is worse than an inert one (the configIntegrity comment says exactly this) — it gets the red treatment and sorts to the top of whatever is wrong.
  5. Pending proposals are part of go-live, not a separate chore. The TX WS3 clock proposal (still pending on live) appears here with one-click review through the existing config-proposal flow — the checklist is where a Director discovers it, because until it is approved the city is running pre-Phase-7 clocks.
  6. The knob card is the unit of the whole screen: what the statute left open (the note), the suggested default (dashed amber — visually “not an answer”), the value editor, and Confirm recording name + date. Citations appear where the template carries them; nothing invents one.
  7. Confirming the suggested default is still a decision — the confirmed card keeps the suggestion chip visible so the trail shows the city chose it, not that nobody looked.
  8. Integrity findings ride along but are not knobs. They are invariants that hold regardless of attestation (test stamps, unknown keys, provenance, clock bands, primary clock) — born from the 77-day incident. They surface here because go-live is when someone is finally looking.
  9. Sections the state never imported stay honestly empty — an empty section is not a warning in a city that hasn't adopted that surface; only ACTIVE-unconfirmed and drift are alarms. (Same three-valued honesty as rule (b): absence of research is unknown, not wrong.)
  10. Columbus renders identically — its own 9 warnings, its own owners, no structural difference. This is the one screen in the set that is genuinely jurisdiction-shape-free: the law lives in the knob notes, not the layout.

Flagged, not designed here: the proposal-review screen itself (exists in the config-proposal flow today); per-knob evidence drill-down (template rule ids → research text); the “Re-open…” un-attest confirmation flow.
