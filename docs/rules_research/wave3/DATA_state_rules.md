### Material negatives

- `clarification.deficiency_cure_clock` — OPRA contains no mandatory clarification/deficiency-cure step or nonresponse-abandonment clock. When a request is incomplete, over-broad, or requires more than reasonable effort to clarify, the statute PERMITS denial (N.J.S.A. 47:1A-5(f), NJ-A14/NJ-A15) rather than requiring the custodian to seek clarification and toll a clock. The only affirmative negotiation duty is the &#x27;reasonable solution&#x27; attempt for requests that would substantially disrupt operations (NJ-A24). No abandonment deadline for an unclarified request was found.
- `intake.acknowledgment_window` — OPRA has no separate acknowledgment-of-receipt step distinct from the substantive grant/deny response deadline. There is no statutory duty to send an interim acknowledgment; the first required communication is the grant/deny (or storage/archive advisory) within the response window (Deadlines sibling).
- `eligibility.identity_verification` — No requestor identity-verification or proof-of-identity requirement at intake. Requests may be anonymous (NJ-A04); the only consequence of not accurately identifying is loss of standing to sue and suspension of the response duty until the requestor makes contact (NJ-A05). No government-ID or notarization requirement found.
- `search.burden_hour_threshold` — No fixed numeric search-burden threshold (e.g., a maximum number of search hours or a defined &#x27;unduly burdensome&#x27; cap) exists. Burden limits are expressed as soft standards - &#x27;substantially disrupt agency operations&#x27; (NJ-A24) and &#x27;reasonable time period&#x27; for correspondence (NJ-A26) - with no day/hour count.
- `coverage.exemptions_out_of_scope` — The confidentiality/exemption catalogue within N.J.S.A. 47:1A-1.1 (deliberative/ACD material, criminal investigatory records, victims&#x27; records, legislative constituent records, etc.), the enacted &#x27;personal identifying information&#x27; redaction definition, and the judicial-officer home-address protection (N.J.S.A. 47:1B-1/-2) are Denials/Redaction scope and are deferred to the sibling agent; only the core government-record and public-agency coverage definitions are captured here.
- `response.acknowledgment_window` — OPRA has no separate receipt-acknowledgment step or deadline distinct from the substantive grant/deny response. The seven-business-day (or 14) response under 47:1A-5(i)(1) is the sole required response; there is no statutory &#x27;we received your request&#x27; acknowledgment clock.
- `clarification.cure_clock` — Within my scope areas, no statutory clock or mandatory back-and-forth to cure a deficient request appears. The custodian may deny incomplete/overbroad requests (47:1A-5(f),(g)) but no fixed cure window is prescribed. Intake/clarification is primarily a sibling area; noted here only to record the absence of a cure deadline.
- `redaction.broad_pii` — A broad mandatory redaction of email, medical, financial, birthdate, or general &#x27;personal information&#x27; was proposed in earlier reprints of S2930 but was DELETED before enactment. Enacted 47:1A-5(a) mandates redaction only of SSN, credit card number, personal telephone number, driver license number, and covered-officer home addresses. Do not treat the broad PII list as an operative redaction rule.
- `intake.data_broker_denial` — A provision requiring denial of all requests by or for data brokers (and a data-broker registration/fee scheme) was in earlier reprints but was DELETED and NOT enacted. No data-broker processing rule exists in current OPRA.
- `special_records.indecent_image_nondisclosure` — P.L.2024, c.16, s.10 bars a non-subject who obtained a photo/video via OPRA from disclosing indecent/graphic images of intimate parts without consent (disorderly persons offense). Excluded from rules: it is a downstream use-restriction with a criminal penalty, not something the platform does differently while processing a request.
- `production.format_priority` — Beyond &#x27;provide in the medium/format maintained&#x27; and &#x27;convert if available without substantial IT manipulation&#x27; (47:1A-5(d)), OPRA states no ranked hierarchy of production formats the platform must apply.
- `fee.indigent_or_public_interest_waiver` — OPRA creates no general fee waiver for indigent, non-profit, or public-interest requestors. The only statutory zero-fee cases are crime victims (NJ-M05) and records available on a public agency website (NJ-M04). Absence of a found rule is not proof none exists.
- `fee.inspection_no_charge` — OPRA attaches fees only to copies/duplication; no fee is prescribed for mere inspection or examination during business hours (47:1A-5(a),(b)). No affirmative &#x27;inspection is free&#x27; clause exists, so this is stated cautiously as an absence rather than an express rule.
- `payment.late_payment_interest` — No statutory provision imposes interest, late fees, or penalties on a requestor for unpaid or delayed OPRA fee payments; the only leverage is prepayment/deposit (NJ-M14, NJ-M15) and the 14-business-day record-retrieval window.
- `fee.special_service_charge_cap` — No numeric cap on cumulative special service charges (47:1A-5(c),(d)); charges are bounded only by &#x27;reasonable / actual direct cost&#x27; with a rebuttable presumption of reasonableness (NJ-M06, NJ-M09, NJ-M10).
- `review.mediation_time_limit` — No statutory deadline governs completion of GRC mediation; mediation time is expressly excluded from the 90-day adjudication clock (NJ-M47), leaving mediation duration open-ended.
- `appeal.grc_bond_requirement` — No bond or filing fee is required to appeal to the GRC (the statute affirmatively bars GRC fees, NJ-M45). No provision addresses a bond for Superior Court OPRA actions within OPRA itself.

### Material negatives

- `intake.acknowledgment_window` — Connecticut FOIA sets NO general acknowledgment/response deadline for granting access. The only fixed statutory window is the four-business-day (ten for personnel/medical) deadline to issue a DENIAL (§ 1-206(a)); grants are governed only by the soft &#x27;promptly&#x27; standard (§§ 1-210(a), 1-212(a)). Absence of a found rule is not proof none exists.
- `clarification.request_clarification_clock` — No statutory provision requires or authorizes a clarification/narrowing round-trip with a tolling clock. CT law is silent; agencies handle vague requests case-by-case (and may pursue vexatious-requester relief under § 1-206(b)(6)).
- `fee.commercial_purpose_surcharge` — Connecticut FOIA draws no commercial-vs-noncommercial fee distinction and defines no &#x27;commercial purpose&#x27;. Copy fees are the same per-page/cost ceilings regardless of requester purpose (§ 1-212).
- `fee.search_labor_charge` — No statute authorizes a separate search or staff-time fee for PAPER copies; the charge is per-page only (§ 1-212(a)). For computer-stored records, staff time for producing/formatting may be included but search/retrieval costs are expressly excluded except limited third-party computer-time charges (§ 1-212(b)).
- `search.reasonable_search_standard` — Chapter 14 contains no express &#x27;adequate/reasonable search&#x27; standard governing how an agency must search for responsive records; the obligation is implied through the access right and FOIC adjudication rather than a codified processing rule.
- `intake.submission_channel_requirement` — No statute prescribes a required submission channel or a mandatory request form for records requests generally; only that a request for a COPY be in writing (§ 1-212(a)). Inspection carries no writing requirement.
- `denials.written_denial_reason_requirement` — Section 1-206(a) requires a denial to be &#x27;in writing&#x27; within the deadline but does not, on its face, require the agency to state the statutory basis/reason for the denial; any reason-statement obligation arises in FOIC practice rather than the statute.
- `production.records_retention_hold_on_request` — No statute imposes an automatic litigation/preservation hold triggered by a records request or a pending FOIC appeal; retention is governed by separate records-management law (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 11-8 et seq.), outside Chapter 14&#x27;s request-processing rules.
