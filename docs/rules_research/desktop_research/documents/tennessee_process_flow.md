# Tennessee City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Tennessee city, under the Tennessee Public Records Act (TPRA) (Tenn. Code Ann. § 10-7-501 et seq. (core access provisions §§ 10-7-503 to 10-7-506; enforcement § 10-7-505; Office of Open Records Counsel §§ 8-4-601 to 8-4-604)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** TN citizens only. Produce promptly; else within 7 BUSINESS days: produce, deny in writing, or send time estimate. No fixed outer production deadline — only 'time reasonably necessary' (T.C.A. 10-7-503(a)(2)(B)). Inspection free; fees for copies only.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Strong residency rule: the inspection right runs to "any citizen of this state" (§ 10-7-503(a)(2)(A)); entities MAY serve non-residents voluntarily but need not (a pending federal lawsuit challenges the citizens-only rule — the AG is defending it and it remains in effect as of July 2026). The entity may require a government-issued photo ID that includes the person's address, or, if the person has no photo ID, other forms of identification evidencing Tennessee residency (§ 10-7-503(a)(7)(A)(vi), text verified). A written request may be required for COPIES but never for mere inspection, and no charge may be assessed to view (§ 10-7-503(a)(7)(A)(i)-(ii)). So practical anonymity is unavailable for emailed copy requests to a city that requires its form/ID. Purpose: neither statute nor case law restricts or permits inquiry into the requester's purpose or downstream use (RCFP, verified), with one narrow exception — commercial-value computer-generated maps/geographic data under § 10-7-506(c). If the city requires written requests, it MUST accept email requests if it uses email to transact official business, and portal submissions if it maintains a portal (§ 10-7-503(a)(7)(A)(iv), verified).

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No general commercial-purpose regime: same 7-business-day rule and same fee schedule regardless of purpose for ordinary records. Narrow exception: T.C.A. § 10-7-506(c) — requests having "commercial value" that require reproduction of all or part of a computer-generated map or other similar geographic data developed with public funds (i.e., city GIS). For those, the city may charge, in addition to reproduction costs, a fee recovering a portion of development costs (labor plus design, development, testing, implementation, and training costs, and costs to keep the data accurate/complete/current), capped at 10% of total development costs; 10-20% recovery requires a business plan approved by the local governing body and submitted to the Information Systems Council (verified against statute text). News-gathering media are exempt and pay reproduction cost only (§ 10-7-506(c)). Personal-use requesters are not charged the commercial surcharge. No deadline differences for commercial requests.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty. The only statutory obligation is to take one of the three required actions (produce, written denial with basis, or written time estimate) within 7 business days if records are not promptly available (§ 10-7-503(a)(2)(B)). Sending the OORC "Public Records Request Response Form" is the recommended practice vehicle. Many city policies and portals auto-acknowledge receipt, but that is practice only, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Records must be made available "promptly." If not practicable to do so promptly, the custodian must, within 7 BUSINESS days of receipt, do one of three things: (i) make the record available; (ii) deny the request in writing (or by completing the OORC public records request response form) stating the basis; or (iii) furnish the requestor in writing the time reasonably necessary to produce the record (T.C.A. § 10-7-503(a)(2)(B)(i)-(iii), verified against the current code). Critically, there is NO fixed outer deadline for actual production — option (iii) is itself the "extension" mechanism, limited only by a reasonableness standard ("time reasonably necessary"), and no further grounds need be stated. There is no statutory deemed-denial label, but failure to take one of the three actions within 7 business days is treated in practice as a denial supporting an immediate § 10-7-505 petition (RCFP guide does not state this expressly; it rests on § 10-7-505's 'denied in whole or in part' language). The deadline requires a DETERMINATION/response, not production. No 2025 amendments changed deadlines (OORC Fall 2025 Legislative Update lists all 2025 public chapters; none touch § 10-7-503 timing or fees), and no 2026 deadline/fee amendment was found as of July 2026.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

A request for copies must be sufficiently detailed to enable identification of the specific records sought (operationalized through the mandatory city records policy and OORC model policy/forms under § 10-7-503(g)); the Act expressly does not require the entity to sort through files to compile information, or to create/recreate a record that does not exist (§ 10-7-503(a)(4), text verified). No statutory duty to assist, but OORC model-policy practice directs the Public Records Request Coordinator to contact the requestor to clarify or narrow an ambiguous request; in practice the 7-business-day handling restarts from receipt of the clarified request (practice, not statute). Separately, § 10-7-503(a)(7)(C) lets an entity, after written notice following a fifth request made "with the intent to disrupt government operations" within a year, petition to enjoin the requester from making requests for up to one year — 2025 Pub. Ch. 392 (eff. May 5, 2025) deleted this provision's July 1, 2025 sunset, making it permanent (verified via OORC Fall 2025 Legislative Update).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Statute is silent on no-records responses; OORC guidance and its model response form direct the custodian to respond in writing (within the same 7-business-day window) that no responsive records exist. No duty to create a record, compile data, or answer questions — the TPRA reaches only existing records (§ 10-7-503(a)(4); OORC FAQs). A "no records" response is practically treated like a denial for petition purposes if the requester believes records exist.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Strong segregability duty: information made confidential by state law "shall be redacted whenever possible" and the redacted record made available; redaction does not constitute creation of a new record (§ 10-7-503(a)(5), text verified). Redaction labor IS chargeable: the OORC Schedule of Reasonable Charges lets custodians charge for time spent locating, retrieving, reviewing, redacting, and reproducing — but only time beyond the free threshold (at least the first hour), deducted from the highest-paid employee's time. If redaction of an electronic record requires newly producing paper, per-page charges can then apply (Schedule, verified).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing (or via the OORC records request response form) and must state the basis for the denial, within 7 business days (§ 10-7-503(a)(2)(B)(ii), verified). Only statutory (or otherwise state-law) exceptions justify withholding — Tennessee courts refuse to create common-law exemptions (Schneider v. City of Jackson, 226 S.W.3d 332 (Tenn. 2007), rejecting a common-law law-enforcement privilege); there are 500+ scattered statutory exceptions (OORC compiles the list; counts vary by methodology). No index/Vaughn requirement at the administrative stage, but in litigation the entity bears the burden of justifying nondisclosure by a preponderance, and the court may review records in camera under seal (§ 10-7-505(b)-(c), verified). No general balancing test — exemptions are legislative only.

**Administrative appeal:** None binding — judicial only for enforcement. However, the Office of Open Records Counsel (in the Comptroller of the Treasury), T.C.A. §§ 8-4-601 to 8-4-604 (Part 6 verified to contain exactly these four sections), provides a free informal channel: it answers questions from requestors and custodians, mediates disputes, and issues informal advisory opinions; no filing deadlines and its opinions are non-binding, but § 10-7-505(g) expressly lets a court consider OORC guidance provided to the custodian when deciding willfulness for attorney fees. There is no state FOI commission and no mandatory pre-suit exhaustion. (2025 Pub. Ch. 370 also tasks OORC with facilitating Advisory Committee on Open Government meetings.)

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Inspection is always free (§ 10-7-503(a)(7)(A)(i)); charges apply only to COPIES. Under the OORC Schedule of Reasonable Charges (authorized by T.C.A. § 8-4-604; effective Jan. 20, 2017 and still the current version as of the Fall 2025 OORC update; all figures verified from the schedule PDF): up to $0.15/page black-and-white and up to $0.50/page color (8.5x11 or 8.5x14; a duplex copy may be charged as two pages; higher charges only if documented actual cost); labor at each employee's hourly wage (base salary excluding benefits divided by required annual work hours) for locating/retrieving/reviewing/redacting/reproducing, with a free threshold of AT LEAST THE FIRST HOUR (entities may adopt a higher free threshold), deducted from the highest-paid employee's time; other media (flash drives/CDs, maps, audio) at actual out-of-pocket cost using the most cost-efficient method; electronic delivery carries no per-page charge (per-page applies only if paper must be newly produced, e.g., for redaction) — email delivery effectively free except labor; postage/delivery at actual cost. Anti-abuse principle: "excessive fees and other rules shall not be used to hinder access." Aggregation: under the OORC frequent-and-multiple-requests policy (§ 8-4-604(a)(2)) as reflected in the OORC model policy, labor may be aggregated when MORE THAN 4 requests are received within a calendar month from one person (or persons acting in concert), excluding routinely released, readily accessible records — so the free hour applies once; adoption is optional per entity policy. GIS/commercial-value surcharge per § 10-7-506(c) (10% development-cost cap, 20% with governing-body/ISC-approved plan; media exempt). An itemized written estimate must be given before producing copies (OORC Schedule/model policy; MTAS).

**Labor charges:** Yes — staff time for search, retrieval, review, redaction, and reproduction is chargeable, but only time EXCEEDING the free threshold (minimum one hour per request; entity policy may set a higher free threshold), with the free hour(s) deducted from the highest-paid involved employee's time; rate is each employee's actual hourly wage computed from base salary excluding benefits (OORC Schedule of Reasonable Charges, verified). Entities adopting the OORC aggregation policy may aggregate more than 4 requests from the same person (or concerted group) in a calendar month so the free hour applies once, excluding routinely released records. No cap on total labor charges beyond the "excessive fees shall not be used to hinder access" principle. No labor charge for inspection-only requests.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Permitted without a percentage cap: the OORC Schedule states custodians "may require payment for copies before producing copies" — i.e., full prepayment of the estimated charge (verified from schedule text); MTAS and the OORC model policy confirm cities may require prepayment per their written policy after giving an itemized estimate (model policy template: "Payment in advance will be required [when costs are estimated to exceed $__]"). Typical city practice sets a small trigger threshold (commonly $5). No statutory deposit percentage, no statutory refund/true-up rule (handled by policy).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency waiver. Waivers are purely discretionary: the OORC Schedule states custodians "may reduce or waive charges, in whole or in part" per entity policy (verified), and the OORC model policy contemplates a de minimis waiver threshold ("When fees for copies and labor do not exceed $__, the fees may be waived"). TCOG survey found only ~41% of reviewed policies allow amount-based waivers. Media pay reproduction-cost-only for GIS commercial-value records (§ 10-7-506(c)) — the closest thing to a statutory class waiver.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Partly STATUTORY (correction to earlier drafts): under § 10-7-503(a)(7)(A)(vii), an entity is not required to comply with (b) a copy request from a person who, after copies were produced, failed to pay the production cost — until payment is made; and (a) further view requests from a person who twice within six months requested to view a record and failed to view it within 15 business days of being notified it was available. Because prepayment may be required (OORC Schedule), non-payment of an estimate simply means copies are never produced and the request is closed as abandoned under the city's policy. No statutory abandonment clock; typical policies close estimates not accepted/paid within a set period (often 10-30 days, varies by city). Unpaid copy bills cannot be used to deny free inspection outside the (vii)(a) mechanism.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No statutory tolling provisions. In practice the clock is managed three ways: (1) a clarification request for an insufficiently detailed request effectively restarts the 7-business-day handling from the clarified request (OORC model-policy practice); (2) the day-7 written time estimate converts the schedule to "time reasonably necessary" with no statutory endpoint; (3) production awaits agreement to the itemized estimate and any required prepayment — processing is suspended until payment (OORC Schedule/MTAS). All three are practice built on statutory silence; only the 7-business-day action deadline is hard law.

## REMEDIES

Judicial: any Tennessee citizen denied access in whole or in part (including via unreasonable/unfounded fees) may petition the chancery or circuit court of the county where the records are located (for municipal records) (§ 10-7-505(a)-(b), verified); on request the court SHALL order the entity to immediately appear and show cause — expedited, no formal answer required; the entity bears the burden by a preponderance; in camera review under seal available; court has full injunctive remedies and must construe the Act broadly to give "the fullest possible public access" (§ 10-7-505(b)-(d)). Attorney fees: DISCRETIONARY ("may"), and only if the entity "knew that such record was public and willfully refused to disclose it" (§ 10-7-505(g), verified); the leading willfulness case is Friedmann v. Marshall County, 471 S.W.3d 427 (Tenn. Ct. App. 2015) — willfulness turns on absence of good faith in the legal position relied on to refuse; a plausible good-faith legal basis defeats fees; § 10-7-505(g) expressly permits considering the custodian's reliance on OORC guidance. Records must be released upon judgment for the petitioner unless an appeal is timely noticed AND the court certifies a substantial legal issue. No civil penalties and no criminal sanctions for wrongful denial.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design implications for a Tennessee city: (1) Identity module is load-bearing — collect name, Tennessee address, and (if the city's policy requires) a government photo-ID upload or alternate residency proof; include a Tennessee-citizenship attestation; decide whether the city will voluntarily serve non-residents (statute permits refusal; a federal challenge to the citizens-only rule is pending, so build residency enforcement as a configurable toggle). (2) Copies require a written request — the portal satisfies this, but § 10-7-503(a)(7)(A)(iv) requires also accepting in-person/mail requests, email requests if the city transacts business by email, and portal submissions if a portal exists — the portal cannot lawfully be made the exclusive channel. (3) No purpose question for ordinary records; add a commercial-use question ONLY on a GIS/map-data branch (§ 10-7-506(c) surcharge module with a news-media exemption checkbox). (4) Build a 7-BUSINESS-day timer with a forced three-outcome action (produce / written denial citing basis / written time-estimate via OORC response form); flag day-7 misses as litigation exposure (failure to act supports a § 10-7-505 petition and feeds the Friedmann willfulness analysis for fee-shifting). (5) Fee engine: up to $0.15/$0.50 per page, per-employee hourly-wage labor with a free threshold of at least one hour deducted from the highest-paid employee, actual-cost media/postage, no per-page fee for electronic delivery; itemized-estimate generation with requestor-acceptance step; full-prepayment gate (no percentage cap; configurable trigger threshold, commonly $5); monthly aggregation counter (>4 requests/requestor/month, excluding routine records) if the city's policy adopts the OORC aggregation option. (6) No fee-waiver application flow needed (discretionary only; configurable de minimis auto-waiver). (7) Auto-acknowledgment exceeds statute but is safe and common. (8) Clarification workflow that restarts handling on ambiguous requests; track repeat requesters both for the § 10-7-503(a)(7)(C) disruptive-request injunction and for the statutory (a)(7)(A)(vii) unpaid-balance / no-show-viewer refusal grounds (unpaid-balance block applies to copy requests only, never to inspection). (9) No admin-appeal routing — offer OORC contact info for free mediation instead.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) The exact codified subsection housing a "sufficiently detailed to identify the specific records" requirement still could not be pinned to statutory text; it operates via the mandatory § 10-7-503(g) policy and OORC model policy/forms. (2) Tennessee has no statutory "deemed denial" label — treating day-7 silence as denial rests on § 10-7-505 practice and commentary, not express text. (3) The Schedule of Reasonable Charges verified is the January 20, 2017 version; the Fall 2025 OORC legislative update confirms no fee/deadline statutory changes through 2025, and searches found no 2026 amendment as of July 2026, but the 2026 legislative session's public chapters have not been exhaustively surveyed (no 2026 OORC update located yet) and the OORC can revise the schedule administratively — re-check the Comptroller site at launch. (4) The >4-requests-per-calendar-month aggregation threshold is now confirmed directly from the OORC model policy text (and MTAS confirms the frequent-and-multiple-requests policy under § 8-4-604(a)(2)); note it is optional and the fee schedule PDF itself does not contain the number. (5) Estimate-expiration/abandonment windows (10-30 days) and prepayment trigger amounts (e.g., $5) are city-policy blanks in the OORC template, varying by city. (6) The disruptive-request provision is confirmed at § 10-7-503(a)(7)(C) and 2025 Pub. Ch. 392 (eff. May 5, 2025) confirmed deleted its July 1, 2025 sunset. (7) Whether cities must deliver records in native electronic format on request is unsettled in the statute; OORC guidance favors electronic delivery when records exist electronically, but practice varies. (8) A federal lawsuit challenging the citizens-only restriction was pending as of the latest reporting found (AG defending); its current posture in July 2026 was not confirmable from accessible sources (paywalled) — the restriction remains in effect. (9) Exact count of statutory exemptions varies by source (commonly "more than 500"); the "roughly 700" figure in the prior draft could not be confirmed and was softened.
