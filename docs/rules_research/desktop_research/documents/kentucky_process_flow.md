# Kentucky City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Kentucky city, under the Kentucky Open Records Act (KRS 61.870 to 61.884). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** KY Open Records Act (KRS 61.870-61.884): written determination + readily available records due in 5 business days; KY residents only; free AG appeal with force of law. 2025 HB 520 (eff. 6/27/25) loosened the police-investigation exemption.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Anonymous requests are NOT permitted: the official custodian may require a written application "signed by the applicant and with his or her name printed legibly," describing the records to be inspected (KRS 61.872(2)); only "residents of the Commonwealth" have a statutory right (residency requirement added eff. 6/29/2021 by HB 312). "Resident of the Commonwealth" (KRS 61.870(10), verified current through 1/1/2025) has seven categories: (a) individual residing in KY; (b) domestic business entity with a KY location; (c) foreign business entity registered with the KY Secretary of State; (d) individual employed at a KY work location; (e) individual or entity owning KY real property; (f) authorized representative of anyone in (a)-(e); (g) a news-gathering organization as defined in KRS 189.635(9)(b)1.a. to e. (cross-reference conformed to renumbered 189.635 by a 2024 amendment eff. 7/15/2024 — the old (8)(b) citation is outdated). Agencies may not mandate their own form and MUST accept the AG's standardized form (KRS 61.872(2), 61.876(4)); the standardized form and city forms (e.g., Lexington) ask HOW the requester is a resident. Purpose may not be demanded generally, with one exception: the agency may require a certified statement of commercial purpose (KRS 61.874(4)(b)). Email requests are valid if sent to the custodian's published email address (KRS 61.872(2), 61.876). Nonresidents have no statutory right; honoring them is discretionary.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** Significant commercial branch. "Commercial purpose" = direct or indirect use of any part of a public record, in any form, for sale, resale, solicitation, rent, or lease of a service, or any use by which the user expects a profit through commission, salary, or fee, EXCLUDING (1) newspaper/periodical publication or related use, (2) radio/TV news or informational program use, and (3) use in litigation preparation/defense or claims settlement (KRS 61.870(4)(a)-(b), verified). The agency may require a certified statement of the commercial purpose and may require the requester to enter a CONTRACT permitting use for the stated purpose for a specified fee (KRS 61.874(4)(b)). Commercial copy fees may be a reasonable fee based on (1) cost of media, mechanical processing AND staff required to produce the copy, and/or (2) the agency's cost of creation, purchase, or other acquisition of the record (KRS 61.874(4)(c)) — i.e., staff time is chargeable only for commercial requests. Same 5-day deadline. Misuse teeth: obtaining records under false pretense of noncommercial purpose or using them for an undeclared commercial purpose violates KRS 61.874(5); the agency may recover THREE TIMES the amount that would have been charged had the actual commercial purpose been stated, plus costs and reasonable attorney fees (KRS 61.8745, verified), and KRS 61.991 provides penalties.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate acknowledgment duty in the statute — the required act by day 5 (business days) is the substantive written determination itself (KRS 61.880(1)). City practice (e.g., Louisville Metro's NextRequest portal) is to auto-acknowledge on receipt, but that is convenience, not law.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Fixed: the agency "shall determine within five (5) days, excepting Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, after the receipt of any such request whether to comply" and must notify the requester IN WRITING of its decision within that 5-business-day window (KRS 61.880(1); extended from 3 to 5 days by 2021 HB 312, eff. 6/29/2021 — verified against current statute text). The deadline requires a determination plus production of records that are readily available; if the record is "in active use, in storage or not otherwise available," the custodian must immediately notify the requester and set a place, time and date for access "not to exceed five (5) days from receipt of the application," UNLESS the custodian gives "a detailed explanation of the cause" for further delay and states the place, time, and EARLIEST DATE the record will be available (KRS 61.872(5)) — there is no fixed outer cap, but AG decisions require the delay to be reasonable and the earliest-date commitment is mandatory. Silence past day 5 is a constructive denial appealable to the Attorney General (KRS 61.880(2)); delay short of denial (including excessive extensions) is separately appealable as subversion of the intent of the Act (KRS 61.880(4)). The clock starts only when the request reaches the official custodian via hand delivery, mail, fax, or email to the agency's published address (KRS 61.872(2), 61.876). No different deadline for commercial requests.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

For COPIES delivered by mail/without onsite inspection, the requester must "precisely describe" records that are "readily available within the public agency" (KRS 61.872(3)(b), verified) — a materially higher bar than for onsite inspection, where only a reasonable description is needed (Commonwealth v. Chestnut, 250 S.W.3d 655 (Ky. 2008), which also holds agencies cannot demand exactness or reject broad-but-identifiable requests). No express statutory duty to assist, but AG decisions require agencies to advise the requester of deficiencies rather than ignore the request, and any denial for imprecision must issue in writing within 5 business days. An "unreasonable burden" or a pattern of disruptive repeated requests permits refusal, but ONLY if sustained by CLEAR AND CONVINCING evidence (KRS 61.872(6), verified) — a deliberately hard standard.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** If the recipient lacks custody or control, they "shall notify the applicant and shall furnish the name and location" of the actual official custodian (KRS 61.872(4), verified). If no responsive record exists, the agency must affirmatively say so in writing within 5 business days (AG decisions applying KRS 61.880(1)). No duty to create a record, compile a list, or answer questions; a requester challenging a no-records response must make a prima facie showing the record should exist before the agency must explain (Bowling v. Lexington-Fayette Urban County Gov't, 172 S.W.3d 333 (Ky. 2005)).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
General rule — mandatory segregability: if a record contains both exempt and nonexempt material, the agency "shall separate the excepted and make the nonexcepted material available for examination" (KRS 61.878(4), verified). MAJOR 2025 CARVE-OUT: HB 520 (2025 Ky. Acts ch. 97, eff. 6/27/2025) amended KRS 61.878(1)(h) and KRS 17.150(2) and, per consistent press/KOGC analysis, relieved law enforcement agencies of the duty to separate exempt from nonexempt material in records of OPEN investigations (enrolled text not independently read — verify before building police-records templates). Outside that carve-out redaction remains required, not optional. Redaction/review labor is NOT chargeable to noncommercial requesters: fees are capped at actual reproduction cost "not including the cost of staff required" (KRS 61.874(3)); Commonwealth v. Courier Journal, 601 S.W.3d 501 (Ky. App. 2020) holds agencies may not charge for redaction.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing within the same 5-business-day window, issued by or under authority of the official custodian, and must include "a statement of the specific exception authorizing the withholding" (citing one of the 19 exemption categories, KRS 61.878(1)(a)-(s) — (s) is the last letter, verified) "and a brief explanation of how the exception applies to the record withheld" (KRS 61.880(1)). NOTE: 2025 HB 520 (Acts ch. 97, eff. 6/27/2025) loosened (1)(h) so law enforcement records may be withheld where disclosure "could pose an articulable risk of harm" to the agency or its prospective/ongoing investigation — a speculative-harm standard the AG now applies (e.g., 25-ORD-401). Boilerplate multi-exemption citations remain inadequate per AG decisions. Exceptions are strictly construed (KRS 61.871) and the burden of proof always rests on the agency (KRS 61.880(2)(c), 61.882(3)). No Vaughn-index requirement, but on AG appeal the agency may be required to produce records for confidential review (KRS 61.880(2)(c)).

**Administrative appeal:** Yes — the Kentucky Attorney General (KRS 61.880(2)), a genuinely adjudicative route: requester sends the AG the written request and the agency's denial (or notes the agency's silence); the AG must issue an open records decision (ORD) "within twenty (20) days, excepting Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," extendable up to 30 additional workdays for unusual circumstances (KRS 61.880(2)(b), verified against statute text — this extension belongs to the AG, not the agency). Burden is on the agency; the AG may confidentially review the disputed records (KRS 61.880(2)(c)). No filing fee and NO statutory deadline to invoke it (except prison inmates: 20 days, KRS 197.025(3), confirmed in AG decisions). The ORD has the force and effect of law and is enforceable in circuit court unless a party appeals to circuit court within 30 days of the decision (KRS 61.880(5), verified). Fee disputes, delay, and other subversion-of-intent complaints (excessive fees, delays, extensions) are appealable under KRS 61.880(4). AG route is optional; requesters need not exhaust it before suing (KRS 61.882(2)). The route remains active in 2026 (e.g., 26-ORD-139, Mar. 2026).

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Noncommercial copies: a reasonable fee that "shall not exceed the actual cost of reproduction, including the costs of the media and any mechanical processing cost... but not including the cost of staff required" (KRS 61.874(3), verified). The accepted paper rate is 10 cents per page — Friend v. Rees, 696 S.W.2d 325 (Ky. App. 1985) (verified via RCFP), codified for state agencies at 200 KAR 1:020 and followed by cities as practice; charging 25-50 cents has repeatedly been held excessive in AG decisions absent proof of actual cost. Postage chargeable at actual cost; mailed copies go out "upon receipt of all fees and the cost of mailing" (KRS 61.872(3)(b)). Electronic records: requester may choose standard electronic or standard hard copy format where the record is maintained in that form (KRS 61.874(2)(a)); fee limited to actual media/duplication cost — email delivery of existing electronic records is effectively free. Nonstandardized/tailored formats: agency MAY provide at its discretion and recover staff costs plus actual costs (KRS 61.874(3)) — the only programming-charge hook for noncommercial requests. Commercial: staff time and record creation/purchase/acquisition costs recoverable (KRS 61.874(4)(c)). County clerks have a separate per-page fee schedule (KRS 64.019), inapplicable to general city records (not re-verified this pass).

**Labor charges:** Search, review, and redaction staff time is NOT chargeable for noncommercial requests — flatly excluded by KRS 61.874(3) ("not including the cost of staff required"), with no free-hours threshold needed because the exclusion is total. Staff time IS chargeable for (a) commercial-purpose requests (KRS 61.874(4)(c)) and (b) any requester who asks for a nonstandardized/tailored format (KRS 61.874(3)).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Full prepayment authorized, no percentage cap: the custodian "may require a written request and advance payment of the prescribed fee" (KRS 61.874(1), verified), and for mailed copies shall mail them "upon receipt of all fees and the cost of mailing" (KRS 61.872(3)(b)) — payment precedes delivery as a statutory condition. Excessive fee estimates are appealable to the AG as subversion of intent under KRS 61.880(4).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
None statutory. No public-interest waiver, no indigency waiver (RCFP guide confirms: "There is no provision for waiving fees"). Any waiver (e.g., cities routinely not billing trivial amounts or emailing electronic records free) is purely discretionary local practice. Inmates and litigants get no special waiver either.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Copies are simply not delivered: KRS 61.872(3)(b) conditions mailing on receipt of all fees and postage, and KRS 61.874(1) authorizes advance payment. The statute prescribes no abandonment procedure; cities in practice close the request after a notice period if the invoice/deposit goes unpaid. Whether an agency may refuse NEW requests over a prior unpaid balance is not settled by statute (AG decisions vary; treat as risky).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No general tolling regime. The 5-day clock excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays by definition (KRS 61.880(1)). The clock does not start until the request reaches the official custodian's published address/email (KRS 61.872(2), 61.876; misdirected requests trigger only the KRS 61.872(4) referral duty). The only statutory extension mechanism is the KRS 61.872(5) notice for records in active use/storage/otherwise unavailable: immediate notice with a detailed explanation of the cause plus the earliest date of availability — that notice itself is still due within the 5-business-day window. A clarification demand does not statutorily pause anything; prudent agencies still respond by day 5. Awaiting prepayment pauses delivery (not the determination duty).

## REMEDIES

Original action or appeal of an AG decision in the circuit court of the county where the agency has its principal place of business or where the record is maintained (KRS 61.882(1), verified); review is de novo with the burden on the agency, courts may examine records in camera, and noncompliance is punishable as contempt (KRS 61.882(3)). A prevailing requester may recover costs including reasonable attorney fees upon a finding the records were WILLFULLY withheld (conditioned on willfulness, not automatic), plus a penalty of up to $25 per day for each day the right to inspect or copy was denied (KRS 61.882(5), verified). Treble-fee damages run to the agency against commercial misusers, plus costs and attorney fees (KRS 61.8745, verified). Criminal: willful concealment or destruction of records with intent to violate the Act is a Class A misdemeanor for each separate violation (KRS 61.991(2)(a)); KRS 519.060 tampering with public records is a felony.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity: require legibly printed name + signature (e-signature accepted in practice), and a mandatory Kentucky-residency attestation with category picklist tracking KRS 61.870(10)(a)-(g): resides in KY / KY-located domestic business / SOS-registered foreign business / employed at KY work location / KY real property owner / authorized representative of the foregoing / news-gathering organization per KRS 189.635(9)(b) — Lexington asks HOW the requester is a resident. The portal must also accept the AG's standardized form (KRS 61.876(4)) — refusing it is a violation. Purpose: do NOT ask a general purpose question; include only a commercial-purpose yes/no with certified-statement capture (KRS 61.874(4)(b)) and a warning about treble damages (KRS 61.8745). Commercial-rate module: staff time + media + acquisition-cost pricing, optional contract generation; same 5-day deadline. Fee module: default 10 cents/page paper, $0 for emailed existing electronic records, actual postage; block any staff-time line item on noncommercial requests (top AG-appeal trigger). No fee-waiver question needed (no statutory waiver). Deposit logic: full prepayment invoice before release is lawful; hold delivery, not the determination. Auto-acknowledgment: recommended (not required); the hard deadline is a substantive written determination in 5 business days — build the timer excluding Sa/Su/state holidays, starting only when the request hits the published custodian email (publish that address per KRS 61.876). Templates needed: no-custody referral (61.872(4)), detained-records delay notice with required earliest-available date (61.872(5)), no-records certification, denial with specific-exemption + application explanation (61.880(1)), and a post-HB 520 police-investigation denial template using the 'could pose an articulable risk of harm' language (61.878(1)(h), as amended eff. 6/27/2025). Litigation-exposure flags: blown 5-day deadline, boilerplate exemption cites, staff-time charges to noncommercial requesters, refusal of the AG standardized form, demanding purpose beyond commercial certification, and fee estimates >10 cents/page without cost documentation — all are free AG appeals with force-of-law outcomes, $25/day and fee-shifting exposure on willfulness.
