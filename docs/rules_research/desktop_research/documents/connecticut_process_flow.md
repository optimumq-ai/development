# Connecticut City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Connecticut city, under the Connecticut Freedom of Information Act (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 1-200 to 1-259 (Chapter 14)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** No fixed production deadline — records "promptly" (§1-210(a)); written denial due in 4 business days (10 if §1-214 personnel notice applies); silence = deemed denial (§1-206(a)); 30 days to appeal to FOI Commission.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
FOIA rights run to "every person" (§ 1-210(a)) — no residency or citizenship requirement; nonresidents have equal rights. Purpose and motive are irrelevant and may not be demanded: Chief of Police, Hartford Police Dept. v. FOIC, 252 Conn. 377, 387 (2000) (disclosability "does not depend in any way on the status or motive of the applicant ... because the act vindicates the public's right to know"). Practical identity limits: copies require "applying in writing" (§ 1-212(a)) — an email qualifies — and a pseudonymous email address is generally workable; agencies may ask requesters to use a form but FOIC treats form completion as non-mandatory. Indigency fee waivers (§ 1-212(d)(1)) require identity/means disclosure only by the requester's choice.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None. The statute contains no commercial-purpose disclosure duty, no different deadline, and no commercial fee tier; bills to authorize extra fees for commercial requests have failed to pass. Case law confirms records must be disclosed "even if used for commercial purposes" (Town of Glastonbury v. FOIC, 35 Conn. App. 111 (1994), teacher name/address lists). The only purpose-adjacent fee provision is the cap of 3 cents per name delivered (or actual cost, whichever is less) for registered-voter lists (§ 1-212(b)).

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty. The only mandated communication is a written denial within 4 business days (10 if § 1-214(b)-(c) applies) under § 1-206(a). Acknowledging receipt, giving an estimated timeline, and explaining any delay is FOIC-recommended practice (Advisory Opinion #51 urges agencies to "politely explain" why immediate compliance is not possible) and common city practice (many CT cities auto-acknowledge via clerk email or portals such as NextRequest), but it is practice, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
No fixed production deadline: records must be provided "promptly" (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 1-210(a), 1-212(a)). FOIC Advisory Opinion #51 (1981) defines "promptly" as "quickly and without undue delay, taking into account all of the factors presented by a particular request" — volume, personnel time, the requester's timeline and need, and the public importance of the agency's other work — and calls timely access a fundamental right that is part of the agency's mission. The hard statutory numbers are in § 1-206(a): any DENIAL must be in writing within 4 business days of receipt — or within 10 business days for requests subject to the § 1-214(b)-(c) personnel/medical-file notice procedure — and "failure to comply with a request ... within the applicable number of business days shall be deemed to be a denial," i.e., silence for 4 (or 10) business days is a constructive denial that starts the 30-day FOIC appeal clock. There is no statutory extension mechanism; agencies must simply remain "prompt," and FOIC evaluates delay complaints on the facts. Compliance after the 4th day is not automatically a violation if still "prompt," but the requester may appeal once the deemed denial occurs.

---

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

The statute imposes no express duty to assist and no clarification procedure; a request need only reasonably identify the records. FOIC decisions require a diligent search for what was described and treat good-faith dialogue as part of promptness (Advisory Opinion #51 encourages communication about delays and scope). Agencies need not answer questions or create records, so purely interrogatory "requests" can be declined. Practice: city clerks typically email back asking the requester to narrow overbroad requests; there is no statutory clock pause for this, but breadth is a factor making a longer response still "prompt." Truly abusive patterns can be addressed via a vexatious-requester petition to FOIC under § 1-206(b)(6) (added by P.A. 18-95; relief up to 1 year of non-compliance with that requester's requests, based on documented request volume, scope, language, and abusive pattern) and, after FOIC denies such a person leave to appeal, a Superior Court injunction barring further abusive FOI appeals (§ 1-241).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No duty to create a record or compile a new one; for computer-stored records the agency must provide a copy of nonexempt data on the requested medium "if the agency can reasonably make any such copy or have any such copy made" (§ 1-211(a)), at the § 1-212(b) actual-cost fees. If no responsive records exist, the agency should so notify the requester; a truthful no-records response is not itself a substantive denial, but a requester who doubts the search's adequacy may still complain to FOIC, which tests whether a diligent search was performed. The 4-business-day deemed-denial rule still applies to saying nothing at all.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Exempt material must be severed and the nonexempt remainder disclosed — exemptions apply to information, not whole files; FOIC routinely orders redacted disclosure (e.g., §§ 1-210(b), 1-217 residential-address redactions for protected classes of officials). Redaction/review labor is NOT chargeable for paper copies — only the per-page copy fee is allowed; no search or service charges beyond statutory fees (FOIC, Pearl v. Town of Newington, cited in the RCFP Open Government Guide). For computer-stored records, § 1-212(b) allows charging the hourly salary of employees engaged in providing/formatting/programming the electronic copy, but FOIC has not treated legal review/redaction time as a chargeable formatting cost.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denials must be in writing within 4 business days — 10 business days when the § 1-214(b)-(c) personnel/medical-file procedure applies (§ 1-206(a)). The agency bears the burden of proving an exemption applies; exemptions are narrowly construed (Wilson v. FOIC, 181 Conn. 324 (1980)). For personnel files, an agency's belief that disclosure would invade privacy must not be "frivolous or patently unfounded" (Shew v. FOIC, 245 Conn. 149 (1998), applying the Perkins v. FOIC invasion-of-privacy test). No statutory Vaughn-index requirement, but FOIC requires record-by-record justification on appeal and conducts in camera review. Most § 1-210(b) exemptions are permissive ("nothing ... shall be construed to require disclosure"), several with built-in balancing (e.g., preliminary drafts where the agency determines the public interest in withholding clearly outweighs disclosure, § 1-210(b)(1)).

**Administrative appeal:** Freedom of Information Commission (FOIC) — one of the strongest administrative enforcement bodies in the U.S. File a complaint (letter or form; free; lawyer optional) within 30 days of the denial or deemed denial (§ 1-206(b)(1)); for unnoticed secret meetings, 30 days from when the violation was or should have been known. An FOIC ombudsman is assigned to each appeal to attempt settlement before hearing; contested cases get a hearing before a hearing officer, and the Commission must render a final decision within 1 year of the filing of the notice of appeal. FOIC can order disclosure, reduce or set aside fees, impose civil penalties, and declare improper meeting actions null and void. Frivolous appeals can be dismissed without hearing, and requester-side civil penalties of $20-$1,000 apply to appeals taken frivolously, without reasonable grounds and solely to harass the agency (§ 1-206(b)(2)). Agencies may petition FOIC for relief from a "vexatious requester" (§ 1-206(b)(6), added by P.A. 18-95) — on a documented showing of abusive request history, relief up to 1 year of not complying with that person's requests.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Copies from municipal (non-state) agencies: max 50 cents per page; state agencies max 25 cents per page (§ 1-212(a)). Transcriptions: not to exceed actual cost to the agency. Computer-stored/electronic records: fee "shall not exceed the cost thereof to the public agency," limited to (1) hourly salary of employees engaged in providing the record, including formatting/programming time, (2) cost of an outside professional electronic copying service if necessary, (3) actual cost of storage devices/media provided, (4) computer time charges where another agency or contractor provides storage/retrieval (§ 1-212(b)); records already in electronic form emailed to the requester typically cost the agency nothing, so FOIC treats little or no fee as proper — the 50-cent page rate does not apply to native electronic copies. No search, review, or inspection fees. Registered-voter lists: max 3 cents per name delivered or actual cost, whichever is less (§ 1-212(b)). Hand-held scanner use by requesters: agency may set a fee up to $20 (§ 1-212(g)) — in-person only. No sales tax on FOIA copies.

**Labor charges:** Not chargeable for paper copying beyond the per-page rate: no search, retrieval, review, or redaction labor charges (FOIC, Pearl v. Town of Newington; RCFP Open Government Guide — no service charge may be added to statutory fees). Chargeable only for computer-stored records under § 1-212(b): hourly salary of employees engaged in providing/formatting/programming the electronic record, plus media and computer-time costs — no free-hours threshold and no dollar cap, but the total may not exceed actual cost to the agency.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Prepayment (not a partial deposit) may be required whenever the estimated fee is $10 or more (§ 1-212(c)) — 100% of the estimate, no percentage cap. Cities routinely invoke this; production waits until payment. No statutory itemized-estimate requirement, but FOIC expects a good-faith estimate, and delay caused by an inflated estimate can itself be a promptness violation.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Mandatory ("shall waive") grounds in § 1-212(d): (1) requester is an indigent individual; (2) the records located are determined by the agency to be exempt from disclosure; (3) in the agency's judgment, compliance benefits the general welfare (public-interest waiver — agency judgment, effectively discretionary in application); (4) elected official of a political subdivision obtaining the record from an agency of that same subdivision, certifying it pertains to official duties; (5) member of the Division of Public Defender Services or assigned counsel certifying the record pertains to their duties. No statutory definition of indigency — cities apply their own tests (practice varies).

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute is silent. Practice: if a $10+ prepayment demand goes unpaid, the agency simply does not produce copies, and cities administratively close stale requests. FOIC has upheld conditioning copies on lawful prepayment; a requester disputing the fee amount may complain to FOIC (which can reduce or eliminate any improper fee under § 1-206(b)). Inspection remains free even if the requester will not pay for copies.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No formal tolling scheme — the "promptly" standard just flexes with volume/complexity (Advisory Opinion #51). Practical pauses: (1) prepayment demand under § 1-212(c) — production awaits payment; (2) personnel/medical/similar files: § 1-214(b) requires the agency to immediately notify in writing the affected employee and union when it reasonably believes disclosure would invade privacy; a written objection filed within 7 business days of receipt of the notice (9 business days from mailing if receipt is not proven) blocks disclosure unless FOIC orders it (§ 1-214(c)) — and the written-denial deadline extends to 10 business days for these requests; (3) clarification exchanges pause nothing legally but factor into promptness. The deemed-denial clock itself is never tolled.

## REMEDIES

Judicial review of FOIC decisions goes to Superior Court under the UAPA, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 4-183 (either party may appeal). Civil penalties: FOIC may fine the official directly responsible $20-$5,000 personally (maximum raised from $1,000 by P.A. 23-200, eff. 10/1/2023) for denial without reasonable grounds (§ 1-206(b)(2)), and under § 1-206(b)(5) (added by P.A. 23-200) for a practice or pattern of conduct constituting obstruction of FOIA rights or reckless, wilful or wanton misconduct; fines are personal, but enforcement is historically rare. Criminal: wilful destruction/alteration of records sought under FOIA is a class A misdemeanor and failure to comply with an FOIC order is a class B misdemeanor, each occurrence a separate offense (§ 1-240). No attorney-fee award for prevailing requesters — the free FOIC process substitutes; the only FOIA fee-shifting runs the other way: an agency that wins a § 1-241 injunction against a frivolous/harassing appellant may recover damages, attorney's fees and costs if the injunction is violated.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for CT cities: (1) Identity fields optional — every person may request; do not require purpose or residency; an email address alone is enough (the written-application requirement is satisfied by the portal/email itself). (2) No commercial-rate module needed — no commercial branch in CT. (3) Auto-acknowledgment is good practice, not statute; but build a hard 4-business-day timer: any denial (full or partial) must issue in writing by then, and silence at day 4 = deemed denial exposing the city to a 30-day FOIC complaint — flag unanswered requests at day 3; switch the timer to 10 business days when the § 1-214 personnel-file notice procedure is triggered. (4) Fee engine: 50¢/page cap for paper; native electronic records delivered by email/portal should default to $0 (actual cost); media/programming cost fields for § 1-212(b) items; block search/review/redaction labor charges. (5) Prepayment logic: trigger full prepayment only when estimate ≥ $10; hold production until paid; auto-close stale unpaid requests with notice. (6) Fee-waiver question: offer indigency and general-welfare waiver checkboxes (§ 1-212(d)). (7) Personnel-records workflow: route to § 1-214 immediate employee/union written notice with a 7-business-day (9 from mailing) objection hold and the 10-day denial clock. (8) Litigation-exposure flags: delay patterns (§ 1-206(b)(5) obstruction penalties, personal fines to $5,000 under P.A. 23-200), missing written denial, per-page charges on electronic records, any demand for purpose. (9) Consider a vexatious-requester tracking view (request counts, scope, language, pattern) to support a § 1-206(b)(6) FOIC petition.
