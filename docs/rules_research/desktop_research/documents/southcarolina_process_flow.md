# South Carolina City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a South Carolina city, under the South Carolina Freedom of Information Act (S.C. Code Ann. § 30-4-10 et seq.). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** SC FOIA sec. 30-4-30(C): written determination in 10 business days (20 if records >24 months old); produce within 30 cal. days of determination (35 if older); silence = request DEEMED APPROVED as to nonexempt records. No admin appeal — circuit court only.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
§ 30-4-30(A): "A person has a right to inspect, copy, or receive an electronic transmission of any public record" — no residency, citizenship, or ID requirement to request, and no general requirement to state a purpose (RCFP guide confirms; the main purpose-related exception is federal DPPA/motor-vehicle-record rules). Statutory exclusion: the § 30-4-30(A) right "does not extend to individuals serving a sentence of imprisonment" in a state, county, another state's, or federal correctional facility (added by Act 67 of 2017). Caveat 1: the judicial enforcement remedy in § 30-4-100(A) is given to "a citizen of the State," so out-of-state requesters' enforcement standing is doubtful even though they may request. Caveat 2: purpose CAN effectively be probed for commercial solicitation — § 30-4-50(B) use restrictions and the Family Privacy Protection Act (§ 30-2-50) prohibit obtaining/using personal information from state agencies, local governments, or political subdivisions for commercial solicitation, and those bodies must notify all requesters of the prohibition; many bodies require a non-solicitation certification on request forms. Anonymous email requests are not statutorily barred, but a written request with contact info is needed for the determination/delivery mechanics.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** Commercial purpose does NOT change deadlines or fee rates — no commercial-rate tier and no longer clock. Instead SC restricts USE: § 30-4-50(B) bars use of police incident report information and employee salary schedule information for commercial solicitation, and bars use of home addresses and home telephone numbers of employees and officers of public bodies for commercial solicitation (while expressly preserving public and press access to the records themselves); §§ 30-4-160 and 30-4-165 separately bar the DMV from selling Social Security numbers, driver's license photographs, and related driver's license personal information. The Family Privacy Protection Act, § 30-2-50, makes it a misdemeanor (fine up to $500 or imprisonment up to 1 year, or both) to knowingly obtain or use personal information obtained from a state agency, local government, or political subdivision for commercial solicitation directed at any person in SC; those bodies must give all requesters (and all persons who obtain records) notice of the prohibition and take reasonable measures to prevent commercial-solicitation misuse. Common city practice: request forms include a certification that records will not be used for commercial solicitation.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory acknowledgment duty. The only statutorily required communication is the written determination itself, with reasons, within 10/20 business days (§ 30-4-30(C)). Common city practice: municipalities using request portals send automatic receipt confirmations, and the Municipal Association of SC (MASC) encourages prompt confirmation as best practice — but this is practice only, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Two-stage system under § 30-4-30(C) (as rewritten by Act 67 of 2017, eff. May 19, 2017; verified still current in the 2025 Code, no later amendments as of July 2026). Stage 1 — DETERMINATION: upon a written request, the public body must notify the requester of its determination (grant/deny) "and the reasons for it" within 10 days "excepting Saturdays, Sundays, and legal public holidays" (i.e., 10 business days) of receipt; "if the record is more than twenty-four months old at the date the request is made, the public body has twenty days" (business days). Stage 2 — PRODUCTION: if granted, records must be furnished "no later than thirty calendar days from the date on which the final determination was provided," or 35 calendar days if the records are more than 24 months old; if a deposit is required, the 30/35-calendar-day production clock runs from receipt of the deposit instead. No extension mechanism exists in the statute — the only escape valve is the public body petitioning circuit court under § 30-4-110(A) for relief from improper requests. CONSTRUCTIVE GRANT (distinctive): if written notification of the determination is neither mailed, electronically transmitted, nor personally delivered within the 10/20-day window, "the request must be considered approved" as to nonexempt records (§ 30-4-30(C)) — a deemed approval, not a deemed denial. Missing the 30/35-day production deadline is a violation enforceable in circuit court under § 30-4-100. Separately, § 30-4-30(D) requires certain records (meeting minutes for the preceding 6 months, § 30-4-50(A)(8) incident reports for the preceding 14 days, jail/detention rosters for the preceding 3 months, documents distributed at public meetings in the preceding 6 months) to be available for immediate in-person inspection without any written request.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

No statutory duty to assist requesters and no statutory clarification/tolling procedure. The statute's tool for problem requests is § 30-4-110(A): the PUBLIC BODY may file a request for hearing in circuit court "to seek relief from unduly burdensome, overly broad, vague, repetitive, or otherwise improper requests," or where it has received a request but is unable to make a good-faith determination as to exemption (added by Act 67 of 2017); the statute does not expressly toll the 10/20-day determination clock while such a suit is pending, so cautious bodies issue a timely determination anyway. Common city practice: staff contact the requester informally to narrow scope (MASC guidance encourages this), but the deemed-approval clock keeps running unless a determination issues.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Statute is silent on no-records responses; the safe reading of § 30-4-30(C) is that a written determination stating that no responsive records exist must issue within the 10/20-business-day window (silence risks deemed approval and litigation). SC Attorney General opinions and the RCFP guide state FOIA does not require a public body to create a record (including an electronic version) that does not exist, and § 30-4-30(A) frames the right as access to existing "public records." Common city practice: a written "no responsive records" letter closing the request.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Express segregability duty: § 30-4-40(b) — if a public record contains material not exempt under subsection (a), the public body "shall separate the exempt and nonexempt material and make the nonexempt material available." Redaction labor IS chargeable: § 30-4-30(B) authorizes fees "not to exceed the actual cost of the search, retrieval, and redaction of records," capped at the prorated hourly salary of the lowest-paid employee who (in the custodian's reasonable discretion) has the necessary skill and training to perform the request.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be part of the written determination within 10/20 business days and must state "the reasons for it" (§ 30-4-30(C)). Exemptions in § 30-4-40(a) are permissive ("may be exempt") and the body bears the burden in court; several exemptions (e.g., (a)(2) personal privacy) involve balancing (Burton v. York County Sheriff's Dept. — privacy exemption construed narrowly against withholding). No Vaughn-index requirement in the statute. Failure to send any timely written determination = request deemed approved as to nonexempt records — SC's constructive-response rule cuts in the requester's favor, unlike most states' deemed denial.

**Administrative appeal:** none — judicial only. South Carolina has no ombudsman, records board, or AG appeal route (a proposed Office of Freedom of Information Act Review in H.3352 was dropped from the enacted 2017 law; the AG's office publishes opinions and guidance but does not adjudicate requester appeals). RCFP confirms enforcement is by private civil action; it lists commission/agency enforcement as "None."

---

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
§ 30-4-30(B): fees may not exceed ACTUAL COST of search, retrieval, and redaction; labor billed at no more than the prorated hourly salary of the lowest-paid employee with the necessary skill and training; each public body must develop a fee schedule and POST IT ONLINE; copy charges must be uniform for the same record and may not exceed the "prevailing commercial rate"; copy charges may NOT be applied to records transmitted in electronic format (though labor to convert/transmit electronically is chargeable); no statutory free-page or free-hour threshold. "The full amount of the total cost must be paid at the time of the production of the request." Verified city example (City of Florence, from its own site): $0.35 per page for paper copies; search/retrieval/redaction labor at the lowest-paid-capable-employee prorated salary with a stated floor of $20/hour; $0.35/page copy charge expressly inapplicable to electronic delivery; 25% deposit may be required. State-agency schedules commonly run $0.10-$0.25/page. No separate statutory programming-fee provision; RCFP notes agencies may charge for customized database searches including the cost of writing a program, under the same actual-cost/lowest-paid-capable-employee rule.

**Labor charges:** Yes — search, retrieval, AND redaction time are all expressly chargeable (§ 30-4-30(B)), a point SC's 2017 amendments settled. Cap: the prorated hourly salary of the lowest-paid employee who has the necessary skill and training to perform the request (custodian's reasonable discretion; MASC notes the applicable rate may differ by task since different requests require different skills). No free-hours threshold in the statute; review time to decide whether records are exempt is not listed as chargeable (only search, retrieval, redaction, copying).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
§ 30-4-30(B): a deposit "not to exceed twenty-five percent of the total reasonably anticipated cost" for reproduction of the records may be required BEFORE the public body searches for or makes copies (requiring a deposit is optional, per MASC). When a deposit is required, the production deadline becomes 30 calendar days (35 for records more than 24 months old) from RECEIPT OF THE DEPOSIT (§ 30-4-30(C)). Balance: the full amount of the total cost must be paid at the time of production.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Statutory discretionary public-interest waiver, § 30-4-30(B): documents "may be furnished when appropriate without charge or at a reduced charge where the agency determines that waiver or reduction of the fee is in the public interest because furnishing the information can be considered as primarily benefiting the general public" (mirrors federal FOIA language). Discretionary — no requester entitlement and no indigency waiver in the statute. MASC advises cities to apply waivers consistently.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute: payment of the full cost is due "at the time of the production of the request" (§ 30-4-30(B)), so a body may withhold delivery until paid; if a required 25% deposit is never paid, the production clock never starts and the body need not begin the search. No statutory abandonment/closure timeline. Common city practice: requests are administratively closed after a stated period of non-payment (often 30 days) and some bodies require payment of prior unpaid balances before processing new requests — practice, not statute.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** Only one statutory shift: requiring a deposit moves the 30/35-calendar-day production clock to run from deposit receipt (§ 30-4-30(C)). The 10/20-business-day determination clock has NO statutory pause — no clarification tolling, no extension notices. A § 30-4-110(A) public-body suit over an improper request may result in court-ordered relief, but the statute does not say filing itself stays the deadlines. The determination window already excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and legal public holidays by definition.

## REMEDIES

§ 30-4-100(A): a citizen of the State may sue in circuit court for declaratory judgment and/or injunctive relief no later than 1 year after the alleged violation (or 1 year after a public vote in public session, whichever is later); the chief administrative judge must schedule an initial hearing within 10 days of service on all parties, and if not finally ruled on then, a scheduling order must conclude the action within 6 months of filing. Attorney fees, § 30-4-100(B): a prevailing requester "may be awarded" reasonable attorney's fees and litigation costs specific to the request (discretionary in text; Campbell v. Marion County Hospital Dist. holds denial of fees to a fully prevailing plaintiff can be an abuse of discretion); partial prevailing = discretionary full or partial fees. § 30-4-110(D): a court determination that records are NOT subject to disclosure constitutes a finding of good faith and is a complete bar to attorney's fees even if that determination is later reversed on appeal. § 30-4-110(F): if the body "arbitrarily and capriciously" violated the Act by refusal or delay, the court may — in addition to actual or compensatory damages or equitable relief — impose a civil fine of $500. Criminal misdemeanor penalties for FOIA violations were removed by Act 67 of 2017 (current § 30-4-110 contains only the civil scheme; a separate misdemeanor survives under § 30-2-50 for commercial-solicitation misuse of personal information).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for SC cities: (1) No purpose or residency field is required by statute — but include a commercial-solicitation certification/notice (§ 30-2-50 requires notifying every requester of the prohibition; a checkbox plus notice text satisfies it); note the statutory exclusion of incarcerated requesters (§ 30-4-30(A)). (2) Auto-acknowledgment is practice-only but valuable because the DETERMINATION deadline (10 or 20 business days keyed to whether records are more than 24 months old) is the real trigger — the portal should ask the record date range at intake to compute the right deadline. (3) Build a hard deadline alarm: silence past the determination deadline = request DEEMED APPROVED as to nonexempt records — a serious litigation-exposure flag distinctive to SC; missing the 30/35-calendar-day production deadline is separately actionable with a possible $500 fine and attorney fees. (4) Fee module: statute REQUIRES the fee schedule be posted online; labor at the lowest-paid-capable-employee prorated hourly salary for search/retrieval/redaction (rate may vary by task); per-page copy fee at or below prevailing commercial rate, uniform per record (verified example: Florence $0.35/page, labor from $20/hr); suppress copy charges automatically for electronic delivery. (5) Deposit module: cap at 25% of reasonably anticipated cost; on deposit receipt, start the production clock (30/35 calendar days); collect balance before release (payment due at production). (6) Fee-waiver question: discretionary public-interest waiver — offer a justification text box. (7) No commercial-rate or extended-commercial-deadline module needed. (8) No administrative appeal workflow — denial letters must state reasons; escalation path is circuit court with a 10-day initial-hearing rule, so route denials through legal review. (9) Certain records (recent minutes, 14-day incident reports, jail rosters) must be available for immediate in-person inspection without a written request (§ 30-4-30(D)) — flag these request types for instant handling.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) Whether a § 30-4-110(A) public-body suit tolls the 10/20-day determination or deemed-approval clock — statute silent; no appellate case found on point. (2) How consistently courts award full fees to fully prevailing requesters under the discretionary 'may be awarded' text post-2017 was not verified against post-2017 appellate decisions (pre-2017 Campbell reversed a fee denial as abuse of discretion). (3) Enforcement standing for non-SC-citizens (§ 30-4-100 says 'a citizen of the State') is untested in reported SC cases as far as confirmed. (4) Whether email submission legally triggers the clock is not expressly addressed — § 30-4-30(C) requires a 'written request' (universally treated in practice as including email, and determinations may be 'electronically transmitted'), but no case confirms; City of Florence's page (verified) lists only online form/mail/hand-delivery, so per-city intake channels vary. (5) Non-payment closure windows and prior-balance rules are pure local practice; confirmed no statutory basis. (6) The Sloan I (2006) public-body holding was taken from secondary sources; the 369 S.C. 20 citation itself was verified from the 2011 Sloan opinion. (7) The claim that Act 67 of 2017 repealed FOIA's criminal misdemeanor was not verified against the session-law text, though the current §§ 30-4-100/110 (2025 Code) contain only the civil scheme, corroborating it. (8) Florence is the sole city fee example verified directly from a city website ($0.35/page, $20/hr floor, 25% deposit); Charleston and Columbia schedules (and the Columbia portal-vendor detail) were not verified; per-page rates vary by municipality. (9) Amendment sweep verified through the 2025 Code: §§ 30-4-30, -100, -110 all show 2017 Act No. 67 (eff. May 19, 2017) as the last amendment; 2025-26 S.6 (would cut the determination deadline to 5 days, add a court-extension petition, and flip deemed approval to deemed denial) remains in Senate Judiciary with no action since introduction on Jan. 14, 2025, and H.3161 (exemptions) is likewise unenacted — but a full 2026-session enrolled-act sweep was not possible, so watch S.6 in particular.
