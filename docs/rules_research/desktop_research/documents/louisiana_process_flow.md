# Louisiana City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Louisiana city, under the Louisiana Public Records Law (La. R.S. 44:1 et seq.; La. Const. art. XII, sec. 3). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** Immediate production if available; in-use records: access set within 3 business days; written determination or time estimate due 5 business days from receipt; silence = deemed denial (R.S. 44:32-35). Weekends/holidays excluded. No admin appeal; mandamus; up to $100/day penalties paid by public body.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Purpose may NOT be demanded: "The custodian shall make no inquiry of any person who applies for a public record, except an inquiry as to the age and identification of the person" (R.S. 44:32(A)(1)); use of records to criticize the public body cannot restrict access (AG Op. 93-0482). Inspection is limited to persons "of the age of majority" (18+), but "any person may obtain a copy" (R.S. 44:31(B)(1)-(2)). Because the custodian may verify age/identity, fully anonymous requests are not guaranteed, though the inquiry is limited to age/ID; per LLA guidance and AG Op. 17-0056A, contact info may be requested but not required as a condition of responding, and ID may be examined but not retained. No residency requirement for municipal records — the 2024 residency restriction applies ONLY to records of the office of the governor: R.S. 44:31.1(B) (Acts 2024, No. 638) limits governor's-office requests to Louisiana residents and lets that custodian verify identity, age, and residency. The broader citizens-only bill (SB 423, 2024) was withdrawn and did not pass.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None in the general Public Records Law: no duty to disclose commercial purpose, no separate deadline, and no higher fee tier for commercial requesters (purpose inquiries are affirmatively prohibited by R.S. 44:32(A)(1)). Record-specific statutes outside the general law (e.g., accident reports, DMV records) carry their own commercial restrictions, but they do not apply to ordinary municipal records.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory acknowledgment duty. The functional equivalent is the 5-day obligation to issue either a written determination or a written estimate of the time reasonably necessary (R.S. 44:32(D), 44:35(A)); silence past 5 business days is a deemed denial permitting suit. R.S. 44:35(A) expressly covers "in-person, written, or electronic" requests, so the clock runs on emailed requests. City practice: portal-based cities (e.g., New Orleans NextRequest) auto-acknowledge on receipt; email-based cities typically send an acknowledgment within the 5-day window doubling as the determination or time-estimate response.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Layered scheme, not one fixed production deadline. (1) Records immediately available and not in active use must be presented immediately (R.S. 44:33(B)(1)). (2) If the record is in active use or storage, the custodian must promptly certify that in writing and fix a day and hour for access within 3 days, exclusive of Saturdays, Sundays, and legal public holidays (R.S. 44:33(B)(1) — verified current; last amended 1982). (3) If the custodian questions whether the record is public, he must give a written determination with reasons within 5 days, exclusive of weekends/holidays, of receipt of the request (R.S. 44:32(D) — raised from 3 to 5 days by Acts 2022, No. 770). (4) Constructive denial: under R.S. 44:35(A) the requester may sue if 5 days (excluding weekends/holidays) pass from the date of the "in-person, written, or electronic request" without either a written determination or "an estimate of the time reasonably necessary for collection, segregation, redaction, examination, or review" — a timely time-estimate letter satisfies the 5-day clock, with production then due within a "reasonable" time (jurisprudence; no statutory outer limit). (5) Since Acts 2024, No. 411, R.S. 44:32(A)(2) lets the custodian deny a request he "reasonably determines... would substantially disrupt required government operations," but only after reasonable attempts to narrow or specify the request with the requester. No other formal extension mechanism exists.

---

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

No statutory duty to assist, but since Acts 2024, No. 411 there is a structured path for burdensome requests: the custodian may deny a request he reasonably determines "would substantially disrupt required government operations" ONLY after reasonable attempts to narrow or specify the request with the requester (R.S. 44:32(A)(2)) — so a narrowing dialogue is now a statutory precondition to that denial. Jurisprudence: a request need not name a specific document; a "reasonable description of the information sought" suffices (Nungesser v. Brown, 664 So.2d 132 (La. App. 1 Cir. 1995), cited by the Legislative Auditor's FAQ); requests may be made in person or by letter (Elliott v. District Attorney, 664 So.2d 122 (La. App. 1 Cir. 1995)), and R.S. 44:35(A) recognizes electronic requests. No duty to create records or answer interrogatories (Nungesser; AG opinions). The statute does not expressly toll the 5-day clock during clarification.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** R.S. 44:34 (verified verbatim): if the record is not in the custodian's custody or control, he must "promptly certify this in writing to the applicant," stating in detail the reason for the record's absence, its location, who then has custody, and the manner/method and exact time it left his custody, plus "ample and detailed answers" to applicant inquiries. No duty to create a record that does not exist. A written no-records certification is the required response, not silence.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Segregability required: where a record contains both public and nonpublic matter, the custodian may separate the nonpublic portion and must make the public portion available (R.S. 44:32(B)); officials must segregate so the public "can reasonably view" records, and if segregation is unreasonably burdensome or expensive the official must so state in writing (R.S. 44:33(A)). Redaction/review labor is NOT chargeable: "no fee shall be charged to any person to examine or review any public records" (R.S. 44:32(C)(3)), and fees are limited to actual copying/reproduction costs (AG Op. 15-0056).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing within 5 days (exclusive of weekends/holidays) of receipt, stating the determination, reasons, and the legal basis exempting the record (R.S. 44:32(D)). A substantial-disruption denial additionally requires prior reasonable attempts to narrow the request (R.S. 44:32(A)(2)). Access is a constitutional right (La. Const. art. XII, sec. 3); exemptions are narrowly construed and the burden of proving a record is not subject to inspection rests on the custodian (R.S. 44:31(B)(3)). No index/Vaughn requirement, but courts review de novo and may examine records in camera (R.S. 44:35(B)).

**Administrative appeal:** None — judicial only. Louisiana has no records ombudsman or FOI commission; the Attorney General issues advisory opinions and public education but does not adjudicate or enforce. Remedy is suit (mandamus/injunctive/declaratory) in the district court for the parish where the custodian is located (R.S. 44:35(A)).

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Cities (non-state agencies) may charge only "reasonable fees for making copies of public records, which may include the transmission of electronic copies" and, since Act 247 of 2023 (eff. 8/1/2023), any custodian electing to charge must adopt a reasonable fee schedule and post it where the public can readily access it (R.S. 44:32(C)(1)(a)). Since Acts 2024, No. 411, the same subsection authorizes the custodian to "request payment of fees in advance of production." State benchmark: uniform schedule of $0.25/page minimum (first copy) for paper/microfiche up to 8.5x14; larger formats at actual cost (LAC 4:I.301, adopted under R.S. 39:241) — this binds state agencies, and cities commonly use it as a reference point. Examination/inspection is free (R.S. 44:32(C)(3)). No statutory search, review, or programming charges for standard requests; electronic copies may carry a reasonable actual-cost fee (R.S. 44:32(C)(1)(a); AG Op. 15-0056). Free copies to qualifying student media at public postsecondary institutions (R.S. 44:32(C)(2)(b), Act 337 of 2022). City rates vary and must be checked against each city's posted schedule (New Orleans has posted roughly $0.50/page paper plus media fees — practice, verify current schedule).

**Labor charges:** Not chargeable for ordinary requests: fees must reflect actual copying/reproduction cost; staff search, review, and redaction time cannot be billed (R.S. 44:32(C)(3); AG Op. 15-0056). Sole statutory labor charge: if the requester wants access OUTSIDE regular office hours, reasonable staffing compensation is payable in advance (R.S. 44:32(A)(1)) — largely irrelevant to an emailed copies request.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Since Acts 2024, No. 411, prepayment is expressly statutory: "The custodian may request payment of fees in advance of production" (R.S. 44:32(C)(1)(a)). There is no percentage cap or formal deposit scheme — the advance amount is simply the posted-schedule fee estimate. Separate statutory advance payment exists for after-hours staffing (R.S. 44:32(A)(1)). Portal cities typically send an itemized estimate/invoice and release copies on payment.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary only. Copies "may be furnished without charge or at a reduced charge to indigent citizens of this state" (R.S. 44:32(C)(2)); for state agencies, LAC 4:I.301 (under R.S. 39:241) requires free/reduced copies for indigents and persons whose use "will be limited to a public purpose" — the public-purpose waiver is written for state agencies, and cities may mirror it voluntarily. Mandatory free copies only for qualifying student media outlets at public postsecondary institutions (R.S. 44:32(C)(2)(b)). No statutory public-interest waiver mandate for municipal requesters; case law on judicial review of waiver discretion is thin (earlier-cited waiver-split cases could not be verified and are omitted).

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute now implies copies may be conditioned on payment (custodian "may request payment of fees in advance of production," R.S. 44:32(C)(1)(a)); cities close requests administratively if the estimate/invoice goes unpaid after a stated period (practice — commonly around 30 days in portal policies; no statutory closure rule). Non-payment does not excuse the 5-day written response duty; it only pauses production of copies.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No formal tolling provisions. Practical pauses: (1) a timely written "estimate of the time reasonably necessary for collection, segregation, redaction, examination, or review" converts the 5-day clock into a reasonableness standard (R.S. 44:35(A)); (2) records in active use defer access up to 3 business days (R.S. 44:33(B)(1)); (3) awaiting advance payment pauses production (R.S. 44:32(C)(1)(a)); (4) the mandatory narrowing dialogue before any substantial-disruption denial (R.S. 44:32(A)(2)) delays resolution in practice, without express statutory tolling. Weekends and legal public holidays are excluded from all statutory day counts.

## REMEDIES

Suit for writ of mandamus, injunctive, or declaratory relief after denial or 5 business days of silence following an in-person, written, or electronic request (R.S. 44:35(A)); de novo review, custodian bears burden, in camera inspection allowed (44:35(B)); trial and appeal by preference/summary proceeding (44:35(C)). Attorney fees: MANDATORY ("shall") for a fully prevailing requester; discretionary if prevailing in part (44:35(D)); fee awards capped at AG-approved outside-counsel rates (44:35(F), Act 394 of 2018). If withholding was arbitrary/capricious the court may award actual damages, and for unreasonable/arbitrary failure to respond, civil penalties up to $100/day (excluding weekends/holidays) (44:35(E)). Since Act 493 of 2024 (HB 768, eff. June 5, 2024), custodians have NO personal liability — the public body pays all penalties, damages, and fees (44:35(E)(2) personal-liability provision repealed). Criminal penalties (R.S. 44:37, verified): $100-$1,000 fine or 1-6 months first offense; $250-$2,000 and/or 2-6 months thereafter; record destruction/falsification separately criminalized (R.S. 14:132, 14:133).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields: collect requester name and an age-of-majority attestation (custodian may verify age/ID only) — do NOT ask purpose; a purpose question would exceed R.S. 44:32(A)(1). Contact info can be requested but must not be a mandatory gate per LLA/AG-opinion guidance (portals in practice require it). No residency screen for city records (governor's office only, R.S. 44:31.1(B)). No commercial-rate module needed (no commercial differential). Auto-acknowledgment should double as the statutory 5-business-day written response by including either a determination or a time-reasonably-necessary estimate — build a 5-business-day SLA timer excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and Louisiana legal holidays, with escalation before deemed denial (R.S. 44:35(A) expressly covers electronic requests). Burdensome-request workflow: before any substantial-disruption denial, the system must document reasonable attempts to narrow or specify the request with the requester (R.S. 44:32(A)(2), new 2024). Fee module: per-page rate from the city's POSTED schedule (posting mandatory since Act 247 of 2023; state benchmark $0.25/page); zero charges for search/review/redaction labor; free inspection option. Deposit logic: advance payment of fees before production is now expressly statutory (R.S. 44:32(C)(1)(a), 2024) but uncapped — keep estimates itemized to actual copy cost. Fee-waiver question: optional indigency checkbox (discretionary waiver). Litigation-exposure flags: missed 5-day response (deemed denial, up to $100/day exposure and mandatory attorney fees paid by the city), missing written legal basis on denials, disruption denials without documented narrowing attempts, and no-records closures without an R.S. 44:34 certification letter.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) Acts 2024, No. 411 is confirmed from the official legis.la.gov codification as the latest amendment to R.S. 44:32, and the current text verifiably contains the substantial-disruption denial (A)(2) and advance-payment sentence (C)(1)(a); however, the sponsoring bill number for Act 411 could not be isolated, and its effective date is presumed the default Aug. 1, 2024. (2) Exact sub-numbering within 44:32(C) (indigent waiver at C(2) vs. C(3); free-examination clause at C(3)) varies across codification snapshots after the 2022-2024 amendments — verify subsection letters before quoting them in legal documents. (3) SB 493 (2026 R.S., Reese — first 20 pages free, 5 cents/page, $10 e-copy cap, eff. 8/1/26) was confirmed "read by title; withdrawn from the files of the Senate," so it is NOT law as of July 2026; monitor future sessions for fee-standardization revivals. (4) Closure-for-nonpayment timelines (~30 days) remain city practice, not statute. (5) New Orleans's $0.50/page and media fees are unverified against the city's current posted schedule; every Louisiana city must post its own schedule and rates vary. (6) AG Ops. 15-0056, 17-0056A, and 93-0482 were sourced through LLA/secondary guidance rather than retrieved originals. (7) The previously cited fee-waiver case split (State v. Jean; Diggs v. Pennington) could not be verified from any strong source and was removed. (8) Older guides (incl. the 2022 LLA FAQ) still state a 3-day written-determination deadline; the current statute says 5 days after Acts 2022, No. 770 — resolved in favor of the codified text.
