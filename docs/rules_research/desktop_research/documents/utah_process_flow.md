# Utah City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Utah city, under the Government Records Access and Management Act (GRAMA) (Utah Code § 63G-2-101 et seq.). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** GRAMA: respond ASAP, max 10 business days (5 expedited public-benefit); 2026 bill to raise to 15 days FAILED. Silence = deemed denial. Extensions: +5 days legal review, 15 days total heavy redaction, estimate for voluminous. Appeals: CAO 30d, then Gov. Records Office director or court.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
No anonymity: a request must contain the requester's name, mailing address, email address (if the person has one and is willing to accept email communications about the request), and daytime telephone number, plus a description identifying the record "with reasonable specificity" (§ 63G-2-204(1)(a); the phone-number element carries no "if available" qualifier in the current text — verified against the current-law reprint in 2026 SB 64). No purpose statement may be demanded for ordinary access — "a person has the right to inspect a public record free of charge" and to take a copy (§ 63G-2-201(1)(a)); purpose becomes relevant only if the requester voluntarily asserts grounds for a fee waiver, expedited (public-benefit) treatment, or status-based access to private/protected records. No residency requirement — any person may request. A single record request may not be submitted to multiple governmental entities (§ 63G-2-204(1)(b)(i)).

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No commercial branch in GRAMA: no duty to disclose commercial purpose, no different deadline, and no separate commercial fee rate. Commercial purpose surfaces only negatively at the margins: (1) the discretionary fee waiver under § 63G-2-203(4) turns on whether release "primarily benefits the public rather than a person," so commercial requesters rarely get waivers; (2) in litigation, since the 2025 amendment (ch. 188, HB 69) a court "may not award attorney fees or costs" at all "if the purpose of the litigation is primarily to benefit the requester's financial or commercial interest" (§ 63G-2-802(3)(c) — now an absolute bar, no longer merely "not ordinarily"). No resale restrictions in GRAMA itself.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty — the only statutory obligation is the substantive response (approve/deny/no-record notice/extraordinary-circumstances notice) within 10 business days under § 63G-2-204(4). State Archives guidance encourages responding "as soon as reasonably possible." Common city practice: cities using the state Open Records Portal (openrecords.utah.gov) or vendor portals (e.g., NextRequest) send automatic receipt confirmations; that is practice, not law.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Entity must respond "as soon as reasonably possible, but no later than 10 business days after receiving a written request" — the response must (i) approve the request and provide a copy of the record, (ii) deny under § 63G-2-205, (iii) notify the requester that it does not maintain the record, or (iv) give notice of extraordinary circumstances (§ 63G-2-204(4)(b), verified from current compiled code, last amended 2023 ch. 173). 5 business days if the requester demonstrates that an expedited response "benefits the public rather than the person" (media typically qualify). Extraordinary circumstances listed in § 63G-2-204(6) trigger the § 63G-2-204(7) extension limits: record in use by another entity — return within 5 business days (7)(a); record in use for audit — notify when available (7)(b); voluminous request / many concurrent requests / large search needed — disclose records as located, give a time estimate, complete as soon as reasonably possible (7)(c); legal counsel review — 5 business days after the original deadline expires (7)(d); extensive editing/segregation — 15 business days from the original request (7)(e); computer programming — complete as soon as reasonably possible (7)(f). Failure to provide records or issue a denial within the time allowed "is considered the equivalent of a determination denying access" (§ 63G-2-204(9)), which starts the 30-day CAO appeal clock. If the request was submitted to the wrong office of the entity, it must be promptly forwarded and the clock starts on receipt by the correct office (§ 63G-2-204(8)). CONFIRMED: 2026 SB 64, which would have moved the standard deadline to 15 business days, FAILED (enacting clause struck 3/6/2026; filed in bills not passed) — 10 business days remains current law as of July 2026.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

The statute requires the requester to identify the record "with reasonable specificity" (§ 63G-2-204(1)(a)); a request failing that standard may be denied. GRAMA imposes no express duty to assist or confer, but § 63G-2-201(7) makes clear the entity need not create a record, "compile, format, manipulate, package, summarize, or tailor information," or provide a record in a format/medium/program not currently maintained. Common city practice is to contact the requester to narrow scope (the state portal facilitates clarification messages); the statute does not expressly toll the 10-day clock for clarification, but entities treat an unreasonably unspecific request as not yet a valid request. The government records ombudsman (within the Government Records Office framework) is available to help requesters frame requests and mediate disputes.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Partial statutory script: one of the four permitted responses is to "notify the requester that it does not maintain the record requested" (§ 63G-2-204(4)(b)(iii)); there is no duty to create a record (§ 63G-2-201(7)). A request submitted to the wrong office of the entity must be promptly forwarded to the appropriate office, with the clock starting on receipt there (§ 63G-2-204(8)). State Archives guidance confirms a government's claim that it does not maintain the requested record is an appealable issue. A requester may not blast one request to multiple entities (§ 63G-2-204(1)(b)(i)).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Segregability is built into the classification scheme: an entity must allow access to public portions and segregate private, controlled, or protected portions (§ 63G-2-202(3) segregation of multi-subject records; § 63G-2-201(13)(c)(ii) segregation of electronic records where doable without undue expenditure). Redaction/segregation labor: chargeable as staff time at actual cost under § 63G-2-203(2), BUT the entity may not charge for "reviewing a record to determine whether it is subject to disclosure" (except as permitted by § 63G-2-203(2)(a)(ii)), for inspection, or for the first quarter hour of staff time (§ 63G-2-203(5)(b)) — so pure disclosure-review time is free while compilation/segregation time beyond 15 minutes may be billed. Note the exception: the free first quarter hour does not apply to a non-media requester who submitted a separate request within the 10 days before the current request (§ 63G-2-203(5)(c)). "Extensive editing" to segregate is an extraordinary circumstance extending the deadline to 15 business days from the request (§ 63G-2-204(6)(g), (7)(e)).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing within the § 63G-2-204 deadline and must include: a description of the record or portions withheld (to the extent disclosure doesn't reveal exempt matter), citations to the GRAMA provision, court rule/order, state or federal statute or regulation that exempts the record, a statement of the right to appeal to the chief administrative officer, and the appeal time limits and contact information (§ 63G-2-205(2)). The entity may not destroy or give up custody of a denied record until the appeal period expires or the appeals process ends (§ 63G-2-205(3)). Silence past the deadline = deemed denial (§ 63G-2-204(9)). Classification scheme: records are public unless properly classified private, controlled, or protected (§§ 63G-2-301 to -305); on appeal to the director, a weighing/balancing of interests can order disclosure of properly classified records where interests favoring access are greater than or equal to interests favoring restriction (§ 63G-2-403(11)(b)).

**Administrative appeal:** Two-tier administrative route with fixed deadlines. Tier 1: appeal to the entity's chief administrative officer (for a city: mayor/manager as designated by ordinance) within 30 days after the denial notice, deemed denial, or extraordinary-circumstances claim (§ 63G-2-401(1)); CAO must decide within 10 business days of receipt (5 if expedited public benefit; 12 business days where a business-confidentiality claimant must be notified) (§ 63G-2-401(5)(a)); CAO silence = deemed affirmance of the denial or of the extraordinary-circumstances claim (§ 63G-2-401(5)(b)). Tier 2 (post-SB 277, 2025 ch. 476): appeal to the director of the Government Records Office ("director" defined at § 63G-2-400.5(3); office replaced the 7-member State Records Committee, dissolved July 1, 2025) within 30 days after the decision being appealed, or within 45 days after the record request was made if the CAO never decided (§ 63G-2-403(1)(a)-(b)); director schedules a hearing within 7 business days of the appeal, set 16–64 calendar days after the notice of appeal (§ 63G-2-403(4)(a)); decision due no later than 7 business days after the hearing (§ 63G-2-403(11)(a)); no decision within 73 calendar days of filing = order denying the appeal (§ 63G-2-403(13)(a)). Appeal to the director is optional — after the CAO (or local board) the requester may instead go straight to district court (§ 63G-2-402). Cities alternative: under § 63G-2-701 a city may establish a 3-member local appeals board (one city employee + two members of the public); absent a board, CAO decisions go to the director under § 63G-2-403; appealing a board decision does not waive judicial review. Director may order compliance and impose a civil penalty of up to $500 for each day of continuing noncompliance, considering gravity and whether the failure was neglectful or willful (§ 63G-2-403(15)(d); section last amended 2026 ch. 324).

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Statute sets no per-page rate: fees are "a reasonable fee to cover the governmental entity's actual cost of providing a record" (§ 63G-2-203(1)(a)), including staff time to compile, format, search, retrieve, and the incremental cost of electronic services (§ 63G-2-203(2)(a)). Hourly labor may not exceed "the salary of the lowest paid employee" who, in the custodian's discretion, has the necessary skill and training (§ 63G-2-203(2)(b)). No charge for: inspecting a record, reviewing a record to determine disclosability (with a narrow (2)(a)(ii) exception), or the first quarter hour (15 minutes) of staff time (§ 63G-2-203(5)(b)); the free quarter hour is unavailable to non-media requesters who filed another request within the prior 10 days (§ 63G-2-203(5)(c)). § 63G-2-203 was last amended by 2025 ch. 33. Cities must adopt fee guidelines by ordinance/consolidated fee schedule under § 63G-2-701. Typical city numbers (practice, not statute; e.g., St. George PD, Salt Lake County): $0.25/page black-and-white, $0.50/page color, ~$5.00 per CD/flash drive, staff hourly rates roughly $20–$50 depending on employee class. Programming/formatting for electronic output billable as actual incremental cost.

**Labor charges:** Yes — staff time for search/retrieval/compilation/formatting is chargeable at actual cost, capped at the hourly salary of the lowest-paid employee with the necessary skill and training (§ 63G-2-203(2)); first 15 minutes (quarter hour) of staff time is free (unless the non-media requester filed a separate request in the prior 10 days, § 63G-2-203(5)(c)), and time spent reviewing a record to determine whether it is disclosable may not be charged (§ 63G-2-203(5)(b)). No statutory cap on total labor hours billed beyond the actual-cost/reasonableness standard.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
§ 63G-2-203(8)(a): before beginning to process a request, an entity may require payment of past fees and future estimated fees when (i) fees are expected to exceed $50, or (ii) the requester has not paid an invoiced fee from a previous request. No percentage cap — the full estimate may be demanded. A fee or estimate may be appealed like a denial. City practice mirrors the $50 statutory trigger.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary: an entity "may fulfill a record request without charge" if it determines that (a) releasing the record "primarily benefits the public rather than a person" (public-interest waiver — the usual media basis), (b) the requester is the subject of the record or an individual specified in § 63G-2-202(1) or (2), or (c) the requester's legal rights are directly implicated by the information and the requester is impecunious (§ 63G-2-203(4)). A fee-waiver denial is appealable through the same channels as an access denial (§ 63G-2-401(1); State Archives FAQ confirms). No mandatory statutory waiver amounts (no free-copy quota beyond the free first quarter hour of staff time).

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
If estimated fees exceed $50 or previously invoiced fees are unpaid, the entity may decline to begin processing until prepayment (§ 63G-2-203(8)); prior unpaid balances justify requiring payment of those balances plus prepayment on any new request. In practice cities close/hold requests pending payment, and unclaimed/unpaid requests are administratively closed (practice, not statute). The deemed-denial clock does not run while the entity has properly demanded prepayment before beginning to process.

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** (1) Extraordinary circumstances under § 63G-2-204(6)–(7) extend the deadline per category (5 extra business days for legal review; 15 business days from the request for extensive segregation; date-estimate/ASAP for voluminous or programming cases). (2) Proper prepayment demand under § 63G-2-203(8) suspends processing until payment. (3) Misdirected requests: the response clock starts only when the correct office receives the forwarded request (§ 63G-2-204(8)). (4) On appeal, the 30-day deadlines to reach the Government Records Office director or district court are suspended during mediation with the government records ombudsman, from the mediation request until mediation concludes (§§ 63G-2-403(1)(c), 63G-2-404(1)(b)). No statutory tolling for clarification requests.

## REMEDIES

Judicial review by petition to district court within 30 days after the CAO, local board, or director order/decision (director step optional, §§ 63G-2-402, 63G-2-404(1)); court decides all questions of fact and law de novo, without a jury (§ 63G-2-404(6)). Injunction available against violating entities (§ 63G-2-802(2)). Attorney fees — SIGNIFICANTLY NARROWED by 2025 ch. 188 (HB 69, eff. 5/7/2025): court "may assess" reasonable fees and costs only if the requester substantially prevails AND the court finds the defending party acted in bad faith (§ 63G-2-802(3)(a)), considering public benefit, the requester's interest, and whether the entity had a reasonable basis (3)(b); fees may NOT be awarded if the litigation's purpose is primarily to benefit the requester's financial or commercial interest (3)(c) — absolute bar; no fees/costs for the administrative phase (§ 63G-2-802(4)); the entity can recover fees against a requester who acted in bad faith if the entity substantially prevails (§ 63G-2-802(5)); fees on appeal limited to those incurred 20 or more days after the requester provided a written explanation of the basis for the requester's position (§ 63G-2-802(6)). Criminal: intentional refusal by a public employee to release a record known to be disclosable, intentional wrongful disclosure/use of private, controlled, or protected records, or gaining access by false pretenses, bribery, or theft is a class B misdemeanor, with good-faith and whistleblower defenses (§ 63G-2-801, last amended 2025 chs. 188, 208, 476). Director civil penalty up to $500/day for noncompliance with disclosure orders (§ 63G-2-403(15)(d)).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields: require name, mailing address, and daytime phone (statutorily required — no anonymous requests); collect email with a consent checkbox (the statute lists email address "if the person has an email address and is willing to accept communications by email" — § 63G-2-204(1)(a)). Purpose question: keep OPTIONAL — purpose may not be demanded; but include optional checkboxes for (a) public-interest fee waiver, (b) subject-of-record, (c) impecunious+legal-rights waiver, and (d) expedited 5-day treatment with a "how does speed benefit the public" text box (media). No commercial-rate module needed (no commercial branch), but note waivers are unlikely for commercial purposes and fee-shifting in litigation is barred for primarily commercial suits. Deposit logic: trigger prepayment demand when estimate > $50 or account has an unpaid invoiced balance; hold clock until paid. Fee engine: free first 15 minutes of staff time (disable for non-media requesters with another request in the prior 10 days per 203(5)(c)), no charge for disclosure review or inspection, per-page rates from each city's consolidated fee schedule (commonly $0.25 B/W, $0.50 color, ~$5 media). Auto-acknowledgment: good practice, not required. Deadline engine: 10 business days standard, 5 expedited, extraordinary-circumstance codes mapping to +5 business days after deadline (legal review), 15 business days from request (segregation), 5 business days to retrieve (record in use), or date-estimate/ASAP (voluminous/programming); silence = deemed denial at day 10 — flag as exposure event because it opens the 30-day CAO appeal window. Wrong-entity routing: forward promptly; clock restarts at correct office (204(8)); block multi-agency blasts (one entity per request, 204(1)(b)). Appeals module: route to city CAO (mayor/manager) with 30-day requester deadline and 10-business-day decision timer (5 expedited / 12 business-confidentiality; silence = affirmance); then local 3-member appeals board (if city has one) or Government Records Office director (30 days from decision; 45 days from the original request if CAO silent); mediation with the records ombudsman suspends deadlines. Track director noncompliance-penalty exposure ($500/day). Support vexatious-requester status flags (director designation on entity petition, up to 1 year, § 63G-2-209). Many small Utah cities take requests through the state Open Records Portal (openrecords.utah.gov) — consider integration. Litigation-fee exposure for cities is now low (bad-faith prerequisite since May 2025), but deemed denials still invite suits and penalties.
