# Wisconsin City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Wisconsin city, under the Wisconsin Public Records Law (Open Records Law) (Wis. Stat. §§ 19.31–19.39). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** No fixed deadline: fill or deny 'as soon as practicable and without delay' (s.19.35(4)(a)). DOJ benchmark: ~10 working days for simple requests. No extensions regime; unreasonable delay = constructive denial via mandamus. Attorney fees only after court-ordered relief (Frame Park 2022).

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Strong anonymity protection. Wis. Stat. § 19.35(1)(i): "no request ... may be refused because the person making the request is unwilling to be identified or to state the purpose of the request," and no request may be refused because it is received by mail (email treated the same in universal practice) unless prepayment is required under § 19.35(3)(f). ID may be required only where the record is kept at a private residence, or where security reasons or federal law/regulations require, or where the requester seeks heightened access to records containing personally identifiable information about themselves (§§ 19.35(1)(am), 19.35(4)(c)). No residency requirement — "any requester" (§ 19.32(3)); the only excluded class is committed/incarcerated persons, who may only request records containing specific references to themselves or their minor children. Purpose/identity can become relevant in the common-law balancing test only in exceptional cases (e.g., documented safety risk).

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No general commercial-purpose branch: same deadline and fees regardless of use, and purpose may not be demanded (§ 19.35(1)(i)). ONE narrow exception created by 2023 Wis. Act 253 (eff. Mar. 31, 2024), § 19.35(3)(h): a law enforcement agency may charge the actual, necessary and direct cost of redacting recorded AUDIO/VIDEO content (at the lowest-paid capable employee's rate), but an individual requesting 10 or fewer recordings per year avoids the fee by written certification that the content will not be used "for financial gain" (damages awards excluded); false certification carries a $10,000 forfeiture per violation. Fees also don't apply to directly involved parties or officer-involved-death requests, and are forfeited if no advance written fee estimate was given.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
Not required by statute. DOJ Compliance Guide recommends, as best practice only, sending a brief acknowledgment with a projected response timeframe (especially when the requester sets a unilateral deadline). Common city practice (e.g., Madison's GovQA portal) is automatic acknowledgment with tracking number.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
NO fixed statutory day count. Wis. Stat. § 19.35(4)(a): the authority "shall, as soon as practicable and without delay, either fill the request or notify the requester of the authority's determination to deny the request in whole or in part and the reasons therefor." So the deadline event is either production OR a reasoned denial determination — there is no separate "determination then produce later" structure and no statutory extension mechanism; reasonableness depends on the nature of the request, staff, and resources. Wisconsin DOJ Office of Open Government guidance (Public Records Law Compliance Guide, June 2025) treats about 10 working days as generally reasonable for simple requests of easily identifiable records; complex requests may reasonably take longer. Custodians need not honor requester-imposed deadlines. There is no statutory deemed-denial day count, but courts treat unreasonable delay as the functional equivalent of denial, enforceable by mandamus under § 19.37(1) (see WIREdata, Inc. v. Village of Sussex, 2008 WI 69; DOJ guide).

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

Wis. Stat. § 19.35(1)(h): a request is sufficient if it "reasonably describes the requested record or the information requested"; a request "without a reasonable limitation as to subject matter or length of time represented by the record" is insufficient and may be rejected on that ground. No magic words; no form may be mandated. No express statutory duty to assist, but DOJ guidance says custodians may (and prudently should) contact requesters to clarify scope or give cost estimates, may not deny solely because of volume or because they think the request could be narrowed, and must not chill the exercise of rights. Rejection for insufficiency effectively resets the process — the requester submits a narrowed request, restarting the "as soon as practicable" clock.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No duty to create records or compile data into new records/formats (§ 19.35(1)(L); WIREdata). Statute does not prescribe a specific "no records" response, but DOJ guidance and mandamus exposure require informing the requester that no responsive records exist; that statement should be distinguished from a denial. A "no records" response is not a denial requiring exemption citations, but a false or careless one risks mandamus.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Mandatory segregability: § 19.36(6) — if a record contains both disclosable and non-disclosable information, the authority must delete the non-disclosable portions and release the rest; burden is no excuse. Redaction/review labor is NOT chargeable to the requester — Milwaukee Journal Sentinel v. City of Milwaukee, 2012 WI 65 (no fee for time spent deleting/redacting confidential information); § 19.35(3) authorizes only reproduction, photographing, location, and mailing costs. Sole exception: law-enforcement audio/video redaction fees under § 19.35(3)(h) (2023 Act 253), chargeable only for time actually redacting (not review time) with an advance written estimate.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
§ 19.35(4)(b): denial of a WRITTEN request (email qualifies) in whole or part must be in writing with specific reasons — mere legal conclusions are insufficient; if no blanket statutory/common-law exception applies, the custodian must articulate the specific public-policy reasons under the common-law balancing test (presumption of complete public access; denial only in "exceptional cases," § 19.31). Every written denial must inform the requester that the determination is reviewable by mandamus under § 19.37(1) or upon application to the attorney general or district attorney. Oral request denials may be oral unless the requester demands a written statement within 5 business days. No statutory deadline for denial beyond "as soon as practicable"; no Vaughn-type index required, but withheld material must be identified with reasonable specificity (e.g., "social security numbers").

**Administrative appeal:** None — no binding administrative appeal body. § 19.37(1)(b) lets a requester ask the local district attorney or the Attorney General to bring the mandamus action on their behalf (discretionary; rarely exercised). The Wisconsin DOJ Office of Open Government offers informal, non-binding guidance (PROM helpline (608) 267-2220; opengov@widoj.gov). Otherwise judicial only.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
§ 19.35(3)(a): copy fee may not exceed the "actual, necessary and direct cost of reproduction" — no profit, no overhead padding. No statutory cents-per-page: DOJ has suggested roughly $0.15/page is typical and charges above $0.25/page are suspect; DOJ's own June 2025 schedule computes $0.0082/page B&W and $0.0551/page color; City of Madison charges $0.15/page paper, $0.07/page scanning paper-to-digital, $0 for digital-to-digital (email/PDF), media at device cost (e.g., $1.89 flash drive), staff location time $27.50/hr. Location/search fee (§ 19.35(3)(c)): chargeable ONLY if the actual cost of location alone is $50 or more (below $50, nothing may be charged); DOJ rate example $30.19/hr. Mailing/shipping at actual cost (§ 19.35(3)(d)). Records held by a contractor: actual cost including contractor charges (§ 19.35(3)(g), codifying WIREdata). No review/redaction charges (2012 WI 65) except LE audio/video redaction at lowest-paid-capable-employee rate (§ 19.35(3)(h); DOJ example $34.03/hr). No charge for a requester's inspection-then-copy strategy beyond these categories; programming to create new records not required at all (§ 19.35(1)(L)).

**Labor charges:** Search/location staff time IS chargeable but only when the location cost alone reaches the $50 threshold (§ 19.35(3)(c)) — an all-or-nothing trigger, not a deductible: below $50 charge nothing; at/above $50 the full actual location cost may be charged. Review-for-exemptions and redaction time is NEVER chargeable (Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, 2012 WI 65), except actual redaction time (not review time) for law-enforcement audio/video under § 19.35(3)(h).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Prepayment (not partial deposit) may be required whenever the total fee exceeds $5 (§ 19.35(3)(f)) — no percentage cap; cities routinely demand 100% of the estimate before producing. § 19.35(3)(h)7-type advance written estimate is mandatory only for LE audio/video redaction fees (fees forfeited without it); for other fees an advance estimate is best practice, and mail/email requests may be held for prepayment.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary only. § 19.35(3)(e): authority "may" provide copies free or at reduced charge where it determines waiver/reduction "is in the public interest." No statutory indigency waiver, no mandatory media/public-interest waiver, no appeal right from a waiver refusal (other than arguing the overall fee exceeds actual cost). Many cities (e.g., Madison) instead build in de minimis thresholds — Madison waives totals under $0.50.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute is silent beyond authorizing prepayment; where prepayment is properly required under § 19.35(3)(f), the authority may simply withhold the copies until payment, and city practice is to administratively close the request after a stated period of non-payment (a practice, not a statutory rule). Charging fees exceeding actual cost, or using fees to obstruct, is actionable as an "arbitrary and capricious" overcharge exposing the authority to punitive damages and forfeiture (§ 19.37(3)-(4)).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No formal tolling regime because there is no fixed clock. Practical pauses: (1) an insufficient request (no reasonable subject/time limitation) may be rejected, restarting on refiling; (2) a lawful prepayment demand (> $5 total) suspends production until paid; (3) clarification dialogue extends what is "practicable." None of these are statutory tolling provisions; throughout, the authority remains bound by "as soon as practicable and without delay," and strategic stalling is challengeable as constructive denial.

## REMEDIES

Mandamus in circuit court (§ 19.37(1)(a)); de novo review. If the requester "prevails in whole or in substantial part": reasonable attorney fees, damages of not less than $100, and other actual costs (§ 19.37(2)(a)) — BUT Friends of Frame Park v. City of Waukesha, 2022 WI 57, requires a judicially sanctioned change in the parties' legal relationship; voluntary release after suit but before ruling defeats fees (2025-26 SB 194/AB 190, which would have restored causation-based fee recovery, passed the Senate May 2025 but failed Mar. 23, 2026). Punitive damages for arbitrary and capricious denial, delay, or overcharging (§ 19.37(3)); forfeiture up to $1,000 (§ 19.37(4)). Incarcerated/committed requesters: 90-day limit to sue and no automatic $100 minimum (§§ 19.37(1m), (2)(a)). No criminal penalties in §§ 19.31–19.39 (records destruction is separately criminal under § 19.21/946.72).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields must be OPTIONAL (§ 19.35(1)(i)) — collect contact info for delivery only, and allow anonymous submission; never require a purpose field. No commercial-rate module for general records; add a conditional certification module ONLY for police audio/video requests: ask whether requester is an individual with ≤10 A/V requests this year and offer the written non-financial-gain certification (§ 19.35(3)(h)) with a prominent warning of the $10,000 false-certification forfeiture; also detect directly-involved-party status (fee-exempt). Auto-acknowledgment with projected response date is safe and DOJ-recommended (no statutory clock is triggered). Deadline engine: use an internal 10-business-day SLA for simple requests with escalation flags, since delay beyond "practicable" is constructive denial exposing the city to mandamus, fees, punitive damages, and a $1,000 forfeiture. Fee engine: reproduction at actual cost (Madison-style $0.15/page paper, $0.07 scan, $0 digital-to-digital); NEVER auto-add redaction/review labor except the LE A/V module (require advance written estimate or fees are forfeited); location fee line item only when computed location cost alone ≥ $50; prepayment gate only when total > $5 (100% of estimate allowed); optional discretionary public-interest waiver question (§ 19.35(3)(e)) — no entitlement language. Denial templates must include specific reasons and the mandatory § 19.35(4)(b) notice of mandamus/AG/DA review. No-records responses should be distinct from denials. No admin-appeal workflow needed — link to DOJ Office of Open Government helpline instead.
