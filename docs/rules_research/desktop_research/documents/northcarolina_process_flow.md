# North Carolina City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a North Carolina city, under the North Carolina Public Records Law (N.C. Gen. Stat. ch. 132 (sec. 132-1 et seq.)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** NC Public Records Law, G.S. ch. 132. No fixed deadline — copies 'as promptly as possible' (132-6(a)). No purpose/ID/residency rules. Fees capped at actual reproduction cost; extensive requests may draw a special service charge. No admin appeal — court only, with quasi-mandatory attorney fees.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Strongly protective — verified against statute text. G.S. 132-6(b): no person requesting to inspect and examine public records, or to obtain copies, 'shall be required to disclose the purpose or motive for the request.' G.S. 132-1(b) declares public records 'the property of the people,' and access runs to any person — no residency or citizenship requirement, no ID requirement. Anonymous requests are permitted (Charlotte's portal accepts anonymous submissions but recommends a contact method for delivery). Narrow carve-outs: an agency may require a WRITTEN request for copies of computer databases (G.S. 132-6.2(c)), and for GIS data a county or city may require a written no-resale agreement (G.S. 132-10). City portal forms that ask for name/address/email reflect practice, not a statutory condition, and cannot lawfully be used to demand purpose.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No general commercial-purpose regime: purpose may not be demanded (G.S. 132-6(b)), and deadlines and fees do not change for commercial requesters. One record-type exception, verified against G.S. 132-10: for geographical information system (GIS) data, a county or city furnishing copies 'at reasonable cost' may require a written agreement that the copy will not be resold or otherwise used for trade or commercial purposes. Statutory carve-outs from that restriction: (1) publication or broadcast by the news media, real estate trade associations, or Multiple Listing Services; (2) use of the information without resale by a licensed professional in the course of practicing the profession; (3) resale at cost by a real estate trade association or an MLS operated by one. GIS copies are priced at 'reasonable cost' rather than the actual-cost cap of 132-6.2(b). This is a resale restriction on one dataset, not a commercial-branch workflow for ordinary email requests.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty — Chapter 132 is silent on acknowledgment. Acknowledgment is city practice only: portal cities (e.g., Charlotte, Durham) auto-send a confirmation email with a request number and tracking link; Cary's acknowledgment included a time estimate in 2026 testing. An emailed request to a city with no portal may get no formal acknowledgment at all, and nothing in the statute penalizes that.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
No fixed day count — verified against current statute text (ncleg.gov, July 2026). G.S. 132-6(a) requires the custodian to permit inspection 'at reasonable times' and to furnish copies 'as promptly as possible.' There is no statutory extension mechanism because there is no base deadline; timing is a pure reasonableness standard that scales with the size/complexity of the request. What must happen 'promptly' is production itself (or a denial) — the statute does not require an interim determination or estimate, except for computer-database copy requests under G.S. 132-6.2(c), where the agency 'shall respond to all such requests as promptly as possible' and a denial 'shall be accompanied by an explanation of the basis for the denial.' No deemed-denial day count exists; unreasonable delay is treated as a constructive denial that ripens a G.S. 132-9 court action. The statute does not define 'as promptly as possible' and March 2026 Sunshine Week reporting (NC Local/WFAE/WUNC, Mar. 17, 2026) confirms enormous practice variance: Chapel Hill answered a test request in under 30 minutes, Apex in about an hour; a 2024 Charlotte Observer analysis found 1,500+ unfilled Charlotte requests, including one pending since 2020. Some jurisdictions self-impose targets by policy only (Greensboro aims for 5 business days; Guilford County targets 10 business days; Cary estimated ~10 business days); most cities set no timeframe at all.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

No statutory clarification procedure and no codified duty to assist. Custodians must furnish records 'as promptly as possible' but the requester must identify records with enough particularity for the custodian to locate them; there is no clock to toll, so clarification dialogue simply delays production within the reasonableness standard. City practice: Charlotte urges date ranges/keywords/named individuals; Raleigh routinely asks requesters to narrow scope with keywords. UNC School of Government guidance (Coates' Canons) tells agencies they may negotiate scope but may not refuse a request merely because it is large.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Statute does not prescribe a no-records response format. G.S. 132-6.2(e) (verified): nothing requires a public agency 'to respond to a request for a copy of a public record by creating or compiling a record that does not exist,' nor to put records into electronic media not used by the agency; if an agency voluntarily creates or compiles a record it may negotiate a reasonable charge with the requester. Practice: cities close the request with a written 'no responsive records' statement through the portal; nothing requires an affidavit of search.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Clear segregability duty with a requester-friendly cost rule, verified against G.S. 132-6(c): when confidential information is commingled with nonconfidential information, the agency must separate and disclose the nonconfidential portions, and 'the public agency shall bear the cost of such separation.' Redaction/confidentiality-review labor is NOT chargeable — a special service charge cannot be assessed for personnel time spent reviewing requested materials for confidentiality or redacting confidential material (Dunham v. Whitaker, N.C. Super. Ct., No. 20-CVS-6454, Feb. 10, 2022, discussed in Coates' Canons, Nov. 7, 2023 — persuasive trial-level authority).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Thin. Chapter 132 does not generally require a written denial, citation of the exemption, or a denial deadline for ordinary requests (written explanation is best practice, not law). Exception (verified): denial of a computer-database copy request under G.S. 132-6.2(c) 'shall be accompanied by an explanation of the basis for the denial.' Exemptions are construed narrowly and the burden is on the agency to justify withholding (News & Observer Publ'g Co. v. Poole, 330 N.C. 465, 412 S.E.2d 7 (1992)). No Vaughn-index requirement; no general balancing test in the statute for cities' records. Note: G.S. 132-6(d) allows temporary withholding of records about prospective business-recruitment projects, with disclosure required within 25 business days of announcement.

**Administrative appeal:** None — judicial only. North Carolina has no Public Access Counselor, records ombudsman, or AG appeal route for city records. The only non-judicial mechanisms are (1) voluntary State CIO (or designee) mediation of fee disputes (G.S. 132-6.2(b)) and (2) mandatory but post-filing mediation of the lawsuit itself under G.S. 7A-38.3E — the plaintiff must initiate it no later than 30 days from the filing of responsive pleadings; it is jurisdictional per Tillett v. Town of Kill Devil Hills (N.C. Ct. App. Dec. 19, 2017), and the parties may waive it by informing the mediator in writing (7A-38.3E(e)).

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
No statutory per-page rate. G.S. 132-6.2(b) (verified): the fee for an uncertified copy may not exceed 'the actual cost to the public agency of making the copy' — direct, chargeable costs related to reproduction, excluding costs the agency would have incurred had the request not been made (no overhead). Electronic copies sent by email are effectively free under this cap, and requesters 'may elect to obtain them in any and all media in which the public agency is capable of providing them' (G.S. 132-6.2(a)). Typical city practice: roughly $0.05-$0.25 per paper page under local fee schedules (practice, not statute); many cities (e.g., Charlotte, Durham) provide most records at no charge; media cost for CDs/flash drives. Special service charge (G.S. 132-6.2(b), verified): only when the request requires 'extensive use of information technology resources or extensive clerical or supervisory assistance' beyond agency norms; must be reasonable and based on actual costs incurred. GIS copies: 'reasonable cost' under G.S. 132-10. Certified copies: fees as otherwise provided by law. Fee disputes: the requester may ask the State Chief Information Officer or a designee to mediate (G.S. 132-6.2(b)).

**Labor charges:** Search, retrieval, and ordinary clerical time are NOT chargeable — only reproduction cost — unless the request requires 'extensive' IT, clerical, or supervisory assistance under G.S. 132-6.2(b), which has no statutory hour trigger (agencies set policy thresholds, commonly 30 minutes to a few hours). Dunham v. Whitaker (N.C. Super. Ct. 2022) disfavored a special-service-charge policy giving staff 'significant and unfettered discretion,' pointing agencies toward readily ascertainable, objective criteria; it also held redaction and confidentiality-review time is never chargeable (consistent with G.S. 132-6(c)). Programming/IT time to extract data can fall within the special service charge if genuinely extensive. No appellate precedent yet on either point.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
No statutory deposit or prepayment provision and no percentage cap — Chapter 132 is silent. G.S. 132-6(a) conditions furnishing copies on 'payment of any fees as may be prescribed by law,' which cities read as allowing payment before release. Common city practice: give an advance estimate of any special service charge and require agreement (sometimes prepayment or a deposit) before starting work on large requests. Purely local policy.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency fee waiver anywhere in Chapter 132 (verified). Waiver/reduction is entirely discretionary with the agency; many NC cities waive fees below a de minimis threshold as a matter of policy, and Charlotte and Durham fill most requests free. The statutory safety valves are the actual-cost cap itself, the agency-borne cost of separation/redaction (G.S. 132-6(c)), and the right to ask the State CIO or designee to mediate a fee the requester believes unfair (G.S. 132-6.2(b)).

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute is silent. Because copies are furnished 'upon payment' of lawful fees (G.S. 132-6(a)), a city may withhold delivery of copies until payment; city practice is to close or administratively abandon requests when an estimate/invoice goes unpaid after notice. Nonpayment does not extinguish the underlying right — the requester can re-request, seek State CIO fee mediation, or bring a G.S. 132-9 action challenging a fee as exceeding actual cost.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No statutory clock, so no formal tolling of a response deadline. In practice the reasonableness standard absorbs delay for clarification, fee-estimate agreement/prepayment, redaction review, and queue position; nothing requires an agency to respond outside its usual business hours (G.S. 132-6.2(d), verified). Litigation-side only: filing a request for mediation tolls limitation-type time periods until 30 days after mediation concludes (G.S. 7A-38.3E(g)). None is a codified response-clock pause.

## REMEDIES

G.S. 132-9(a) (verified): any person denied access or copies may apply to the appropriate division of the General Court of Justice for an order compelling disclosure; actions 'shall be set down for immediate hearing' with priority for subsequent proceedings, and the court has jurisdiction to issue orders if the person has complied with G.S. 7A-38.3E. Mediation under 7A-38.3E is a jurisdictional prerequisite — initiated within 30 days of responsive pleadings; failure voids the judgment (Tillett, 2017); waivable in writing to the mediator. Attorney fees, G.S. 132-9(c) (verified): the court SHALL allow reasonable fees to a requester who substantially prevails, UNLESS the agency acted in reasonable reliance on (i) a court judgment or order applicable to it, (ii) a published appellate opinion, or (iii) a written opinion, decision, or letter of the Attorney General — so quasi-mandatory. Fees SHALL be assessed against a requester who filed frivolously or in bad faith. Fees may be ordered paid personally by an official who knowingly or intentionally committed, caused, permitted, suborned, or participated in the violation, unless the official sought and followed an attorney's advice (G.S. 132-9(d)); no fee award against Chapter 131E public hospitals in competitor suits brought to gain competitive advantage (132-9(e)). No civil penalties or criminal sanctions for wrongful denial itself; unlawful destruction/disposal of records is separately restricted (G.S. 132-3; ch. 121).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields must be optional (anonymous requests are lawful; recommend but do not require a contact method). NEVER ask purpose — a purpose question violates G.S. 132-6(b); the only purpose-adjacent module needed is a GIS no-resale agreement checkbox when GIS data is requested. No commercial-rate module. No fee-waiver question (no statutory waiver; optionally surface 'request discretionary reduction'). Deadline logic: no statutory timer — configure a policy-based target (e.g., acknowledge immediately, status update at 5-10 business days) and never display a legal due date; do flag aging requests since prolonged silence is constructive denial and litigation exposure (attorney fees are quasi-mandatory for substantially prevailing requesters under 132-9(c)). Auto-acknowledgment is pure practice but common among NC city portals — include request number and tracking. Fee module: default electronic delivery free; per-page rate configurable to actual cost; special-service-charge workflow should require an objective, pre-published threshold (Dunham disfavors unfettered discretion), an advance written estimate, and requester agreement before work, and must exclude redaction/confidentiality-review time from billable hours. Deposit logic: allowed by practice with no statutory cap, but only against special service charges. Litigation-exposure flags: any denial without a stated basis, database-copy denials lacking a written explanation (132-6.2(c)), fee charges that include redaction time, and requests pending unreasonably long.

I did not locate an appellate decision defining the phrase, but absence of case law is hard to prove conclusively. (2) Per-page rates are entirely local; the $0.05-$0.25 range is common practice, not statute — no individual city fee schedule was independently retrieved. (8) Deposit/prepayment and abandonment-on-nonpayment practices vary city to city and rest on the 'upon payment' clause, not express statutory authority.
