# Indiana City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Indiana city, under the Access to Public Records Act (APRA) (Ind. Code § 5-14-3-1 et seq.). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** Indiana APRA: emailed/portal request to a city is DEEMED DENIED if no response in 7 days (IC 5-14-3-9(c)); only a response is due - production in 'reasonable time.' 10c/page BW, 25c color; email delivery free; no labor fees. PAC complaint within 30 days. HEA 1360 eff. 7/1/2026.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
No residency requirement to make a request, and traditionally no identity proof needed, but the agency may require the request be in writing or on the agency's form (IC 5-14-3-3(a)). PURPOSE: a request may not be denied because the requester refuses to state the purpose, "unless such condition is required by other applicable statute" (IC 5-14-3-3(a)) — e.g., restricted data/lists under 3(e)-(f). Since July 1, 2026 (HEA 1360, new IC 5-14-3-3.3): agencies MAY establish electronic request portals imposing CAPTCHA/human verification, physical-address verification, and an Indiana-residency indication, and may decline requests suspected of data scraping or phishing or that would jeopardize the security of agency electronic systems (with PAC notification within 7 days of declining). Non-residents/out-of-state entities may now face supplemental fees (IC 5-14-3-8(n), waivable under 8(o)) and lower priority/delay (IC 5-14-3-8.1). Practical effect: anonymity remains possible for a plain email request, but address/residency fields are now statutorily sanctioned and financially meaningful.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No across-the-board commercial deadline or general commercial copy rate, but several purpose-based provisions: (1) IC 5-14-3-3(e): rules may restrict resale/commercial use of electronically provided data (uses for news publication, nonprofit activities, and academic research exempt); (2) IC 5-14-3-3(f): lists of public employees, state-educational-institution attendees, and public-school students may not be disclosed for commercial or political purposes; (3) IC 5-14-3-8(j)-(k): electronic map (GIS) fees may be a uniform "reasonable percentage of the agency's direct cost" (exceeding copy caps) and are waivable under 8(k) only for noncommercial purposes (public agency program support, nonprofit activities, journalism, academic research) — PAC upheld a uniform $750 commercial bulk-GIS fee (25-FC-145, Mar. 9, 2026); (4) eff. 7/1/2026, IC 5-14-3-8.1 lets agencies PRIORITIZE Indiana residents and "civic, journalistic, academic, or personal use" requests — commercial requests may lawfully be deprioritized; out-of-state requests may be delayed (8.1(b)) and charged supplemental fees reasonably related to fulfillment cost, capped at $0.25/page and $25/hour of staff time (IC 5-14-3-8(n)), waivable if the request "serves the public interest" (8(o)). Purpose still cannot be compelled as a condition of a routine copy request.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory "acknowledgment" duty; the 7-day response obligation of IC 5-14-3-9(c) functions as one — any timely response (even "we received it and are working on it") avoids deemed denial. City practice (e.g., Bloomington's NextRequest portal) is an automated acknowledgment followed by a determination notice. Timing: within 7 days of receipt for emailed/mailed/faxed and (since 7/1/2026) portal-submitted requests; 24 hours for in-person/phone (out of scope here). Separately, an agency declining a request as suspected data scraping/phishing under new IC 5-14-3-3.3 must notify the PAC of the declination and reason within 7 days.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Two-track deemed-denial system, not a production deadline. In-person/telephone requests: denial occurs on oral refusal or when 24 hours elapse (IC 5-14-3-9(b); 9(a) merely excludes IC 5-14-3-4.4 information requests from the section). Requests received by mail or facsimile — and, effective 7/1/2026 (HEA 1360, SECTION 7), requests submitted through an electronic portal under new IC 5-14-3-3.3: if the agency does not respond within 7 days of receipt, the request is DEEMED DENIED (IC 5-14-3-9(c)). Plain EMAIL requests are not named in 9(c)'s text, but the Public Access Counselor (PAC) consistently applies the 7-day constructive-denial rule to them (e.g., PAC Op. 25-FC-127, Feb. 18, 2026). The 7-day duty requires only a RESPONSE (acknowledgment/determination) — not production and not a fee estimate. Actual production is due within a "reasonable time" based on facts and circumstances (IC 5-14-3-3(b); PAC opinions); there is no fixed production day-count and no statutory extension mechanism. New eff. 7/1/2026 (HEA 1360): out-of-state or automated-system requests may be "delayed as necessary to prevent disruption of core agency functions" (IC 5-14-3-8.1(b)), and agencies may prioritize Indiana residents and civic/journalistic/academic/personal-use requests (IC 5-14-3-8.1(a)).

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

Requests must "identify with reasonable particularity the record being requested" (IC 5-14-3-3(a)(1)). PAC opinions (e.g., 25-FC-127) hold the requester must give enough information to let the agency search for, locate, and retrieve the records; the agency need not guess what is sought or create records — e.g., "all contracts and agreements" spanning multiple years without more failed particularity and was properly denied. No express statutory duty to assist, but PAC guidance urges agencies to seek clarification and work with requesters. Clarification has no formal tolling effect — the 7-day response clock still requires a timely reply (which may itself be the clarification request); the "reasonable time" for production flexes accordingly.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** APRA requires access only to "records already created" — no duty to create, compile, or answer interrogatories (PAC Op. 25-FC-127 and long-standing PAC doctrine). If nothing responsive exists, the proper response is a timely statement that no responsive records exist; that is not a denial requiring exemption citation, though silence still ripens into deemed denial after 7 days.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Mandatory segregability: if a record contains disclosable and nondisclosable information, the agency "shall... separate the material that may be disclosed and make it available" (IC 5-14-3-6(a)), including computer/microfilm records (6(b)). Redacted productions should identify the exemption applied. Redaction LABOR IS NOT CHARGEABLE for a city — "actual cost" of copying excludes labor and overhead (IC 5-14-3-8(d)), and no fee may be charged to search/examine/review a record to determine disclosability (8(b)); the only separation charge allowed is the agency's direct cost of reprogramming a computer system when reprogramming is necessary to separate disclosable from nondisclosable data (IC 5-14-3-6(c); no reprogramming duty for enhanced access, 6(d)).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
A denial of a written (mail/fax/email/portal) request must be in WRITING and must include: (1) a statement of the specific exemption or exemptions authorizing the withholding, and (2) the name and the title or position of the person responsible for the denial (IC 5-14-3-9(d)). No deadline beyond the 7-day response rule; no balancing test for most exemptions (IC 5-14-3-4(a) mandatory vs. 4(b) discretionary); no Vaughn-index requirement, but on suit the court determines the matter de novo with the burden on the agency and may review in camera (IC 5-14-3-9(f), (h)). New grounds eff. 7/1/2026: request by a litigation party that duplicates a discovery demand in that litigation (IC 5-14-3-4(e)); suspected data-scraping/phishing submissions or requests that would jeopardize agency electronic-system security (IC 5-14-3-3.3, with PAC notification within 7 days of declining).

**Administrative appeal:** Indiana Public Access Counselor (PAC), IC 5-14-4 and 5-14-5. Formal complaint must be filed within 30 DAYS after the denial or deemed denial (IC 5-14-5-7; deemed filed when received, or on postmark if received more than 30 days after denial); the PAC must issue an advisory opinion within 30 days of filing (IC 5-14-5-9, "except as provided in section 10"). Opinions are ADVISORY, not binding — but seeking a PAC informal-inquiry response or advisory opinion first is effectively mandatory: it is a statutory prerequisite to recovering attorney's fees in court (IC 5-14-3-9(i), narrow exceptions) and to the civil-penalty pathway (IC 5-14-3-9.5 requires denial contrary to a PAC advisory opinion). Informal inquiries are also available without the 30-day formality.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
For a city (non-state agency), copies are charged under a locally adopted uniform fee schedule capped at the greater of $0.10/page (non-color) or $0.25/page (color), or the actual copying cost — paper plus per-page equipment cost, EXCLUDING labor and overhead (IC 5-14-3-8(d)). Inspection is free (8(b)). No fee may be charged to provide an electronic copy by ELECTRONIC MAIL (8(b), with narrow exceptions such as enhanced-access and electronic-map fees). Certification capped at $5/document (8(d)). No search/review/labor fee for cities — the electronic-search fee (first 5 hours free, then the LESSER of $20/hour or the searcher's hourly rate) applies only to school corporations and charter schools (8(b), 8(m)). Computer reprogramming to segregate data: direct cost (6(c)). Enhanced access: contract/uniform fees (8(f)-(h)); electronic maps/GIS: uniform fee at a reasonable percentage of direct cost, may exceed copy caps (8(j)), waivable for noncommercial use (8(k)); law-enforcement recording copies capped at $150 (8(l)). New eff. 7/1/2026: supplemental fee on out-of-state/non-resident requesters, reasonably related to fulfillment cost, capped at $0.25/page plus $25/hour of staff time (IC 5-14-3-8(n), HEA 1360), waivable if the request serves the public interest (8(o)).

**Labor charges:** Not chargeable for a city request for copies: search, review, and redaction time cannot be billed — "actual cost" expressly excludes labor and overhead (IC 5-14-3-8(d)), and no fee may be charged for inspecting or for searching/examining/reviewing a record to determine disclosability (8(b)). Exceptions: direct cost of computer reprogramming needed to segregate data (6(c)); GIS/enhanced-access fees; school-corporation electronic-search fee (lesser of $20/hr or searcher's wage after 5 free hours — inapplicable to cities, 8(m)); and, since 7/1/2026, the up-to-$25/hour supplemental staff-time fee for OUT-OF-STATE requesters only (8(n)).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
"A public agency may require that the payment for search and copying costs be made in advance" (IC 5-14-3-8(e)) — full prepayment may be demanded; there is no percentage cap and no estimate-dollar threshold in the statute. Common practice: agencies quote a fee after review and require payment before release (state Treasurer's APRA guide describes advance payment by cashier's check/money order; small emailed batches are free by statute).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No general statutory public-interest or indigency waiver — waivers are discretionary. Two narrow statutory waivers: electronic map/GIS fees may be waived for noncommercial purposes — public agency program support, nonprofit activities, journalism, or academic research (IC 5-14-3-8(k)); and the new out-of-state supplemental fee may be waived if the request "serves the public interest" (IC 5-14-3-8(o), eff. 7/1/2026). Intentionally charging a copying fee known to exceed the amount set by statute, fee schedule, ordinance, or court order can trigger civil penalties (IC 5-14-3-9.5).

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Because advance payment may be required (8(e)), a city may simply withhold copies until paid; no statutory abandonment/closure procedure or deadline exists. Common city practice is to close the request after a stated period of non-payment. The statute does not expressly authorize conditioning new copies on payment of a requester's PRIOR unpaid balances, though some agencies do so by policy.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No statutory tolling. The 7-day response clock (IC 5-14-3-9(c)) runs from receipt and is not paused by clarification requests, fee estimates, or deposits — but only a response, not production, is due within it. Production runs on the elastic "reasonable time" standard (3(b)), which in practice absorbs clarification exchanges, volume, and redaction needs; the PAC evaluates delay case-by-case. Since 7/1/2026, out-of-state and automated-system requests may additionally be "delayed as necessary to prevent disruption of core agency functions" (IC 5-14-3-8.1(b)).

## REMEDIES

Action to compel disclosure in the circuit or superior court of the county where the denial occurred (IC 5-14-3-9(e)); deemed denial ripens the claim. The court determines the matter de novo with the burden on the agency to sustain the denial (9(f)) and may review the record in camera (9(h)). Attorney's fees: awarded to a plaintiff who SUBSTANTIALLY PREVAILS, but the plaintiff is ineligible unless they first sought and received a PAC informal-inquiry response or advisory opinion — except where filing first was necessary because the denial would prevent presenting the record to a public agency preparing to act on a related matter (9(i)); a separate exception exists for law-enforcement-recording appeals (9(k), § 5.1); a substantially prevailing defendant recovers fees if the action was frivolous or vexatious (9(i)). Civil penalties (IC 5-14-3-9.5), sought by the denied requester in the § 9 action: an officer or management-level employee who continues to deny a compliant request CONTRARY to a PAC advisory opinion with specific intent to unlawfully withhold, or who intentionally charges a copying fee known to exceed the lawful amount — up to $100 first violation, up to $500 each additional; defense of reliance on an opinion of agency counsel or the Attorney General; penalties are deposited in the state education fund. No criminal penalty for wrongful denial (criminal/disciplinary exposure under APRA runs to wrongful DISCLOSURE of confidential records, IC 5-14-3-10 pre-2026 numbering).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Auto-acknowledgment is the single highest-value feature: an emailed or portal request is deemed denied if the city stays silent 7 days (IC 5-14-3-9(c), portal requests textually covered eff. 7/1/2026), which starts the requester's 30-day PAC complaint window and builds attorney-fee exposure — every intake should generate a same-day response. Identity/address/residency fields are now statutorily sanctioned for portals (IC 5-14-3-3.3, eff. 7/1/2026) and residency matters financially (out-of-state supplemental fee up to $0.25/page + $25/hr under 8(n), waivable under 8(o), plus permissible delay/deprioritization under 8.1) — include a residency attestation and CAPTCHA. A PURPOSE question must be optional (denial for refusing to state purpose is illegal, 3(a)), but an optional use-category picker (civic/journalistic/academic/personal/commercial) supports 8.1 prioritization, GIS commercial pricing (8(j)), the 8(k) noncommercial GIS waiver, and the 8(o) public-interest waiver. Fee module: $0.10 non-color / $0.25 color per page from the city's adopted schedule; $0 line item for records delivered by email; no labor charges for search/review/redaction; $5 cap on certification; advance-payment (full prepay) gate before release, with no statutory percentage cap. Fee-waiver question: discretionary only — do not promise statutory waivers except GIS-noncommercial (8(k)) and out-of-state public-interest (8(o)). If declining a request as suspected scraping/phishing, the portal must trigger PAC notification within 7 days (3.3) and suspicious-request reporting (new § 11). Litigation-exposure flags: missed 7-day response; denial letters missing exemption citation or responsible person's name/title (9(d)); charging above caps (intentional overcharge triggers § 9.5 penalties); continuing denial after an adverse PAC opinion ($100/$500 penalties). Also build a clarification workflow for particularity failures and a no-records-certification response.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) EMAIL and the 7-day rule: IC 5-14-3-9(c) as amended by HEA 1360 covers mail, facsimile, and electronic-portal submissions; plain electronic mail is still not named in the text (verified against the enrolled act) — application to email rests on consistent PAC constructive-denial doctrine (e.g., 25-FC-127) and universal practice. (2) HEA 1360 content was verified from the enrolled-act PDF (HB1360.05.ENRS) and secondary reports confirming a July 1, 2026 effective date; the Public Law number and the governor's signature date were not independently confirmed, and the official codified text on iga.in.gov (JavaScript wall) was not fetched — subsection lettering of 8(n)/(o) and 3-12 comes from the enrolled act. (3) One legislative-committee summary described portals as required, but the enrolled act reads as permissive ("may establish") — permissive reading adopted here. (4) Postage/mailing-cost chargeability as an "actual cost" is common practice but was not verified in statutory text. (5) The Evansville Courier & Press reporter citation (17 N.E.3d 922) was confirmed to exist as a 2014 Indiana Supreme Court decision via Indiana Court Times case clips, but the N.E.3d page number was not independently re-verified. (6) IC 5-14-5-10 exceptions to the PAC's 30-day opinion deadline were not reviewed in full. (7) Whether an agency may condition new copies on payment of past unpaid balances is policy-based, not statutory. (8) City fee practices vary widely (e.g., Bloomington's portal publishes no fee schedule); the Treasurer's-guide practices are state-agency practice, not law. (9) The pre-2026 confidentiality-disclosure discipline provision cited as IC 5-14-3-10 may have been renumbered by HEA 1360's additions; not re-verified post-amendment.
