# Georgia City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Georgia city, under the Georgia Open Records Act (GORA) (O.C.G.A. § 50-18-70 through § 50-18-77 (Art. 4, Ch. 18, Title 50)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** GA: produce within 3 business days or send description + timeline / estimate / cited denial. 10c per page; labor at lowest-paid capable employee rate, first 15 min free. Estimate notice over $25; full prepayment allowed over $500. No admin appeal — superior court, near-mandatory fee-shifting.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
No purpose statement required and no residency/citizenship requirement — VERIFIED via RCFP Georgia guide: "any person, firm, corporation or other entity may request records"; "no person requesting inspection need have, assert or prove any special personal interest"; agencies cannot condition access on identity or purpose. Anonymous or pseudonymous email requests are accepted in practice, though contact info is practically needed for estimates and delivery. Post-2012, the access right in § 50-18-71(a) is not limited to Georgia citizens (older "citizen of this state" phrasing persists only in secondary summaries such as NFOIC). Narrow exception (mechanics corrected this pass): booking photographs of arrestees may be released only in accordance with O.C.G.A. § 35-1-19 (cross-referenced by § 50-18-72(a)(4)); § 35-1-19 requires the requester to submit a statement affirming that the use complies with its subsection (c) — i.e., the photo will not be posted to a website/publication that charges a fee or other consideration for removal — and a knowingly false statement is punishable under O.C.G.A. § 16-10-20. This is Georgia's one purpose-affirmation requirement.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None — VERIFIED. Georgia repealed its commercial-solicitation denial provision in 1993 (RCFP: the General Assembly removed the prior authority to deny requests made for commercial-solicitation purposes). The Act imposes no commercial-purpose disclosure duty, no different deadline, no different fee schedule for commercial requesters, and generally no restrictions on subsequent use or resale of records obtained (RCFP Georgia guide). The only purpose-linked carve-out is the booking-photograph affirmation under § 35-1-19 (via § 50-18-72(a)(4)) — an anti-mugshot-extortion rule that does not change deadlines or fees for ordinary requests.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory acknowledgment duty (verified — nothing in § 50-18-71 requires an acknowledgment distinct from the substantive 3-business-day response). The 3-business-day obligation itself is the mandatory first contact: produce, or describe records + give a timeline, or deny with statutory citations, or send a >$25 cost estimate. City PRACTICE: portal-based cities (e.g., Roswell via JustFOIA; Atlanta and many others via GovQA/NextRequest) send automated acknowledgment emails with a tracking number immediately on submission — practice only, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
VERIFIED against 2024 Code text: O.C.G.A. § 50-18-71(b)(1)(A) — agency must "produce for inspection all records responsive to a request within a reasonable amount of time not to exceed three business days of receipt of a request." This is a PRODUCTION deadline, not merely a determination deadline. If responsive records exist but cannot be produced within 3 business days, the agency must, within those 3 days, provide the requester a description of the records and a timeline for when they will be available, and must produce records "as soon as practicable" (records available within the 3 days must be produced then). If costs will exceed $25.00, the cost-estimate notice must go out within the same 3-business-day window (§ 50-18-71(d)). Denials citing exemptions must come within 3 business days — or, for records not initially available, within 3 business days of retrieval (§ 50-18-71(d)). No formal extension mechanism beyond the agency's self-set (reasonable) timeline, and no express "deemed denied" clause — but silence past 3 business days is treated as a violation/constructive denial supporting suit under § 50-18-73. CLOCK-START RULE (verified): if the agency has designated individuals to receive written requests (director/chairperson, senior satellite-office official, records clerk, or open records officer — as cities commonly do, and as SB 12 of 2025 reinforces via the new "custodian" definition and routing rule in § 50-18-71(a)), "the three-day period for response to a written request shall not begin to run until the request is made in writing upon such individuals" (§ 50-18-71(b)(1)(B)-(C)) — an email to a random employee may not start the clock. Agencies that use email or fax in the ordinary course must accept requests by those means (§ 50-18-71(b)). Only WRITTEN requests are enforceable via §§ 50-18-73/50-18-74; oral requests are permitted but carry no enforceable clock — email counts as written.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

No express statutory duty to assist or to seek clarification, and no statutory tolling for clarification. The Act obligates production of "all records responsive to a request" that exist at the time of the request; agencies need not answer questions, compile, or create new records (though they must run queries/exports using existing computer programs, § 50-18-71(f)). PRACTICE: cities (per Georgia Municipal Association guidance) contact the requester to narrow overbroad requests and use the >$25 cost-estimate notice under § 50-18-71(d) as the practical narrowing lever — the agency may defer search and retrieval until the requester agrees to pay the estimated costs. The safest statutory response to a vague request within 3 business days is a written description + timeline or a cost estimate; document requester-caused delay.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No dedicated no-records subsection. The 3-business-day duty still applies: standard practice and AG guidance call for a written response stating that no responsive records exist. Post-SB 12 (2025), a custodian "shall have no obligation to retrieve public records held by another agency or any other public entity" (§ 50-18-71(a) as amended by 2025 Ga. Laws Act 276) — but the custodian MUST retrieve records prepared/maintained/received by a private person or entity performing a service or function for the agency, or transferred to a private entity for storage or future governmental use. No duty to create a record to satisfy a request. Silence invites a § 50-18-73 enforcement action, so a written "no records" determination within 3 business days is the compliant close-out.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Segregability is mandatory — VERIFIED, § 50-18-72(b): "This Code section shall be interpreted narrowly so as to exclude from disclosure only that portion of a public record to which an exclusion is directly applicable. It shall be the duty of the agency having custody of a record to provide all other portions of a record for public inspection or copying." Redaction labor IS chargeable — § 50-18-71(c)(1) permits charges for search, retrieval, and redaction capped at the prorated hourly salary of the lowest-paid full-time employee who, in the custodian's reasonable discretion, has the necessary skill and training, with no charge for the first quarter hour. Agencies may not refuse electronic production on the ground that redaction/export requires entering range, search, filter, or report parameters executable with programs the agency already uses (§ 50-18-71(f)).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
§ 50-18-71(d) (verified): when withholding all or part of a record, the agency must notify the requester of the specific legal authority exempting the record "by Code section, subsection, and paragraph," within 3 business days of the request — or, for records not initially available, within 3 business days of retrieval. In writing where the request was written. No Vaughn-style index required. Exemptions are interpreted narrowly and only the directly applicable portion may be withheld (§ 50-18-72(b); Deal v. Coleman, 294 Ga. 170 (2013): Act construed broadly in favor of disclosure). No general balancing test — records are open unless a specific exemption or court order applies. Blanket unexplained denials and missing citations are classic fee-shifting triggers under § 50-18-73(b).

**Administrative appeal:** None — judicial only. Georgia has no records ombudsman and no binding administrative appeal. The Attorney General's Open Government Mediation Program (verified on law.georgia.gov) is a free, informal, voluntary service "designed to assist members of the public whose local governments may not be complying" with the Open Meetings or Open Records Acts — LOCAL governments (cities/counties) only, not state agencies (RCFP); no deadlines, no tolling, non-binding; complaints filed via the AG's online complaint form (submissions themselves become open records). The AG also has discretionary authority to bring civil or criminal enforcement actions (§ 50-18-73(a), § 50-18-74) but is not an appeal body.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
VERIFIED against § 50-18-71(c), (d), (f): (1) Labor (search, retrieval, redaction): "shall not exceed the prorated hourly salary of the lowest paid full-time employee who, in the reasonable discretion of the custodian of the records, has the necessary skill and training to perform the request; provided, however, that no charge shall be made for the first quarter hour" (§ 50-18-71(c)(1)). (2) Copies: "not to exceed 10¢ per page for letter or legal size documents or, in the case of other documents, the actual cost of producing the copy" (§ 50-18-71(c)(2)). (3) Electronic records: no per-page charge — actual cost of the media plus (c)(1) labor; emailed PDFs have effectively zero copy cost. (4) § 50-18-71(f): agencies must produce electronic copies (or printouts at requester's preference) using existing computer programs; requester may choose the format in which data is kept or a standard export format such as flat-file ASCII if the agency's programs support it; no separate statutory programming fee. (5) § 50-18-71(d): estimate notice within 3 business days whenever costs will exceed $25.00; agency may defer search/retrieval until the requester agrees to pay; if estimated costs exceed $500.00 the agency may require full prepayment before beginning search, retrieval, review, or production. Higher fees only where another statute specifically authorizes them.

**Labor charges:** Yes — search, retrieval, and redaction time are chargeable, but only at the prorated hourly salary (no benefits loading stated in statute) of the lowest-paid full-time employee capable of the task, and the first quarter hour (15 minutes) is free (§ 50-18-71(c)(1), verified). No statutory dollar cap on the hourly rate and no cap on total hours; the >$25 estimate-notice and >$500 prepayment rules of § 50-18-71(d) are the practical checks.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
VERIFIED, § 50-18-71(d): if estimated costs exceed $25.00, the agency must notify the requester within 3 business days and MAY defer search and retrieval until the requester agrees to pay the estimated costs; if estimated costs exceed $500.00, the agency MAY require full prepayment before beginning search, retrieval, review, or production — there is no percentage cap (100% prepayment allowed, unlike Michigan's 50% deposit rule). Additionally, if a requester left fees unpaid on a past request, the agency may require prepayment on all future requests from that requester "until the costs for the prior production of records have been paid or the dispute regarding payment resolved."

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency waiver — VERIFIED. RCFP: "The Act neither requires nor prohibits waiver of search or copying fees." Waiver (including the common practice of not billing small amounts) is purely discretionary with the agency/city; some cities waive fees for small requests or for other governments as a matter of local policy only.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute: unpaid fees from a prior request authorize the agency to demand prepayment for all future requests from that requester until the prior costs are paid or the payment dispute is resolved (§ 50-18-71(d), verified). For the pending request, the agency may lawfully defer search/production while awaiting agreement to pay (>$25) or prepayment (>$500). PRACTICE: cities administratively close requests when the requester does not respond to an estimate or pay within a stated window (often 30 days) — closure windows are city policy, not statute. Fees are collectible like other municipal charges.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No express tolling provision. Functional pauses (all verified in statute text): (1) the 3-day clock never starts until a written request reaches the agency's designated individuals/open records officer where the agency has so required (§ 50-18-71(b)(1)(B)-(C)), a routing rule SB 12 (2025) reinforces by directing all requests to the "custodian"; (2) after a timely >$25 estimate, the agency may defer search/retrieval until the requester agrees to pay (§ 50-18-71(d)); (3) >$500 estimates: work may be deferred until full prepayment (§ 50-18-71(d)); (4) records not producible in 3 days run on the agency's stated (reasonable) timeline rather than a fixed statutory count (§ 50-18-71(b)(1)(A)). Clarification requests do NOT formally toll anything — statutory silence, practice varies.

## REMEDIES

§ 50-18-73(a) (verified): any person, firm, corporation, or other entity may sue in superior court (jurisdiction in law and equity — injunction/mandamus to compel production); the AG may also bring enforcement actions and seek civil or criminal penalties. Attorney fees, § 50-18-73(b) (verified): if either party "acted without substantial justification" in noncompliance (or in instituting the litigation), the court "shall, unless it finds that special circumstances exist, assess in favor of the complaining party reasonable attorney's fees" and litigation costs — effectively mandatory fee-shifting against unjustified denials (2012 standard). Good-faith defense, § 50-18-73(c) (verified): no liability for providing access in good-faith reliance on the chapter. Penalties, § 50-18-74 (verified): knowing and willful violation — including frustrating or attempting to frustrate access — is a misdemeanor, fine up to $1,000.00 for a first violation and up to $2,500.00 per additional violation within 12 months; parallel civil penalties for NEGLIGENT violations up to $1,000.00 (first) and $2,500.00 per additional violation within 12 months, imposable "in any civil action brought pursuant to this article." Private plaintiffs as well as the AG may seek civil penalties (Cardinale v. Keane, 362 Ga. App. 644, 869 S.E.2d 613 (2022), cited by RCFP for exactly this proposition under the Open Records Act); individual employees can be criminally prosecuted (Garland v. State, Ga. Ct. App. Nov. 1, 2021 (A21A1059), reported at 361 Ga. App. 724 — Atlanta mayoral press secretary's misdemeanor convictions for directing that water-billing records requests be dragged out were affirmed).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

(1) Routing is everything: build the intake so email/portal requests land with the designated open records officer/custodian immediately — the 3-business-day clock only starts on receipt by the designated individuals (§ 50-18-71(b)(1)(C)), and SB 12 (2025) formalizes custodian routing; log the receipt timestamp. Email requests are 'written' and fully enforceable. (2) Auto-acknowledgment with tracking number is standard city practice (JustFOIA/GovQA) though not statutory — include it. (3) No identity/purpose fields needed (keep optional); NO commercial-rate module (Georgia has none); add the § 35-1-19 affirmation checkbox only for booking-photo requests. (4) Fee engine: 10c/page paper (letter/legal), $0 copy charge for emailed electronic records plus media cost if physical, labor at lowest-paid-capable-employee prorated hourly salary with automatic first-15-minutes deduction; hard trigger at $25 (send estimate within 3 business days, may pause fulfillment until requester agrees to pay) and at $500 (may require 100% prepayment). (5) Fee-waiver question: omit or mark discretionary — no statutory waiver. (6) Non-payment flag: auto-demand prepayment on new requests from requesters with unpaid balances (§ 50-18-71(d)). (7) Litigation-exposure flags: any request older than 3 business days with no production, no timeline letter, no estimate, and no exemption-cited denial = near-mandatory attorney-fee exposure under § 50-18-73(b) plus possible $1,000/$2,500 civil or criminal penalties and individual misdemeanor risk (Garland) — surface these prominently. (8) Denial templates must force entry of Code section + subsection + paragraph. (9) Timeline letters: require staff to state a specific availability date when production exceeds 3 days. (10) Contractor-held records (post-SB 12): the custodian must retrieve them from vendors performing city functions or storing city records — build a vendor-referral workflow, but records held by OTHER agencies can be declined with a referral.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) RESOLVED this pass: no residency/citizenship requirement — RCFP confirms any person/firm/corporation/entity may request and identity/purpose are irrelevant; the 'state citizen' phrasing appears only in outdated secondary summaries.
