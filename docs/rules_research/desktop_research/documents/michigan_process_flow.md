# Michigan City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Michigan city, under the Michigan Freedom of Information Act (MCL 15.231–15.246 (1976 PA 442, as amended through 2020 PA 36 and 2020 PA 38)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** MI FOIA, MCL 15.231-.246: 5 business days to a determination (email deemed received +1 day); one 10-business-day extension; fees strictly capped (10c/page; labor only if 'unreasonably high costs'); late response auto-cuts labor fees 5%/day up to 50%; no state appeal body.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Purpose is irrelevant and may not be demanded; use of records is unrestricted. MCL 15.233(1) grants 'a person' the right to inspect, copy, or receive copies upon a written request that describes the record sufficiently. No residency requirement — any person, in-state or out-of-state, may request. One major exclusion: 'person' under MCL 15.232(g) excludes an individual serving a sentence of imprisonment in a state, county, or federal correctional facility, in Michigan or any other state (prisoner exclusion). The statute does not require a stated purpose or proof of identity; practically, a name/contact point is needed for the written response, and city forms request name and address (practice, not a statutory condition). Effective anonymity via a mail drop or dedicated email is generally possible.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None. Michigan FOIA draws no distinction for commercial-purpose requesters: same 5-day deadline, same capped fees, no required purpose disclosure, no resale restriction in the FOIA itself (separate statutes may restrict use of specific record types, e.g., driver data). Confirmed against the statute text and RCFP's Michigan guide.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty. The 5-business-day determination under MCL 15.235(2) is the only required response; there is no separate receipt/acknowledgment obligation. Common city practice: portal or email auto-acknowledgment (e.g., Ann Arbor asks for 'FOIA' in the email subject line and confirms receipt), but that is practice only. Note the statutory deemed-receipt rule: an emailed request is deemed received 1 business day after it is sent (MCL 15.235(1)).

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
MCL 15.235(2): unless otherwise agreed in writing by the requester, within 5 business days after receipt the public body must respond by (a) granting, (b) issuing a written notice denying, (c) granting in part and denying in part in writing, or (d) issuing a written notice extending the deadline by not more than 10 business days (one extension only per request; notice must state the reasons and the date by which the body will act). Critically for email requests, MCL 15.235(1): a request made by fax, email, or other electronic transmission is not received until 1 business day after the electronic transmission is made (and an email delivered to a spam/junk folder is not received until 1 day after the public body first becomes aware of it). The deadline requires a determination, not production: a grant is typically accompanied by the records or by a detailed fee itemization/deposit demand, with production after payment; when a deposit is required the body must give a nonbinding best-efforts estimate of when records will be provided (MCL 15.234(8)). Failure to respond within the period constitutes the body's final determination to deny (constructive denial) if the failure was willful and intentional or the request conspicuously identified itself as a records request (e.g., the words 'freedom of information,' 'FOIA,' or 'copy,' or a recognizable misspelling, in the email subject line or early in the body) (MCL 15.235(3)); an untimely response also triggers mandatory labor-fee reductions of 5% per day up to 50% on the same conditions (MCL 15.234(9)). Verified against the official compiled text (legislature.mi.gov, retrieved July 2026): last amendments to 15.235 and 15.234 remain 2020 PA 36 and 2020 PA 38 (both imd. eff. Mar. 3, 2020). The 2025 SB 1/2 package (extending FOIA to the Governor and Legislature) passed the Senate in January 2025 but House leadership declared it dead; no enactment found through July 2026.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

MCL 15.233(1): the request must 'describe a public record sufficiently to enable the public body to find the public record.' Case law requires only enough description that the body can locate the records. There is no express statutory duty to assist requesters and no statutory clarification/tolling mechanism; a body faced with an insufficient description properly denies (with the required written explanation) or, in near-universal city practice, contacts the requester to clarify within the 5-day window (Ann Arbor lists 'request clarification' among its response options — practice layered on the statute). FOIA imposes no duty to answer questions, make a compilation, summary, or report, or create new records (MCL 15.233(4)-(5)).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** If the record does not exist, the written response must include a certificate that the public record does not exist under the name given by the requester or by another name reasonably known to the public body (MCL 15.235(5)(b)). No duty to create a record or make a compilation, summary, or report of information (MCL 15.233(4)-(5)). A nonexistence certificate functions as a denial appealable under MCL 15.240.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
MCL 15.244(1): if a record contains both exempt and nonexempt material, the body shall separate the exempt material and make the nonexempt material available; it must generally describe what was separated unless that description would reveal the exempt content and defeat the exemption (15.244(2)). Redaction labor IS chargeable but only conditionally: under MCL 15.234(3), labor for separating/redacting (like search labor under (1)(a)) may be charged only if failure to charge would result in 'unreasonably high costs' to the public body because of the nature of the request in the particular instance, and the body must specifically identify the nature of those unreasonably high costs. Contracted redaction labor is allowed only if the body does not employ a person capable of the separation, capped at 6 times the state minimum hourly wage (15.234(1)(b)); no charge if an already-redacted version exists.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing within the 5-day (or extended) period and must contain (MCL 15.235(5)(a)-(e)): an explanation of the basis for any claimed exemption (particularized, not conclusory — Evening News Ass'n v City of Troy); or a certificate of nonexistence; a description of any separated/deleted material (per MCL 15.244); a full explanation of the requester's right to appeal to the head of the public body; and notice of the right to judicial review (180 days) with attorney fees and damages. Failure to respond is a deemed denial per 15.235(3). Exemptions (MCL 15.243) are discretionary and narrowly construed; some (e.g., privacy, law-enforcement public-interest balancing) embed balancing tests. No Vaughn-style index is required by statute, but courts demand particularized justification.

**Administrative appeal:** Optional internal appeal only — no state oversight body (no AG or ombuds jurisdiction over city denials). Denial appeal: written appeal to the head of the public body (for a city, the governing body or its designee — Ann Arbor designates the City Administrator; East Grand Rapids the Mayor) that specifically states the word 'appeal' and identifies why the denial should be reversed; the head must, within 10 business days, reverse, uphold in writing, reverse in part and uphold in part in writing, or issue one extension of up to 10 business days (MCL 15.240(1)(a), (2)). A board or commission that is the head of a public body is not considered to have RECEIVED the appeal until its first regular meeting after submission (15.240(3)). Fee appeal: available ONLY IF the public body provides for fee appeals in its publicly available procedures and guidelines (MCL 15.240a(1)(a)); same mechanism — written appeal stating 'appeal' and how the fee exceeds the permitted amount; 10 business days to waive, reduce with written basis and certification of statutory compliance, uphold with written basis and certification, or one 10-business-day extension (15.240a(2)). Internal appeal is NOT a prerequisite to suit — the requester may go straight to circuit court.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
MCL 15.234(1)(a)-(f), six exclusive components: (a) search/location/examination labor and (b) separation/redaction labor — both only if 'unreasonably high costs' are shown and specifically identified (15.234(3)), billed at the hourly wage of the lowest-paid employee capable of the task (regardless of who does it), in increments of 15 minutes or more with all partial increments rounded DOWN; (c) nonpaper physical media (CD/DVD/flash) at actual and most reasonably economical cost — only if the requester stipulates physical media and the body has the capability; records emailed/electronically delivered carry no media charge; (d) paper copies capped at 10 cents per sheet for 8.5x11 or 8.5x14 paper, using the most economical means (double-sided counts per sheet); (e) duplication/publication labor at actual time (no unreasonably-high-costs precondition); (f) actual mailing cost, reasonably economical, no expedited shipping unless requested. Under 15.234(2), fringe benefits may be added up to 50% of the labor charge (not exceeding actual fringe cost, multiplier noted); overtime only if the requester stipulates. No fee or deposit may be charged unless the body has established FOIA procedures and guidelines plus a written public summary (posted on its website if it has one) and uses a detailed fee-itemization form (15.234(4)). If a record is already available on the body's website, it is exempt from charges and the body must notify the requester of the address, with limited charges if paper/physical copies are still insisted on (15.234(5)). Untimely responses cut chargeable labor 5% per day up to 50% (15.234(9)). City practice examples: Ann Arbor charges $0.05/page and gives the first hour of staff time free (more generous than statute, voluntary); East Grand Rapids charges the statutory $0.10/page.

**Labor charges:** Search, examination, review, and separation/redaction labor is chargeable ONLY if the body shows that failure to charge would result in 'unreasonably high costs to the public body because of the nature of the request in the particular instance,' specifically identified (MCL 15.234(3), conditioning components (1)(a) and (1)(b)). When chargeable: lowest-paid-capable-employee wage + up to 50% fringe (actual-cost cap) per 15.234(2), 15-minute-or-greater increments with partial increments rounded down. Duplication labor (component (1)(e)) has no such precondition. There is no statutory free-hour threshold — the 'unreasonably high costs' test is the gate — though some cities voluntarily give the first hour free (Ann Arbor). Contractor labor generally barred (Coblentz v City of Novi; fee provisions strictly construed) except redaction outsourcing capped at 6x state minimum wage when the body employs no one capable (15.234(1)(b)).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
MCL 15.234(8): a good-faith deposit may be required before processing only if the estimated total fee exceeds $50.00, and the deposit must not exceed 1/2 (50%) of the total estimated fee; the deposit demand must include a detailed itemization and a nonbinding best-efforts estimate of the time needed to provide the records. Enhanced rule for prior nonpayers: up to 100% deposit if the requester previously failed to pay a FOIA bill in full, subject to strict conditions (final fee was not more than 105% of the estimate, records were made available in the promised timeframe, at least 90 days have passed since the availability notice, no proof of payment) (15.234(11)); the 100%-deposit requirement ends upon payment or 365 days after the unpaid request (15.234(12)). If the required deposit is not received within 45 days from the requester's receipt of the deposit notice, the request is considered abandoned and the body need not fulfill it; the notice must state the abandonment date and is deemed received 3 days after it is sent — effectively 48 days from sending (15.234(14); matches Ann Arbor's stated 48 days).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Two statutory mechanisms, both in MCL 15.234(2): (1) Discretionary public-interest waiver — fees may be waived or reduced if the body determines waiver is in the public interest because search/furnishing copies 'can be considered as primarily benefiting the general public'; entirely discretionary. (2) Indigency discount — the FIRST $20.00 of the fee is waived for an individual who submits an affidavit stating indigency and receipt of specific public assistance (or, if not receiving assistance, facts showing inability to pay); ineligible if the individual has already received the discount twice from that public body in the calendar year, or requests in conjunction with outside parties offering or providing payment or other remuneration to make the request (the body may require the affidavit to so state). The same $20 waiver extends to state-designated nonprofit protection-and-advocacy organizations (developmental disability/mental illness), for requests made directly on behalf of the organization or its clients and wholly consistent with their mission. No full statutory indigency or media waiver.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Deposit not received within 45 days of the requester's (deemed) receipt of the deposit notice: request considered abandoned; processing stops with no further obligation (MCL 15.234(14)). Final invoice unpaid 90+ days after records were made available: on the requester's next request the body may demand a 100% deposit up front (conditions in 15.234(11); ends on payment or 365 days after the unpaid request, 15.234(12)). Cities routinely condition release of the prepared records on payment of the balance of the final itemized fee (practice consistent with the deposit/itemization scheme).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** The only statutory clock modifications: (1) the one-time extension notice of up to 10 business days (MCL 15.235(2)(d)); (2) the deemed-receipt delay — emailed/faxed requests received 1 business day after transmission, spam-folder emails 1 day after the body becomes aware (15.235(1)); (3) written agreement of the requester can modify the response period (15.235(2) 'unless otherwise agreed to in writing'); (4) a proper >$50 deposit demand effectively suspends processing until the deposit is paid (production timing then governed only by the body's nonbinding best-efforts estimate). There is NO statutory tolling for clarification — a clarification exchange does not restart the 5-day clock by statute, though cities treat a clarified request as a new submission in practice. The 45-day deposit-abandonment period runs against the requester (15.234(14)).

## REMEDIES

Judicial: circuit court action within 180 days after the final determination to deny (MCL 15.240(1)(b)); de novo review with the burden on the public body to sustain its denial (15.240(4)); court may order disclosure and conduct in camera review. Fee suits: within 45 days after receiving the notice of the required fee or the determination of a fee appeal (MCL 15.240a(1)(b)); burden on the body to establish the fee complies with its procedures and MCL 15.234; court orders a reduction/refund if excessive. Attorney fees: MANDATORY reasonable attorney fees, costs, and disbursements if the requester prevails in a denial action — construed as prevailing completely (Amberg v City of Dearborn, 497 Mich 28 (2014)); discretionary apportionment if prevailing in part (15.240(6)). In FEE actions, attorney fees are DISCRETIONARY ('may, in its discretion') and available only if the requester obtains a reduction of 50% or more of the total fee (15.240a(6)). Damages/penalties: mandatory $1,000 punitive damages to the requester plus mandatory $1,000 civil fine to the state if the body arbitrarily and capriciously violated the act by refusal or delay (15.240(7)); for arbitrary and capricious overcharging, a mandatory $500 civil fine to the state and DISCRETIONARY $500 punitive damages to the requester (15.240a(7)); and an additional civil fine of $2,500–$7,500 per occurrence, payable to the state general fund, for willful and intentional failure to comply or other bad faith (MCL 15.240b; court considers the body's budget and prior penalties). No criminal penalties.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for Michigan cities: (1) No purpose question and no residency gate — do not require a stated purpose; a name/contact field is fine but flag that purpose demands are unlawful practice; screen only for the prisoner exclusion (MCL 15.232(g)) if relevant. (2) No commercial-rate module — fees are identical for all requesters. (3) Deadline engine: start the 5-business-day clock 1 business day AFTER email submission (statutory deemed receipt); allow exactly one 10-business-day extension with reason and date fields; allow deadline modification by written agreement. (4) Auto-acknowledgment is practice, not law — safe to send; advise requesters to put 'FOIA' in the subject line, which both speeds routing and locks in the deemed-denial and 5%/day fee-reduction protections. (5) Fee module: hard-cap paper at $0.10/sheet; labor in 15-minute increments rounded down at lowest-paid-capable rate + fringe ≤50% (actual-cost cap, multiplier disclosed); require the statutory detailed fee-itemization form on every charge; block any fee or deposit if the city hasn't published procedures/guidelines and a public summary (15.234(4)); suppress search/redaction labor unless an 'unreasonably high costs' justification is entered (15.234(3)); auto-link website-available records in lieu of charging (15.234(5)). (6) Deposit logic: trigger only when estimate >$50, cap at 50% (100% only for flagged prior nonpayers, 365-day sunset), attach the nonbinding completion estimate, and start a 45-day abandonment timer measured from deemed receipt of the notice (3 days after sending — 48 days total). (7) Fee-waiver question: offer the $20 indigency affidavit (max 2/year per body; affidavit may attest no outside-party payment) and a discretionary public-interest waiver request box. (8) Litigation-exposure flags: missed 5-day deadline on a request labeled 'FOIA' (deemed denial + automatic 5%/day labor discount up to 50%, $1,000+$1,000 exposure, $2,500–$7,500 fine for willful noncompliance); denials lacking exemption explanations, nonexistence certificate, or appeal-rights notice (15.235(5)); fees later cut ≥50% in court (discretionary attorney-fee exposure plus mandatory $500 fine if arbitrary/capricious). (9) Appeals: route denial appeals (must contain the word 'appeal') to the head of the public body with a 10-business-day SLA (received at next regular meeting if the head is a board); only offer the fee-appeal path if the city's procedures provide for it — and note adopting one is best practice; show the 180-day (denial) and 45-day (fee) court clocks.
