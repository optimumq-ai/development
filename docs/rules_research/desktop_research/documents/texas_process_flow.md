# Texas City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Texas city, under the Texas Public Information Act (PIA/TPIA) (Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552; cost rules at 1 Tex. Admin. Code sec. 70.3 (adopted under sec. 552.262)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** No fixed production deadline — produce promptly. 10 business days to: produce, certify a later date in writing, send no-records or prior-determination notice (HB 4219, 9/1/25), or ask the AG to rule stating specific exceptions. Miss the AG deadline and records are presumed public (552.301-.302).

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Anyone may request — no residency or citizenship requirement. The body "may not inquire into the purpose for which information will be used" (sec. 552.222(b)); the only permitted identity inquiry is to establish "proper identification" (sec. 552.222(a)), and requests may effectively be pseudonymous/anonymous by email. Exception: a body need not accept requests from persons incarcerated in correctional facilities or their agents, other than the individual's attorney (sec. 552.028). Trap for email requests: a request is only effective if sent to the body's one designated email/mailing address, hand-delivered, or sent via another designated method (portal); if the city has properly posted its designated addresses, an email to a random employee triggers no duties (sec. 552.234, added by SB 944 (2019)); HB 4214 (2025, immediate effect) requires bodies to submit their designated addresses to the AG annually for a public database.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None. The TPIA has no commercial-purpose branch: purpose may not be asked (sec. 552.222(b)), and deadlines and fees do not vary by commercial use. The only volume/requestor-based differentiation is sec. 552.275 (optional cap on uncompensated personnel time per requestor — yearly limit not less than 36 hours per 12-month period, monthly limit not less than 15 hours; once exceeded, the body gives a written cost estimate within 10 days and the requestor must respond/pay within 10 days), from which representatives of news media (radio/TV/newspaper), elected officials, publicly funded 501(c)(3) legal-services organizations, and journalists/scholars/researchers employed by institutions of higher education are exempt. (Separate non-TPIA statutes, e.g. Transp. Code ch. 730 for motor-vehicle records, impose use-based limits.)

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No generic statutory acknowledgment duty. The statute instead compels one of several substantive responses: prompt production; a sec. 552.221(c)-(d) written certification (records in active use/storage, or cannot produce within 10 business days) setting a date; an itemized cost estimate (sec. 552.2615); a clarification request (sec. 552.222); the sec. 552.301 AG-ruling notice; or, since 9/1/2025 (HB 4219), the sec. 552.221(f) no-responsive-records notice or (g) previous-determination notice — so every request now requires some written answer within 10 business days. City practice: portals (e.g., Austin's GovQA Public Records Center) auto-acknowledge immediately; Austin also sends a status update on business day 8 — practice, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Texas has no fixed production deadline: the officer for public information must produce records "promptly" — as soon as possible under the circumstances, without delay (sec. 552.221(a)). The famous "10 business days" is a set of checkpoint duties, not a production deadline: (1) if the body cannot produce within 10 business days of receipt, it must certify that in writing and set a reasonable date and hour when the records will be available (sec. 552.221(d)); (2) to withhold anything not covered by a previous determination, it must request an Attorney General ruling and notify the requestor within 10 business days, and under HB 4219 (89th Leg., eff. 9/1/2025) must state the specific exceptions claimed in that window (sec. 552.301(a)-(b), (d)); written arguments to the AG are due by the 15th business day (sec. 552.301(e)); the AG rules within 45 business days, extendable once by 10 (sec. 552.306); (3) HB 4219 added sec. 552.221(f) (written notice within 10 business days if no responsive information exists) and sec. 552.221(g) (written notice within 10 business days identifying the specific previous determination relied on to withhold). Constructive denial: missing the sec. 552.301 deadlines means the information is presumed public and must be released absent a compelling reason (sec. 552.302); failure to produce promptly or to request a ruling is actionable by mandamus (sec. 552.321) and, post-HB 4219, by complaint to the AG (sec. 552.328). For electronic data requiring programming/manipulation, a written statement and cost estimate are due within 20 days (calendar, not business), extendable once by 10 days on written notice within the initial 20 (sec. 552.231); the requestor then has 30 days to respond or the request is withdrawn.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

The body may ask the requestor to clarify an unclear request and may discuss narrowing an unduly broad one, but may not use the dialogue to probe purpose (sec. 552.222(b)-(c)). A written clarification request must include a statement of the consequences of not responding; if the requestor does not respond in writing by the 61st day, the request is considered withdrawn (sec. 552.222(d)-(e)); for emailed requests the clarification may be sent to the same email address (sec. 552.222(f)). The 10-business-day AG-ruling clock is measured from the date the request is clarified or narrowed. The AG has signaled clarification may not be used as a delay tactic; only genuinely unclear requests toll deadlines.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Since 9/1/2025 (HB 4219), if the body has no information responsive to a request it must notify the requestor in writing within 10 business days (sec. 552.221(f)); before that, no-records letters were practice, not statute. No duty to create new information, perform legal research, or answer questions (longstanding AG constructions); programming/manipulation of existing electronic data is handled — with the sec. 552.231 written statement and charges — and is not "creation" of new information.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Segregability is built in: exceptions apply to "information," not whole documents, so non-excepted portions must be released with excepted material redacted (sec. 552.301(e)(1)(D) labeling; sec. 552.352 risk for over-release). Under the AG's cost rules, no labor charge may be recovered for time spent by an attorney, legal assistant, or any other person reviewing the requested information to determine whether exceptions apply or to research/prepare an AG-ruling request (1 TAC 70.3; authority from sec. 552.262) — so exemption-review/redaction-decision labor is free to the requestor. Physical redaction time can in practice be swept into the $15/hr processing labor only when labor is chargeable at all (copy requests over 50 pages, remote storage, multiple unconnected buildings, or a sec. 552.275 capped requestor); for copy requests of 50 or fewer pages, no materials, labor, or overhead may be charged — only the per-page copy charge (sec. 552.261(a)).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
A Texas city cannot simply deny a request on its own authority. To withhold, it must within 10 business days (1) request an AG open-records ruling stating the specific exceptions claimed (sec. 552.301(b), as amended by HB 4219) and (2) give the requestor written notice of that, then submit written comments, the request, a signed statement of the receipt date, and labeled copies or representative samples by the 15th business day, with a redacted copy of its comments to the requestor (sec. 552.301(d)-(e-1)); or rely on a previous determination, which post-HB 4219 requires written notice identifying the specific previous determination within 10 business days (sec. 552.221(g)). Missing the deadlines triggers the sec. 552.302 presumption of openness. Some confidentiality statutes are mandatory (sec. 552.352 criminalizes release of confidential info). Certain exceptions permit redaction without an AG ruling with notice to the requestor of the right to seek AG review (e.g., secs. 552.130(c), 552.136(c), 552.024/552.1175 personal info).

**Administrative appeal:** No true administrative appeal that orders release on the merits — the AG open-records ruling process (secs. 552.301-.306) is initiated by the GOVERNMENT, not the requestor. Requestor-side administrative routes: (1) NEW under HB 4219 (eff. 9/1/2025) — written complaint to the AG, attaching the request and any correspondence, for failure to comply with sec. 552.221 response/notice duties (sec. 552.328); on a finding of noncompliance the AG may require open-records training for the PIO within 6 months, prohibit charging that requestor for the information, and require an AG-ruling request within 5 business days with release absent a compelling reason; no statutory deadline for filing the complaint. (2) Cost/overcharge complaint to the AG (sec. 552.269); the body must answer AG questions within 10 business days; requestor may recover three times the overcharge if the body did not act in good faith. (3) Signed written complaint to the county or district attorney where the body is located (Travis County DA for state agencies; the AG if the body IS the county/district attorney) seeking civil enforcement (sec. 552.3215); the prosecutor must decide and notify the complainant within 31 days; if declined, the complainant may re-file with the AG before the 31st day after return, or after 90 days of prosecutor inaction. AG open-government hotline mediates informally.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Set by AG rule, 1 TAC 70.3, incorporated via sec. 552.262: standard paper copy $0.10/page or part of a page; oversized copy $0.50/page; rewritable CD $1.00 (non-rewritable CD at actual cost); DVD $3.00; diskette $1.00; audio cassette $1.00; VHS $2.50; magnetic tape and specialty media at actual cost; postage/shipping at actual cost; no sales tax. Labor for locating/compiling/reproducing: $15.00/hr — chargeable on copy requests only if more than 50 pages, or records are in a remote storage facility or two or more separate unconnected buildings (sec. 552.261(a)); programming personnel $28.50/hr; overhead surcharge of 20% on chargeable labor; computer resource charges per rule (mainframe $10/CPU minute, midsize $1.50/CPU minute, client-server $2.20/clock hour, PC $1.00/clock hour). Records existing in electronic form must be provided electronically if requested and feasible; emailing existing electronic copies typically incurs no copy charge beyond any allowable labor. Inspection-only is generally free (out of scope here). An itemized written estimate is mandatory whenever charges will exceed $40 (sec. 552.2615(a)); an updated statement is required if charges will exceed the estimate by 20% or more, and the final bill may not exceed the (updated) estimate by 20% or more (sec. 552.2615(c)-(f)). If labor is charged, the requestor may demand a written statement of the time required (sec. 552.261(b)).

**Labor charges:** Chargeable only within limits: $15/hr for locating/compiling/reproducing, plus 20% overhead, but ONLY if the copy request exceeds 50 pages of paper records, or the records sit in a remote storage facility or two or more separate buildings not physically connected (sec. 552.261(a)); at 50 pages or fewer, only the per-page charge (plus any nonstandard media). Time spent by an attorney, legal assistant, or anyone else reviewing for exemptions or researching/preparing AG-ruling requests is never chargeable (1 TAC 70.3, under sec. 552.262 rulemaking). Programming/manipulation of electronic data: $28.50/hr (1 TAC 70.3; sec. 552.231). Requestors past a sec. 552.275 cap (yearly limit of at least 36 hours) may be charged for ALL personnel time and overhead thereafter, after a written estimate.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
A city may require a deposit or bond for anticipated charges, but only after providing the sec. 552.2615 itemized estimate, and only when estimated charges exceed $100 (bodies with more than 15 full-time employees) or $50 (fewer than 16 full-time employees) (sec. 552.263(a)-(b)). No percentage cap on the deposit is stated (cities commonly demand 50-100% of the estimate — practice varies). The request is considered received on the date the deposit/bond is paid (sec. 552.263(e)), so the clock does not run meanwhile; if the requestor instead modifies the request in response, the modified request is a new request. A body may also require a deposit for unpaid amounts over $100 owed from the same requestor's past requests before processing new ones (sec. 552.263(c)), though it cannot otherwise sue to collect. Requiring a deposit as a down payment for hypothetical future requests is prohibited (sec. 552.263(d)).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary in practice: a body "shall" waive or reduce charges if it determines that waiver is "in the public interest because providing the copy of the information primarily benefits the general public" (sec. 552.267(a)) — mandatory once the body makes the public-benefit determination, but the determination itself rests with the body; there is no requestor-enforceable public-interest or indigency waiver and no media rate. A body may also waive when the cost of processing collection exceeds the charge (sec. 552.267(b)). City practice: Austin waives fees for minimal responsive documents; many cities waive bills under $5-$10 — practice, not statute.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
If the requestor fails to respond in writing within 10 business days to a required itemized estimate or updated estimate (sec. 552.2615(b), (d)), or fails to pay a demanded deposit/bond by the 10th business day after it is required (sec. 552.263(e-1)), the request is considered withdrawn. After records are ready, failure to inspect/pick up or pay postage and charges within 60 days of the availability notice also deems the request withdrawn (sec. 552.221(e)). Unpaid balances over $100 from prior requests can be demanded as a deposit before new requests are processed (sec. 552.263(c)); the body cannot otherwise pursue collection. HB 4219 twist: if the AG finds on a sec. 552.328 complaint that the body violated its sec. 552.221 response duties, one sanction bars the body from charging that requestor at all for the information.

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** (1) Clarification: a genuinely unclear request pauses obligations; the AG-ruling clock runs from the date the request is clarified/narrowed; no written response by the 61st day = withdrawal (sec. 552.222(d)-(e)). (2) Estimates: sending the mandatory over-$40 itemized statement suspends production duties until the requestor accepts, modifies, or files an overcharge complaint; 10 business days of silence = withdrawal (sec. 552.2615). (3) Deposits: request deemed received only when deposit/bond is paid (sec. 552.263(e)). (4) Catastrophe: a body currently significantly impacted by a catastrophe may suspend PIA duties for up to 7 consecutive calendar days, extendable once by up to 7 more (14 max per catastrophe), after filing a catastrophe notice with the AG on the prescribed form; deadlines toll and mid-suspension requests are deemed received the first business day after the suspension ends (sec. 552.2325, added 2019, narrowed 2021 — remote-work situations excluded). (5) Skeleton-crew/closure days: days the body's offices are closed generally do not count as business days (AG guidance; post-COVID disputes narrowed this). (6) Active use/storage certification defers production to a certified date (sec. 552.221(c)). (7) Programming statement: the sec. 552.231 written statement pauses matters until the requestor responds (30 days or withdrawn).

## REMEDIES

Judicial: requestor (or the AG) may sue for a writ of mandamus to compel release or to compel the body to request an AG ruling (sec. 552.321); a requestor's suit is filed in district court of the county where the body's main offices are located (the AG files in Travis County) (sec. 552.321(b)). The body may sue only the attorney general, in Travis County district court, to challenge a ruling, and must file within 30 calendar days of receiving the decision or must comply (sec. 552.324). Attorney fees: mandatory ('shall assess') for a plaintiff who substantially prevails under secs. 552.321/.3215, EXCEPT where the body reasonably relied on a court judgment/order applicable to it, a published appellate opinion, or a written AG decision (sec. 552.323(a)); in sec. 552.324 suits fees are discretionary and only where the action or defense was groundless in fact or law, considering reasonable basis in law and good faith (sec. 552.323(b)). Civil: declaratory/injunctive relief via sec. 552.3215 complaints and suits. Criminal misdemeanors, each "official misconduct": willful destruction/alteration/removal of records — $25-$4,000 fine and/or 3 days-3 months county jail (sec. 552.351); distribution of confidential information — misdemeanor (sec. 552.352); failure/refusal to provide access with criminal negligence — up to $1,000 fine and/or 6 months, with affirmative defenses for pending AG rulings, reasonable reliance on court orders/AG opinions, a declaratory suit filed within 10 days, or an agent's reliance on written instructions (sec. 552.353).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for Texas cities: (1) Intake — route all email requests to the single designated PIA email (sec. 552.234) and warn users that requests sent elsewhere are void when addresses are properly posted; capture received date; treat portal as a designated method; keep the AG address registry current (HB 4214 annual submission). (2) Identity — name/contact optional beyond delivery needs; NEVER ask purpose or commercial use (sec. 552.222(b) prohibition), so no purpose question and no commercial-rate module; do include an incarcerated-requestor screen (sec. 552.028). (3) Auto-acknowledgment is safe and standard city practice though not statutory; build a business-day-8 status ping (Austin model). (4) Timers — 10-business-day timer with five exits: produce, certify a date (552.221(d)), no-records notice (552.221(f)), previous-determination notice (552.221(g)), or AG-ruling notice stating specific exceptions (552.301); post-HB 4219 no request may silently close. (5) Fee engine — $0.10/page; $15/hr labor only if over 50 pages or remote storage/multiple buildings (552.261(a)); $28.50/hr programming; 20% overhead; auto-generate itemized estimate at over $40 with a 10-business-day acceptance timer and deemed-withdrawal; deposit gate at over $100 (over $50 for bodies under 16 FTEs) that resets the received-date to payment; bar final bills from exceeding estimate by 20%+; deposit-demand option for requestors owing over $100. (6) Clarification module with the statutorily required consequences warning and 61-day auto-withdrawal (552.222). (7) Track cumulative personnel hours per requestor per 12 months for the sec. 552.275 cap (36-hr yearly floor) with statutory exemption flags for media/officials/legal aid/scholars. (8) Litigation-exposure flags: missed 552.301 deadline (presumed open, 552.302), sec. 552.328 AG complaints (can void all fees and force a 5-day ruling request), mandatory fee-shifting risk (552.323(a)), and criminal exposure for the PIO (552.353). (9) Catastrophe mode implementing sec. 552.2325 tolling (7+7 calendar days max).

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) statutes.capitol.texas.gov and the enrolled HB 4219 text remained unfetchable; the new sec. 552.221(f)/(g) subsection letters, sec. 552.301(b) specific-exceptions language, and sec. 552.328 sanctions are confirmed from the official HB 4219 bill analysis (capitol.texas.gov), the OAG 2025 legislative update, and TML's 2025 PIA guide — convergent but not the codified text itself; texas.public.law mirrors were last updated 2017-2019 for these sections. (2) Whether physical redaction labor is chargeable on 50+ page requests (as part of $15/hr processing) vs. barred as review time is not crisply resolved; the 1 TAC 70.3 bar clearly covers exemption-determination and AG-brief time. (3) 1 TAC 70.3 rates verified against a compilation current through Sept. 2024; no 2025-26 rate amendment found, but re-check the AG schedule periodically. (4) The 2025 designated-address registry bill is HB 4214 per the OAG's own legislative update; TML's guide calls it HB 4212 with an Oct. 1 annual deadline — bill number taken from OAG, the Oct. 1 date not confirmed on a primary source. (5) Press claims (KETR) that HB 4219 expanded contractor/personnel-record disclosure are NOT supported by the bill analysis, which shows no such provisions; personnel-information changes came via separate 2025 bills (e.g., SB 1540). (6) Sec. 552.352 (confidential-info distribution) penalty amounts not independently re-verified this pass. (7) Deposit percentage practice (50-100% of estimate) and small-bill waivers vary by city; Austin practices from its site, other cities differ.
