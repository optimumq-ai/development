# Pennsylvania City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Pennsylvania city, under the Right-to-Know Law (RTKL), Act 3 of 2008 (65 P.S. §§ 67.101–67.3104). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** PA RTKL: 5 business days from open-records officer receipt to grant, deny, or extend; one 30-day extension by notice; silence = deemed denial. No purpose questions allowed. No search/review/redaction labor fees ever; email delivery free. OOR appeal within 15 business days.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Requester must be "a person that is a legal resident of the United States" (§ 67.102 definition of "requester," verified verbatim) — a residency screen unusual among states; "person" includes organizations. A written request "shall include the name and address to which the agency should address its response" (§ 67.703). Purpose may NOT be demanded: "A written request need not include any explanation of the requester's reason for requesting or intended use of the records unless otherwise required by law" (§ 67.703, verified verbatim). Anonymous requests: § 67.702 permits (does not require) agencies to fulfill anonymous verbal or written requests; per OOR guidance an anonymous requester has no RTKL appeal rights — only a written request from an identified requester carries Chapter 11 remedies, and a legal name is needed to appeal to the OOR. City practice (not re-verified this pass): Pittsburgh refuses anonymous requests and requires a physical street address; Philadelphia requires name and contact info.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None in the RTKL. No commercial-purpose disclosure may be required (§ 67.703 — no explanation of reason or intended use required), no different deadline, and no different fee tier for commercial requesters. Exception outside the RTKL: certain record classes have their own commercial regimes (e.g., vehicle records under 75 Pa.C.S. § 6114; police audio/video under Act 22 of 2017, 42 Pa.C.S. ch. 67A, which is outside the RTKL and carries its own fees — Philadelphia has charged about $125 per recording, reported practice not re-verified). For an ordinary city records request, commercial purpose changes nothing.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory acknowledgment duty. The only statutory clock is the 5-business-day response under § 67.901; a § 67.902 extension notice sent within that window functions as the interim acknowledgment (must state reason, expected date, fee estimate). § 67.502(b) requires the open-records officer, on receipt, to note the date of receipt on the written request and to compute and note the date the 5-business-day period expires (verified verbatim). City practice: many cities (and portal vendors) send an automatic email acknowledgment noting the date of ORO receipt, but this is practice, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Five business days from the date the written request is received by the agency's open-records officer (65 P.S. § 67.901, verified verbatim: "The time for response shall not exceed five business days from the date the written request is received by the open-records officer"). By day 5 the agency must make a determination: grant access (produce or make copies available), deny with reasons, or invoke an extension; § 67.901 also requires a good-faith effort to determine whether the record is public and to respond promptly. Failure to send a response within 5 business days = deemed denial (§ 67.901). Extension: § 67.902(a) lists seven grounds (record requires redaction; record stored offsite/in a remote location; response requires retrieval from a timely-unavailable location; legal review needed to determine public status; requester failed to comply with agency policy; requester refuses to pay applicable fees; extent or nature of request precludes response in 5 days). The agency must send written notice within the 5-day period stating the reason, a reasonable expected response date, and an estimate of applicable fees (§ 67.902(b)(2)). If the expected date is more than 30 days after the 5-business-day period, the request is deemed denied unless the requester agrees in writing to the longer period (§ 67.902(b)(3), verified verbatim). So the outer statutory limit without requester consent is 5 business days + 30 calendar days. The deadline is for a determination and production of what is granted; if copies are made available for pickup, § 67.905 governs retrieval.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

§ 67.703 (verified verbatim): a written request "should identify or describe the records sought with sufficient specificity to enable the agency to ascertain which records are being requested" — statutory text says "should," but case law and OOR practice treat insufficient specificity as a valid denial ground the agency must state under § 67.903. No express statutory duty to assist requesters, though the open-records officer has receipt/tracking duties (§ 67.502) and OOR guidance encourages agencies to seek clarification. Case law supplies the test: Pa. Dept. of Educ. v. Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, 119 A.3d 1121 (Pa. Cmwlth. 2015) — three-part specificity inquiry (subject matter identifying the transaction or activity, limited scope, finite timeframe); Office of the District Attorney of Philadelphia v. Bagwell, 155 A.3d 1119 (Pa. Cmwlth. 2017) upheld email/correspondence requests tied to named custodians and defined subjects over 12–15-month periods, holding timeframes must be finite but need not be narrow and that burden of compliance does not excuse RTKL obligations. There is no statutory tolling for clarification — agencies typically deny for lack of specificity or negotiate a rewritten request (which restarts the 5-day clock as a new request; practice, not statute).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Agency is not required to create a record that does not currently exist or to compile, maintain, format or organize a record in a manner it does not currently use (§ 67.705, verified verbatim). A no-records response is effectively a denial the requester can appeal to the OOR. Case law: Hodges v. Pa. Dept. of Health, 29 A.3d 1190 (Pa. Cmwlth. 2011) — an agency affidavit/attestation that records do not exist after a good-faith search is generally dispositive absent contrary evidence; OOR expects a sworn attestation on appeal describing the search. § 67.901 requires a good-faith effort to determine whether records exist and are public.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Mandatory segregability: if information not subject to access is an integral part of the record and cannot be separated, the agency shall redact it and grant access to the remainder (§ 67.706, verified verbatim); redaction is treated as a partial denial appealable to the OOR. Redaction labor is NOT chargeable: the OOR Official RTKL Fee Schedule states "No additional fee may be imposed" for redaction, and staff time/salary may not be charged (SERS v. OOR, 10 A.3d 358 (Pa. Cmwlth. 2010)). The OOR fee schedule itself allows one narrow exception: if the agency cannot securely redact electronically, it may charge the per-page copy rate for the paper prints necessary to perform the redaction.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Written denial required containing: description of the record requested; specific reasons for denial including citation of supporting legal authority; name, title, business address, business telephone number, and signature of the open-records officer; date of the response; and the procedure to appeal (§ 67.903). Exemptions (§ 67.708(b), roughly 30 categories) must be proven by the agency by a preponderance of the evidence (§ 67.708(a)); records of local agencies are presumed public unless exempt, privileged, or protected by other law (§ 67.305). No Vaughn-index requirement in the statute, but attestations/exemption logs are expected on OOR appeal. Deadline for denial = same 5-business-day/extended deadline; silence = deemed denial (§ 67.901).

**Administrative appeal:** Yes — Office of Open Records (OOR), an independent state agency, hears appeals from local agencies (cities) for non-criminal records. Requester must appeal within 15 business days of the mailing date of the agency's response or of a deemed denial (§ 67.1101(a), verified verbatim); the appeal must state grounds and address the agency's stated reasons. The appeals officer must mail a final determination within 30 days of receipt of the appeal or the appeal is deemed denied (§ 67.1101(b), verified verbatim). Exception: appeals about criminal investigative records in the possession of a local agency go to the appeals officer designated by the county district attorney (§ 67.503(d)(2)) — e.g., Philadelphia police criminal-record appeals go to the DA's appeals officer, not the OOR. OOR appeal is free; either party may then seek judicial review.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
§ 67.1307 plus the OOR Official RTKL Fee Schedule (binding on Commonwealth and local agencies; current schedule adopted Dec. 30, 2024, per openrecords.pa.gov, all figures verified): B&W copies up to $0.25/page for the first 1,000 pages, up to $0.20/page beyond 1,000; color up to $0.50/page; conversion of an electronic record to paper up to $0.25/page; records delivered by email — "No additional fee may be imposed"; CD/DVD up to actual cost not exceeding $1.00/disc; flash drive up to actual cost; specialized documents (blueprints, nonstandard sizes) actual cost; certification up to $5.00 per record (not for compliance-only certification); postage up to actual USPS first-class cost. No fee for the agency's review of a record to determine whether it is subject to access (§ 67.1307(g), verified verbatim); no search/retrieval or staff-time/salary charges (OOR schedule; SERS v. OOR). No programming charges for ordinary requests; "enhanced electronic access" fees only under an OOR-approved user-fee schedule (§ 67.1307(e)). Except as otherwise provided by statute, no other fees may be imposed unless the agency necessarily incurs actual costs, which must be reasonable (§ 67.1307(g)).

**Labor charges:** Not chargeable. No fee for search, retrieval, review, or redaction staff time under any threshold — zero, not capped (§ 67.1307(g) bars review fees; OOR Official Fee Schedule: no charge for searching/retrieval and no staff time or salary charges; SERS v. OOR, 10 A.3d 358 (Pa. Cmwlth. 2010)). This makes Pennsylvania one of the strictest no-labor-charge states. Statutory exception outside the RTKL: Act 22 of 2017 police audio/video requests (42 Pa.C.S. ch. 67A) carry their own fees, which in Philadelphia have included labor components (reported practice).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Prepayment (not a percentage deposit): an agency may require prepayment of an estimate of the fees if the fees required to fulfill the request are expected to exceed $100 (§ 67.1307(h), verified verbatim). The § 67.902(b)(2) extension notice must include a fee estimate. No statutory percentage cap — the agency may demand the full estimated amount up front once over $100. Under $100, fees are collected at delivery (Philadelphia practice: payment required at time of disclosure).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary only. § 67.1307(f) (verified verbatim): an agency MAY waive duplication fees, including when the requester duplicates the record or when "the agency deems it is in the public interest to do so." No mandatory public-interest waiver, no statutory indigency waiver. OOR fee schedule: all fees waivable at agency discretion. Because email delivery of electronic records must be free, a fee-waiver module matters mainly for paper copies.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
If the agency's response states that copies are available for delivery at the agency office and the requester fails to retrieve them within 60 days of the response, the agency may dispose of the copies and retain any fees paid to date (§ 67.905, verified verbatim) — effective abandonment. If a requester refuses to pay applicable fees, that is itself a ground for a § 67.902(a)(6) extension, and agencies condition delivery on payment; where fees exceed $100 the agency may await prepayment (§ 67.1307(h)). OOR decisions have permitted agencies to require payment of outstanding fees from prior requests before processing new ones (decisional practice, not express statute).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No statutory tolling for clarification — the RTKL clock is not paused; a vague request is denied for insufficient specificity or voluntarily rewritten (new request, new clock). Pauses/limits that do exist: (1) § 67.902 extension notice sent within the 5 business days extends the response up to 30 calendar days; (2) beyond 30 days only by the requester's written agreement (§ 67.902(b)(3)); (3) prepayment demand when the estimate exceeds $100 (§ 67.1307(h)) — production awaits payment (OOR treats the response as timely if the determination issued; tolling pending payment is decisional practice, not express statute); (4) the clock starts only when the OPEN-RECORDS OFFICER receives the request (§§ 67.901, 67.502(b)) — a request emailed to the wrong employee has not started the clock, a major portal-design point.

## REMEDIES

From an OOR final determination involving a local agency, either party petitions the court of common pleas of the county within 30 days of the mailing date of the final determination (§ 67.1302); Commonwealth Court for Commonwealth agencies (§ 67.1301). Review is de novo with a full record (Bowling v. OOR, 75 A.3d 453 (Pa. 2013)). Attorney fees: discretionary — the court MAY award reasonable attorney fees and costs if it reverses (or grants access after a deemed denial) and finds the agency willfully or with wanton disregard deprived access or otherwise acted in bad faith, OR that the exemptions/defenses asserted were not based on a reasonable interpretation of law (§ 67.1304(a), verified verbatim); sanctions available against frivolous requester appeals (§ 67.1304(b)). Civil penalties: up to $1,500 for bad-faith denial (§ 67.1305(a), verified); up to $500 per day for failure to promptly comply with a court order (§ 67.1305(b), verified). No criminal penalties. Uniontown Newspapers, Inc. v. Pa. Dept. of Corrections, 243 A.3d 19 (Pa. 2020) upheld bad-faith sanctions and $118,458 in attorney fees; Bagwell (2017) affirmed a $500 bad-faith penalty against the Philadelphia DA.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for a PA city: (1) Identity fields: require name and mailing or email address (§ 67.703); optionally warn that anonymous submissions are honored only at city discretion and carry no OOR appeal rights (§ 67.702); consider a US-legal-resident attestation checkbox (§ 67.102) — many cities do not police it. (2) NO purpose question and NO commercial-rate module — the statute affirmatively provides that no explanation of reason or intended use may be required (§ 67.703); commercialBranch is false. (3) Route all submissions to the Open Records Officer inbox — the 5-day clock starts only on ORO receipt (§§ 67.901, 67.502(b)), so the portal should timestamp ORO receipt and auto-acknowledge with that date (acknowledgment is practice, not statute). (4) Deadline engine: 5 business days to determination; one extension notice (reason + expected date + fee estimate) buys up to 30 calendar days; anything longer needs the requester's written agreement captured in-portal; missed deadlines auto-flag as deemed denial with 15-business-day OOR appeal exposure. (5) Fee module: $0.25/page B&W (tier to $0.20 after 1,000), $0.50 color, $1 CD/DVD, $5 certification, actual postage; hard-code $0 for email delivery of electronic records and $0 for search/review/redaction labor; prepayment gate only when estimate > $100 (full amount, no percentage cap); discretionary fee-waiver checkbox only. (6) Non-payment: 60-day pickup window, then disposal with fees retained (§ 67.905). (7) Litigation flags: deemed denials, bad-faith indicators ($1,500 penalty + fee-shifting exposure under §§ 1304–1305), and criminal-investigative-record requests (appeal language must route to the county DA's appeals officer, not the OOR, per § 67.503(d)(2); Act 22 police video has a separate non-RTKL regime and fees). (8) Offer the OOR standard/uniform form — all Commonwealth and local agencies must accept it (§ 67.505); Philadelphia uses the statewide form.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) No enacted 2025–26 RTKL amendment found as of July 2026. Note: SB 226 (2025–26 session) is NOT a general RTKL modernization bill — it narrowly subjects the PA School Boards Association to the RTKL (per the Senate State Government Committee's May 6, 2025 agenda); its post-committee status could not be confirmed because the General Assembly site (palegis.us) blocks automated fetching. Even if enacted, it would not change city request handling. The RTKL's core response/fee/appeal provisions remain the 2008 Act 3 text (verified against the OOR's posted statute). (2) OOR fee schedule: current adoption date Dec. 30, 2024 confirmed on the OOR website (all rates verified); note the PDF at one openrecords.pa.gov URL still carries a 'Updated December 30, 2022' header with identical rates — the numbers are consistent across both. (3) Email submission of requests: § 67.702/agency policy governs submission channels; Philadelphia and Pittsburgh accept email, but there is no verbatim statutory mandate to accept email — treat as near-universal practice under agency policy (not re-verified this pass). (4) Whether the response clock is formally tolled pending prepayment over $100 is OOR decisional practice, not express statute. (5) Requiring payment of outstanding prior-request fees before processing new requests is supported by OOR decisions, not statute — varies by city. (6) City-specific practices (Pittsburgh no-anonymous/no-P.O.-box policy; Philadelphia $125 Act 22 video fee and form requirements) were carried over from the draft and not re-verified this pass. (7) § 67.703 phrases the specificity requirement as 'should,' not 'shall' — the enforceable test is judicial (Post-Gazette/Bagwell); portals should not overstate it as a statutory mandate. (8) Residency enforcement varies: many cities never verify US legal residency.
