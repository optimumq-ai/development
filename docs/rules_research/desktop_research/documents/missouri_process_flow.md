# Missouri City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Missouri city, under the Missouri Sunshine Law (RSMo Chapter 610 (secs. 610.010-610.035); requests governed chiefly by secs. 610.023 and 610.026). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** Act within 3 business days (610.023.3): produce, deny with citation, or delay notice with detailed cause + specific earliest date/time. No admin appeal. Fees 10c/page + actual-cost research; full prepayment OK; unpaid or unclarified requests withdrawn after 90/150 days with notice (eff. 8/28/2025).

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
The Sunshine Law imposes no identity, purpose, or residency requirements: any member of the public may request, and access "is not limited to United States, Missouri or community citizens" (RCFP Missouri guide; sec. 610.011 liberal-construction mandate). Purpose does not affect the right to records; it matters only if the requester seeks the discretionary public-interest fee waiver under 610.026.1(1) (public understanding vs. primarily commercial interest). Note: the separate, older State and Local Records Law (RSMo 109.180) speaks of access by "citizens" of Missouri, but city email requests are handled under the Sunshine Law, which has no such limit. Email requests to a city are valid; no form required. Practical anonymity is possible (an email address suffices), though a name is typically needed for payment/invoicing.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No commercial-requester branch: the statute never requires disclosure of commercial purpose and sets no different deadline or fee schedule for commercial requests. Commercial interest is relevant only negatively — the discretionary fee waiver in 610.026.1(1) applies when disclosure "is likely to contribute significantly to public understanding of the operations or activities of the public governmental body" and "is not primarily in the commercial interest of the requester" (text verified on Justia 2025 code). (HB 145 (2025) also amended the utility-customer exemption, 610.021(26), to require municipal utilities to disclose customer name, billing address, service location, and service dates for commercial utility ACCOUNTS — an exemption nuance, not request handling.)

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory acknowledgment duty; the 3-business-day duty to act (610.023.3) functions as the required initial response (grant, provide delay explanation + specific earliest date/time, or deny). City practice: portal cities (e.g., Columbia's NextRequest) send automatic email confirmation plus a custodian acknowledgment; smaller cities acknowledge by reply email as practice only.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Sec. 610.023.3 (verified against revisor.mo.gov, text eff. 8/28/2004 and unchanged by the 2025 amendments): each request must be ACTED UPON "as soon as possible, but in no event later than the end of the third business day following the date the request is received by the custodian." The deadline requires action/response, not necessarily production. If access is not granted immediately, the custodian must give (a) a detailed explanation of the cause for further delay and (b) the place and EARLIEST time and date the record will be available; the period for producing may be extended only for reasonable cause. Gross v. Parson, 624 S.W.3d 877 (Mo. banc 2021) held that an estimate ("120 business days" from payment) without a specific earliest date, and without a contemporaneous detailed explanation of the cause for delay, violates 610.023.3. There is no fixed outer production deadline and no statutory deemed-denial rule; silence past 3 business days is simply a violation enforceable in circuit court under sec. 610.027. Reasonableness governs actual production timing.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

No statutory duty to assist or to narrow requests; custodians commonly ask clarifying questions as practice. New since Aug. 28, 2025 (HB 145, adding 610.026.2(2)): if the body responds to a request "in order to seek a clarification" and no response to the clarification request is received within 90 days (150 days if requested fees exceed $1,000), the request is CONSIDERED WITHDRAWN; the statute requires the body to include notice of this consequence. The 3-business-day clock is not expressly tolled by a clarification request — flag as a litigation-exposure gray area; best practice is to send the clarification request within the 3 days as the "action" on the request.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Statute is silent on no-records responses; AG guidance and case law establish that the Sunshine Law does not require a body to create a record that does not exist or compile new records to answer questions. Practice (and prudent compliance with 610.023.3) is a written response within 3 business days stating no responsive records exist. A false no-records response risks knowing/purposeful violation penalties under 610.027.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Sec. 610.024 (verified): records containing both exempt and nonexempt material must be separated, with the nonexempt material made available; the body must generally describe the material exempted unless the description would reveal the exempt contents. Redaction labor: attorney review time is NOT chargeable — Gross v. Parson, 624 S.W.3d 877 (Mo. banc 2021) held legal review is not "[r]esearch time required for fulfilling records requests" under 610.026.1(1) (because segregation is required regardless of any request) and is not "staff time" under 610.026.1(2). Non-attorney staff time spent segregating/redacting is arguably chargeable as research/duplication time at actual cost, but no case squarely blesses charging for redaction itself — gray area; Gross's regardless-of-request rationale cuts against it.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Sec. 610.023.4 (verified): if a request is denied, the custodian must, upon request, provide a WRITTEN statement of the grounds for denial citing "the specific provision of law under which access is denied," furnished within 3 business days after the statement is requested. Closures under sec. 610.021 are discretionary ("may close"); sec. 610.011 requires exemptions be strictly construed. No Vaughn-index requirement. In litigation, once the requester shows the body is subject to the law and withheld/closed the record, the burden of persuasion shifts to the body (610.027.2); the requester bears the burden (preponderance) to prove a KNOWING or PURPOSEFUL violation for penalties/fees (Laut v. City of Arnold).

**Administrative appeal:** none — judicial only. No state FOI commission or public access counselor. The Missouri Attorney General accepts Sunshine Law complaints for review (ago.mo.gov complaint form) and may sue under 610.027, but AG review is discretionary, non-binding, has no deadlines, and the AG "cannot act as your attorney"; it is not an administrative appeal.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Sec. 610.026.1(1) (text verified as amended eff. 8/28/2025): paper copies not larger than 9x14 inches capped at 10 cents per page; hourly fee for duplicating time not to exceed the average hourly rate of pay for the body's clerical staff; research time chargeable at actual cost; the body "shall produce the copies using employees of the body that result in the lowest amount of charges for search, research, and duplication time." Requester may ask for a cost estimate before copies are produced. Sec. 610.026.1(2): computer records, disks, tapes, videotapes, films, and similar media: only the cost of copies, staff time (not exceeding the average hourly rate of pay for staff of the body required to make copies), and the cost of the medium, including programming where required; maps/blueprints/plats requiring trained personnel may be billed at the actual rate of compensation of such personnel. City practice example: Columbia charges $0.10/page after the first 10 free pages, $1 per CD, $2 per USB drive (free-page threshold is local practice, not statute).

**Labor charges:** Yes — search/research time is chargeable at ACTUAL COST with no free-hour threshold and no dollar cap, but the body must use the employees resulting in the lowest charges for search, research, and duplication time (610.026.1(1)). Duplication time capped at the average clerical hourly rate. Attorney review time is not chargeable (Gross v. Parson, Mo. banc 2021). No statutory free tier; some cities waive small amounts as practice.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Sec. 610.026.2(1) (verified): "Payment of fees may be requested prior to fulfilling the request" — i.e., 100% prepayment is permitted; there is no percentage cap and no deposit-vs-balance structure. Advance cost estimates available on request (610.026.1(1)). Gross v. Parson requires that even when prepayment is demanded, the 610.023.3 delay explanation and specific earliest-available date/time must still be given.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary only: 610.026.1(1) — documents "may be furnished without charge or at a reduced charge" when the body determines waiver/reduction is in the public interest because disclosure is likely to contribute significantly to public understanding of the operations or activities of the body and is not primarily in the requester's commercial interest (FOIA-style test; text verified). No indigency waiver. Waiver is discretionary and fact-dependent (Gross v. Parson vacated summary judgment on the waiver issue); no statutory duty to explain a waiver denial — explaining denials is recommended practice.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
HB 145 rule (eff. 8/28/2025, 610.026.2(2), text verified): a request is CONSIDERED WITHDRAWN if the requester fails to remit all fees within 90 days of the body's request for payment (150 days if requested fees exceed $1,000); the body "shall include notice" to the requester of this consequence. Six-month resubmission rule: if the same or a substantially similar request is made within six months after expiration and no fee was remitted (or no clarification response received), the body may require payment of the original request's fees plus any new fees. Transition rule: requests pending on 8/28/2025 were deemed withdrawn if all fees were not remitted by 1/1/2026. Requester must submit (and potentially re-pay for) a new request to revive.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No formal tolling scheme. Practical pauses: (1) prepayment demand (610.026.2(1)) — production waits for payment, with the 90/150-day withdrawal clock running against the requester; (2) clarification request — non-response for 90/150 days deems the request withdrawn (610.026.2(2), notice required); (3) the 610.023.3 delay notice (detailed cause + specific earliest date/time) lawfully extends production beyond 3 business days for reasonable cause. Whether a clarification request tolls the initial 3-day clock is unaddressed by statute.

## REMEDIES

Sec. 610.027 (verified): suit in circuit court (county of the body's principal place of business) by any aggrieved person, Missouri taxpayer or citizen, the AG, or a prosecuting attorney; injunctive/enforcement relief; once the threshold showing is made, burden of persuasion shifts to the body (610.027.2). KNOWING violation (requester proves by preponderance): civil penalty up to $1,000, plus DISCRETIONARY ("may order") costs and reasonable attorney fees (610.027.3). PURPOSEFUL violation: civil penalty up to $5,000, plus MANDATORY ("shall order") costs and reasonable attorney fees (610.027.4). Courts may void governmental action taken in violation (balancing test). Laut v. City of Arnold, 491 S.W.3d 191 (Mo. banc 2016): knowing = actual knowledge that the conduct violated the statute; purposeful = conscious design, intent, or plan to violate, with awareness of probable consequences (adopting Strake v. Robinwood West CID, 473 S.W.3d 642 (Mo. banc 2015)). Remedies are cumulative with other law; no criminal provisions in ch. 610.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for a Missouri city: (1) Identity fields optional by law — require only an email for delivery; name needed for invoicing; no residency gate. (2) No purpose question needed EXCEPT an optional fee-waiver module asking public-understanding/non-commercial justification (610.026.1(1)); no commercial-rate module. (3) Auto-acknowledgment satisfies nothing statutory by itself — build a 3-business-day SLA timer forcing a substantive action: produce, deny with citation, or send a delay notice that MUST contain a specific earliest date/time plus a detailed cause (Gross v. Parson); templated vague delay notices ('within 120 business days') are a litigation-exposure flag. (4) Fee engine: $0.10/page cap (paper to 9x14), clerical-rate duplication time, actual-cost research time using lowest-cost capable staff, never bill attorney review time; offer advance estimates. (5) Prepayment allowed at 100% (610.026.2(1)); every fee request MUST embed the statutory withdrawal notice, then start a 90-day payment clock (150 days if fees > $1,000) and auto-close as withdrawn on expiry (610.026.2(2)). (6) Same 90/150-day auto-withdrawal (with notice) for unanswered clarification requests — send clarifications within the initial 3 days. (7) Track closed requests for 6 months: a same/substantially-similar refiling can be billed the original fees plus new fees. (8) No admin appeal to route; denial letters must cite the specific 610.021 (or other) provision on request; log knowing/purposeful exposure ($1,000 discretionary fees / $5,000 mandatory fees).

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) Whether non-attorney staff time spent on redaction itself (vs. locating records) is chargeable 'research time' — no controlling case; Gross's rationale (segregation required regardless of request) cuts against charging, but the question is open. (2) Tolling effect of a clarification request on the initial 3-business-day clock — statute silent. (3) No-records handling rests on AG guidance/case law, not statutory text. (4) One practitioner summary (municipalfirm.com) adds an 'unless litigation is filed' qualifier to the 1/1/2026 transition rule that I did not see in the statutory text quoted from the revisor/Justia — treat the qualifier as unconfirmed. (5) Could not confirm whether the 2026 legislative session passed further Chapter 610 amendments (mopress.com session-wrap article was inaccessible); any 2026-passed changes would generally take effect Aug. 28, 2026, i.e., after this July 2026 snapshot, but should be re-checked before then. (6) Free-page thresholds (e.g., Columbia's first 10 pages free) and portal acknowledgment behavior vary city by city — practice, not statute. (7) Whether a body must explain a fee-waiver denial was left open in Gross. All statute texts (610.011, 610.023, 610.024, 610.026 (2025), 610.027, 610.028), the HB 145 effective date/content, and all four case citations (Gross 624 S.W.3d 877; Laut 491 S.W.3d 191; Strake 473 S.W.3d 642; Chasnoff 466 S.W.3d 571) were verified against revisor.mo.gov, Justia's 2025 code, senate.mo.gov, and primary case-law sources this session.
