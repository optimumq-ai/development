# Kansas City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Kansas city, under the Kansas Open Records Act (KORA) (K.S.A. 45-215 through 45-223 (plus 45-226 to 45-230; AG enforcement at 45-251 et seq.)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** KORA (K.S.A. 45-215 et seq.): act on request no later than end of 3rd business day — produce, deny citing specific law, or explain delay with earliest date. HB 2134 (eff. 7/1/2025): actual-cost fees only, lowest-cost staff at wage sans benefits, 5-hr/$200 cost-mitigation contact duty.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Not fully anonymous: K.S.A. 45-220(b) lets the agency require a written request containing the requester's name and address and the information necessary to ascertain the records (it may not demand more), and the custodian may require proof of identity. Purpose may NOT be demanded (Data Tree, LLC v. Meek, 279 Kan. 445, 109 P.3d 1226 (2005) — requester's purpose, including commercial purpose, is generally irrelevant to the right of access; AG guidance accords), EXCEPT the agency may require the K.S.A. 45-220(c) certification when records contain name/address lists. No residency or citizenship requirement — "all public records shall be open for inspection by any person" (K.S.A. 45-218(a), verified).

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** Kansas has no different deadline and no higher fee tier for commercial requesters, and no general commercial-purpose disclosure duty. The commercial branch is narrow: when requested records contain (or can be used to derive) a list of names and addresses, K.S.A. 45-220(c) allows the agency to require written certification that the requester will not use the list for the purpose of selling or offering for sale any property or service to persons listed, nor sell/give the list to others for that purpose. K.S.A. 45-230 makes knowingly selling, giving, or receiving such lists for commercial-solicitation purposes unlawful, with a civil penalty of up to $500 per violation in actions by the AG or county/district attorney (statutory exceptions exist, e.g., vehicle-division records, professional-licensing lists to trade organizations, voter data for campaigns, and other uses authorized by law). Cities implement this with a certification form (e.g., Wichita).

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory acknowledgment duty. The 3-business-day "act upon" duty in K.S.A. 45-218(d) functions as the acknowledgment deadline: by then the city must produce, deny, or send the detailed delay explanation with the earliest availability date. If the request was misdirected, K.S.A. 45-218(c) requires the recipient to notify the requester and supply the custodian's name and location if known. Cities using portals (e.g., Wichita's NextRequest) auto-acknowledge as a matter of practice, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
K.S.A. 45-218(d) (verified verbatim): each request "shall be acted upon as soon as possible, but not later than the end of the third business day following the date that the request is received." "Acted upon" does not require full production by day 3: the custodian must either (1) grant access/produce, (2) deny, or (3) if access is not granted immediately, give a detailed explanation of the cause for further delay and the place and earliest time and date that the record will be available. There is no fixed outer limit on production, only the reasonableness implicit in "earliest time and date." No formal extension mechanism and no statutory constructive-denial provision exists; failure to act within 3 business days is itself a KORA violation (enforceable via sworn AG complaint under K.S.A. 45-251/45-252 or suit under 45-222), and enforcement practice/RCFP commentary treat prolonged delay as denial. City practice (e.g., Wichita) states most records are produced within the 3 business days.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

K.S.A. 45-220(b): the request must provide information necessary to ascertain the records sought; the agency may require a written request "but shall not otherwise require a request to be made in any particular form" (verified verbatim) and may not return or delay a request because of a technicality if the desired records are ascertainable. Every Kansas public agency that maintains records — including cities — must designate a Local Freedom of Information Officer (K.S.A. 45-226, L. 2000 ch. 156) whose duties include providing educational materials, responding to KORA inquiries, and being available to resolve disputes — a statutory duty to assist. Since July 1, 2025, K.S.A. 45-219(e)(1) (2025 HB 2134, L. 2025 ch. 89) adds an interactive-communication duty for expensive requests (staff time exceeding 5 hours or estimated staff cost exceeding $200): the agency shall make reasonable efforts to contact the requester and engage in interactive communication about mitigating costs; no response by the end of the third business day deems the request withdrawn until the requester recontacts the agency (45-219(e)(2), verified verbatim). Custodian may refuse outright if a request imposes an unreasonable burden or repeated requests are intended to disrupt agency functions, but the refusal must be sustained by a preponderance of the evidence (K.S.A. 45-218(e)).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No duty to create a record that does not exist, to conduct research, to answer questions, or to compile new data (AG KORA FAQ, verified: KORA "does not require that a public agency do research for you, create a record it does not already possess, or write out their response to your questions"). The agency should respond within the 3-business-day window stating no responsive records exist; KORA itself imposes no retention duties (those come from separate records-retention statutes). Note Roe v. Phillips County Hospital, 522 P.3d 277 (Kan. 2023): producing an existing electronic record in electronic form is required — "the only accurate reproduction of an electronic file is a copy of the electronic file" — and is not creating a new record.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Mandatory segregability: K.S.A. 45-221(d) (verified verbatim) — if a record contains material not subject to disclosure, the agency "shall separate or delete such material and make available to the requester that material in the public record that is subject to disclosure" (unless the request itself targets information about identified individuals within an exempt category). Redaction labor is chargeable as staff time under K.S.A. 45-219(c) (AG FAQ, which predates the 2025 amendments, confirms staff/review time is part of actual cost); since July 1, 2025 it must be billed at the lowest-cost category of staff reasonably necessary, at salary/wage excluding benefits.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
K.S.A. 45-218(d): if access is denied, the custodian must provide, upon request, a written statement of the grounds for denial citing the specific provision of law relied on, furnished no later than the end of the third business day after the statement is requested (AG guidance: with enough specificity to show why the exemption applies). Most exemptions in K.S.A. 45-221(a) (56 numbered exemptions, verified; 2025 HB 2134 added new exemption (56) for obscene material as defined in K.S.A. 21-6401 and amended exemption (11) to permit withholding records of civil/administrative investigations that are formally closed with a determination that no violation occurred) are discretionary ("not required to disclose"). An unreasonable-burden/disruptive-repeat-request refusal is separately authorized by 45-218(e) and must be sustained by a preponderance of the evidence if challenged. No Vaughn-style index requirement.

**Administrative appeal:** No local administrative appeal body for city denials. Options: (1) complain to the city's Local Freedom of Information Officer (K.S.A. 45-226 — dispute-resolution duty, informal, no deadline); (2) file a KORA complaint with the Kansas Attorney General (or county/district attorney) under K.S.A. 45-251/45-252 — the complaint must be on the AG's prescribed form with facts attested under penalty of perjury (45-252, verified); the AG may investigate, enter consent orders (civil penalty up to $250/violation), or issue findings of violation (up to $500/violation, plus ordered compliance and AG-approved training), enforceable in district court; no statutory filing deadline for AG complaints was found. The fee-reasonableness appeal to the secretary of administration under K.S.A. 45-219(d) (verified verbatim: "Any person requesting records within the executive branch may appeal the reasonableness of the fees ... to the secretary of administration, whose decision shall be final") applies only to state executive branch records, not cities.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
K.S.A. 45-219(c) (as amended by 2025 HB 2134, L. 2025, ch. 89, § 1, eff. 7/1/2025 — all verified against the enrolled bill and current statute text): fees may not exceed the ACTUAL COST of furnishing the records, including the cost of staff time but excluding incidental costs unrelated to the request. The old provision deeming fees of $.25/page or less reasonable was STRICKEN in 2025 — there is now no statutory per-page rate; per-page charges must reflect actual copying cost. Computer/electronic access fees: limited to actual cost of computer services including staff time (45-219(c)). Staff time: must use the lowest-cost category of staff reasonably necessary, excluding costs of employee benefits (45-219(c)(3)); no free-hours threshold. Itemized statement of costs, including the hourly rates charged for each employee involved and an itemized list of other fees, must be provided on request (45-219(c)(4), verified verbatim). Cost-mitigation trigger: staff time exceeding 5 hours or estimated staff cost exceeding $200 requires reasonable efforts to contact the requester (45-219(e)). Overhead (rent, utilities) not chargeable (AG FAQ: fees "are not meant to pay for costs that would already be incurred"). Media charges (CDs, USB drives) are set by city fee schedules at actual cost; specific Wichita figures reported previously could not be re-verified.

**Labor charges:** Yes — staff time for search, review, and redaction is expressly chargeable (K.S.A. 45-219(c); AG FAQ). No free-hour threshold and no hourly cap, but since 7/1/2025: lowest-cost category of staff reasonably necessary, actual salary/wage only (no benefits), itemization with per-employee hourly rates on demand, and the 5-hour/$200 interactive-communication duty before running up large bills. A February 2020 AG enforcement finding (City of Frontenac) held "an hourly rate of $225.00 per hour for attorney time is per se unreasonable" (verified via RCFP).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Full advance payment may be required: K.S.A. 45-218(f) ("A public agency may charge and require advance payment of a fee for providing access to or furnishing copies of public records, subject to K.S.A. 45-219") and 45-219(a) ("If copies are requested, the public agency may require a written request and advance payment of the prescribed fee") — both verified verbatim. 100% prepayment of the estimate is permitted; there is no percentage cap like Michigan's 50%. AG FAQ (verified): if the actual end cost is less than the prepaid estimate, the agency must refund the difference. Prior unpaid balances are commonly cited by agencies as grounds to insist on prepayment (practice, not statute).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
None statutory. KORA contains no public-interest or indigency fee waiver — AG FAQ and RCFP (both verified): while federal FOIA provides a poverty exception, "the KORA does not have such an exception." Waivers or reductions are purely discretionary under individual agency/city fee policies; some cities waive small requests as a matter of practice.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Agency may withhold production until the required advance payment is made (45-218(f), 45-219(a)) — non-payment simply means no copies. Separately, under the 2025 amendments, if the agency makes reasonable efforts at the 5-hour/$200 cost-mitigation contact and the requester fails to respond by the end of the third business day, the request "will be deemed to be withdrawn until a subsequent contact has been made by the requester to the public agency" (45-219(e)(2), verified verbatim). Cities close unpaid/abandoned requests administratively; some condition future requests on settling unpaid balances (practice).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No express statutory tolling of the 3-business-day clock. Practical pauses recognized in the scheme: (1) the 45-218(d) delay notice converts the deadline into the stated "earliest time and date" of availability; (2) awaiting required advance payment suspends production (45-218(f), 45-219(a)); (3) the 45-219(e) cost-mitigation contact effectively stops the clock — no requester response by end of the third business day = deemed withdrawal until requester recontacts; (4) awaiting the 45-220(c) list-use certification or proof of identity the agency lawfully requires. Clarification requests pause processing as a matter of practice only.

## REMEDIES

K.S.A. 45-222 (verified): district courts have jurisdiction to enforce KORA by injunction, mandamus, declaratory judgment, or other appropriate order; matters are determined de novo, records may be reviewed in camera, and the burden of proof is on the public agency. Attorney fees: the court SHALL award costs and reasonable attorney fees to a prevailing plaintiff if the agency's denial was not in good faith and without a reasonable basis in fact or law; conversely, mandatory to a prevailing defendant if the plaintiff maintained the action not in good faith and without a reasonable basis (same standard applies in AG/county-attorney actions). Knowing violations or intentional failure to furnish information: civil penalty up to $500 per violation in actions brought by the AG or a county/district attorney (K.S.A. 45-223, verified verbatim; AG recoveries go to the open government fund). AG administrative enforcement under 45-251 (consent order penalty up to $250/violation, finding of violation up to $500/violation, plus court costs, investigative expenses, and fees on judicial enforcement). No damages remedy for requesters and no criminal penalties. Misuse of name/address lists: civil penalty up to $500 per violation (45-230).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for Kansas cities: (1) Identity fields: name and address required if the city requires a written request (45-220(b) — the agency may demand no more than name, address, and information to ascertain records); build optional proof-of-identity upload the city can toggle; NO purpose question — asking purpose is improper except the conditional module below. (2) Conditional certification module: if the request seeks name/address lists, present the 45-220(c) non-commercial-use certification checkbox/e-signature (misuse carries a civil penalty up to $500, 45-230). No commercial rate or deadline module — fees/deadlines are identical for commercial requesters. (3) Auto-acknowledgment: send immediately, but the system must force a substantive action by end of day 3 (business days, from receipt) — produce, deny, or send a delay notice that includes the cause AND the earliest date/time of availability; template that delay notice; on denial, generate the written statement citing the specific provision of law within 3 business days of any request for it. (4) Fee engine: actual-cost only; per-page rate must be cost-justified (no $.25 safe harbor since 7/1/2025); labor billed at lowest-cost capable employee's wage without benefits; generate itemized cost statements with per-employee hourly rates on demand (45-219(c)(4)). (5) Estimate/deposit logic: allow 100% prepayment demand with automatic overage refund; auto-trigger the cost-mitigation contact when the estimate exceeds 5 staff hours or $200 staff cost, start a 3-business-day response timer, and auto-mark the request deemed withdrawn (reopenable on requester recontact) on silence. (6) No fee-waiver question needed statutorily; offer an optional discretionary-waiver field. (7) Litigation-exposure flags: missed day-3 action, denial statement lacking a specific statutory citation, and fee line-items above actual cost (incl. attorney-rate labor billing — Frontenac 2020: $225/hr per se unreasonable) — each risks AG penalties ($250-$500/violation, 45-251) and mandatory attorney fees for denials not in good faith and without reasonable basis (45-222).

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) Post-2025 chargeability of redaction labor: the AG KORA FAQ (verified current as of this pass) still predates HB 2134 and has not been updated for the 2025 amendments; the amended 45-219(c) plainly allows staff time, so redaction time remains chargeable at the lowest-cost-employee rate, but no post-amendment AG guidance expressly confirms it. (2) No statutory constructive-denial rule exists; treating day-3 silence as denial rests on enforcement practice/RCFP commentary, not statute text. (3) City per-page and media rates post-repeal of the $.25 presumption vary and are often unpublished; the previously reported Wichita media prices (CD ~$1.15, USB ~$5) could not be re-verified this pass and should be treated as unconfirmed practice. (4) No statutory deadline for filing an AG complaint under 45-251/45-252 was found (none appears in the statute text). (5) The purpose-may-not-be-demanded rule rests on Data Tree and AG guidance rather than an express statutory prohibition; the AG FAQ page reviewed did not restate it directly. (6) The official Kansas Reports parallel citation for Roe v. Phillips County Hospital was not confirmed (P.3d cite 522 P.3d 277 verified via secondary source); Justia confirms No. 122,810, decided Jan. 6, 2023. (7) Whether a city may steer requesters to its own portal/form: statute bars requiring any particular form, but practice varies by city.
