# Washington City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Washington city, under the Public Records Act (PRA) (RCW ch. 42.56 (originally Initiative 276 (1972); recodified from RCW 42.17 in 2005)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** WA PRA (RCW 42.56): 5 business days to respond (produce, link, estimate, clarify, or deny); tiny copy fees, no search fees; court can award mandatory attorney fees + up to $100/day penalties.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Any person may request; no residency requirement and no requirement to identify oneself — anonymous requests are valid. RCW 42.56.080(2) (verified verbatim): "Agencies shall not distinguish among persons requesting records, and such persons shall not be required to provide information as to the purpose for the request except to establish whether inspection and copying would violate RCW 42.56.070(8) or 42.56.240(14), or other statute which exempts or prohibits disclosure of specific information or records to certain persons." Practical caveats: a requester who wants mailed copies, installment/fee notices, or the ability to sue and collect penalties needs to give contact information; and RCW 42.56.080(3) lets an agency deny automated "bot" requests (defined as requests the agency reasonably believes were automatically generated by a computer program or script) when one requester makes multiple bot requests within 24 hours and responding would cause excessive interference with other essential agency functions.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No commercial-rate fees and no different deadline for commercial requests. The single commercial provision is RCW 42.56.070(8): the PRA gives no authority to "give, sell or provide access to lists of individuals requested for commercial purposes," and agencies shall not do so unless specifically authorized or directed by law (proviso: lists of professional-license applicants and licensees are available to professional associations or educational organizations recognized by the licensing board, for a reasonable charge). To enforce it, RCW 42.56.080(2) lets the agency demand the purpose of a request that seeks a list of individuals; cities commonly require a signed non-commercial-use declaration for such requests (standard city-clerk practice reflected in AG model-rule commentary). The outcome is denial of the list, not a surcharge. Requests for records that are not lists of individuals cannot be conditioned on purpose at all.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate acknowledgment duty: the mandatory 5-business-day response under RCW 42.56.520(1) itself serves as the acknowledgment (options (c) and (d) are literally "acknowledging" receipt plus a time estimate or clarification request). City practice: portal/email auto-acknowledgments are common (e.g., Seattle Public Records Request Center) but the statutory obligation is the 5-day substantive response, not the auto-receipt.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
RCW 42.56.520(1) (last amended 2017): within 5 business days of receipt the city must do one or more of: (a) provide the record; (b) provide an internet address and link to the record on the agency website (with paper copy or terminal access if the requester cannot access the link); (c) acknowledge receipt and provide a reasonable estimate of the time needed to respond; (d) acknowledge receipt, ask the requester to clarify an unclear request, and give a reasonable estimate of time to respond if not clarified; or (e) deny the request. The deadline requires a DETERMINATION/response, not production — production may follow on the "reasonable estimate" timeline, which must be genuine and event-specific (WAC 44-14-04003(7); courts review estimate reasonableness under RCW 42.56.550(2)). RCW 42.56.520(2): additional time may be based on the need to clarify the request, to locate and assemble records, to notify third persons or agencies affected, or to determine whether records are exempt. There is no fixed outer production deadline. Constructive denial: failing to respond within 5 business days, or failing to produce by the estimate, is treated as a denial reviewable under RCW 42.56.550 (WAC 44-14-04004(4) lists 'fails to respond' among denials; one-year limitations period runs from the claim of exemption or last installment produced, RCW 42.56.550(6)). Counting: the day of receipt, Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays are not counted (WAC 44-14-040 model rule). Treating an email arriving after office hours as received the next business day is common CITY LOCAL-RULE practice (e.g., Seattle and many municipal codes) — it appears in neither RCW 42.56.520 nor WAC ch. 44-14.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

Request must be for "identifiable records"; a request for "all or substantially all" agency records is not valid, but a request for all records on a particular topic, keyword, or name is valid (RCW 42.56.080(1)(a)); an agency may not deny a request solely as overbroad (RCW 42.56.080(2)). Duty to assist: agencies must adopt rules providing "fullest assistance to inquirers and the most timely possible action on requests" (RCW 42.56.100). For unclear requests the city responds within 5 days seeking clarification while giving a time estimate (RCW 42.56.520(1)(d)); if the requester fails to clarify and the ENTIRE request is unclear, the agency need not respond further, but it MUST respond to any portions that are clear (RCW 42.56.520(3)). No duty to create a new record or answer questions (Bonamy v. City of Seattle, 92 Wn. App. 403 (1998)). Special rule: requests for school district board recordings must specify the date or range of dates (RCW 42.56.080(1)(b), added 2023 c 67).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No duty to create records; agency need only provide identifiable records existing when the request is received (Bonamy; WAC 44-14-04004 — no duty to go outside the agency's own records). If none exist, the proper response is a written statement that no responsive records exist, closing the request. The search must be adequate — reasonably calculated to uncover responsive records, searching the places they are reasonably likely to be (Neighborhood Alliance of Spokane County v. Spokane County, 172 Wn.2d 702 (2011), adopting FOIA adequate-search standards); an inadequate search is itself a PRA violation.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Strong segregability duty: agencies must delete only the exempt portions and disclose the remainder (RCW 42.56.210(1)); categorical withholding of partially exempt records and silent withholding violate the Act (Progressive Animal Welfare Society v. UW, 125 Wn.2d 243 (1994)). Redaction labor is NOT chargeable: RCW 42.56.120(1) bars fees for locating records and preparing them for inspection/copying, and WAC 44-14-07001 confirms no "redaction fee" for staff time to search, review, and redact; only actual copying/transmission/delivery costs may be charged. (The customized service charge under RCW 42.56.120(3) covers genuine IT data-compilation work, not ordinary redaction.)

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denials (full or partial, including redactions) must be accompanied by a written statement of the specific reasons (RCW 42.56.520(4)). RCW 42.56.210(3): a response refusing in whole or part must include a statement of the specific exemption authorizing withholding and a brief explanation of how it applies to the record withheld — in practice a withholding/redaction log identifying each record and exemption (WAC 44-14-04004). Exemptions are narrowly construed and the agency bears the burden (RCW 42.56.030, .550(1)). The agency must provide a mechanism for prompt internal review of denials; review is deemed completed at the end of the second business day following the denial and constitutes final agency action for judicial review (RCW 42.56.520(4)). No statutory deadline forces denial by a fixed day beyond the 5-day response/estimate structure.

**Administrative appeal:** None external for cities — judicial only. Internal: the city must provide prompt review of denials, deemed complete at the end of the second business day following the denial, which is final agency action ripe for court (RCW 42.56.520(4)); many cities route this petition to the city attorney or clerk (local-rule practice; see WAC 44-14-080). Attorney General written-opinion review under RCW 42.56.530 applies by its terms only to STATE agency denials ("Whenever a state agency concludes that a public record is exempt..."), not cities; a requester or city may still informally consult the AG's Open Government Ombuds (non-binding).

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
RCW 42.56.120 (last amended 2017 c 304, EHB 1595): no fee for inspection or for locating records ((1)). For copies, either (A) actual costs per a statement of costs adopted after notice and public hearing (RCW 42.56.070(7); staff salaries and overhead excluded except as directly related to copying), or (B) statutory default rates for agencies that do not calculate actual costs (42.56.120(2)(b)): 15 cents/page for photocopies or printed copies of electronic records ((b)(i)); 10 cents/page for scanning paper into electronic format ((b)(ii)); 5 cents per each 4 electronic files or attachments uploaded to email, cloud, or storage device ((b)(iii)); 10 cents per gigabyte transmitted electronically ((b)(iv)); plus actual cost of digital storage media/device, container/envelope, and postage ((b)(v)). Charges may be combined ((2)(c)). Alternative flat fee up to $2 per request ((2)(d)); if the flat fee is charged for the first installment, no additional flat fee on later installments. No copying charges for records the agency routinely posts on its public website before the request ((2)(e)); requester can be referred to the link. Requester may ask for a summary of applicable charges before copies are made and may revise the request to lower costs ((2)(f)). Customized service charge ((3)): actual cost of IT expertise to prepare data compilations or provide customized electronic access services not used by the agency for other purposes — only with advance notice, estimate, and explanation; requester may amend to reduce it. City practice example: Seattle's Multidepartmental Admin. Rule MDAR 17-0001 fee schedule — $0.15/page paper, $0.10/page scan, $0.02 minimum for uploads under 1 GB and $0.09/GB above, $0.41/minute staff upload time, $1/disc — showing hearing-adopted actual-cost schedules can add labor-type line items the statutory defaults lack.

**Labor charges:** Staff time for searching, reviewing, and redacting is NOT chargeable at all — no free-hours threshold exists because the prohibition is total (RCW 42.56.120(1); WAC 44-14-07001: no charge for locating records or preparing them for inspection, and no "redaction fee"). Chargeable labor is narrow: actual cost of the physical act of copying/scanning/uploading if included in a duly adopted actual-cost schedule (e.g., Seattle's $0.41/minute upload charge), and the customized service charge for genuine IT data-compilation or customized-access work (RCW 42.56.120(3)) with mandatory advance notice and estimate.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
RCW 42.56.120(4): agency may require a deposit "in an amount not to exceed ten percent of the estimated cost" of providing copies for a request. It may also charge per installment as installments are produced and require payment before delivery of each installment. A customized service charge requires an advance estimate and explanation, and the requester may amend the request to reduce it (42.56.120(3)(b)).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency waiver. Purely discretionary: RCW 42.56.120(4) — "An agency may waive any charge assessed for a request pursuant to agency rules and regulations." Common city practice is to waive small amounts (e.g., charges under a de-minimis threshold or first N pages) via local rule; thresholds vary by city and are policy, not statute.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
If the requester fails to pay a required deposit, the agency need not begin copying; if an installment is not claimed, reviewed, or paid for, the agency is not obligated to fulfill the balance of the request and may close it (RCW 42.56.120(4)). Model-rule practice: notify the requester that records are available and that failure to inspect/copy or make other arrangements within 30 days of the notice closes the request (WAC 44-14-04004), requiring a new request to revive it. Closure for non-payment after proper notice is lawful final action.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No formal statutory tolling scheme; the mechanisms are: (1) clarification — the agency responds within 5 days seeking clarification, and if no clarification comes and the whole request is unclear, the obligation to respond ends (RCW 42.56.520(1)(d), (3)); (2) additional-time grounds extend the production estimate (clarification, locating/assembling, third-party notice under RCW 42.56.540, exemption determination — RCW 42.56.520(2)); (3) deposit/installment non-payment suspends and can end processing (RCW 42.56.120(4)); (4) revised estimates are permitted if explained (WAC 44-14-04003 practice). Third-party injunction actions under RCW 42.56.540 can judicially halt production of noticed records.

## REMEDIES

RCW 42.56.550: motion/show-cause procedure in superior court (county where the record is maintained); agency bears the burden of proving an exemption ((1)) and the reasonableness of time estimates ((2)); review is de novo, may be on affidavits, with in camera review allowed ((3)). Prevailing requester SHALL be awarded all costs including reasonable attorney fees (mandatory), and the court MAY (discretionary since 2011 c 273; no minimum) award a penalty of up to $100 per day for each day access was denied — amount set using the 16 aggravating/mitigating Yousoufian factors (Yousoufian v. Office of Ron Sims, 168 Wn.2d 444 (2010)); courts may assess per page or per record group. One-year statute of limitations from the agency's claim of exemption or the last production of a record on a partial/installment basis (RCW 42.56.550(6)). No criminal penalties for wrongful denial; incarcerated requesters get no penalty absent agency bad faith (RCW 42.56.565).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity/contact fields must be optional in principle (anonymous requests valid), but collect email for installment, clarification, and payment notices. No purpose question generally — show a purpose/commercial-use declaration ONLY when the request is flagged as a list of individuals (RCW 42.56.070(8) denial branch, not a rate change); no commercial-rate module needed. Auto-acknowledge but ensure a substantive response (production, link, estimate, clarification, or denial) goes out within 5 business days — build the counter to exclude the day of receipt, weekends, and holidays (WAC 44-14-040), and make after-hours-email-received-next-business-day a per-city configurable local-rule setting (it is practice, not statute). Fee engine: statutory defaults (15¢/10¢/5¢-per-4-files/10¢-per-GB plus media/postage) unless the city adopted an actual-cost schedule after notice and public hearing — make the schedule configurable per city; $2 flat-fee shortcut with no repeat on later installments; pre-copy charge-summary on request (42.56.120(2)(f)); customized-service-charge workflow requiring advance estimate + explanation + option to narrow. Deposit logic capped at 10% of estimate; per-installment payment gating; auto-closure letters for unpaid/unclaimed installments and unanswered clarifications (30-day timers per WAC 44-14-04004 practice). Fee-waiver question optional/discretionary only. Litigation-exposure flags: missed 5-day response, blown estimates, missing exemption logs/brief explanations, and inadequate-search indicators — mandatory attorney fees plus up to $100/day discretionary penalties make these high-cost; track the 1-year SOL from claim of exemption or last production.
