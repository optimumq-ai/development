# Oklahoma City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Oklahoma city, under the Oklahoma Open Records Act (51 O.S. §§ 24A.1–24A.40). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** No fixed deadline — only prompt, reasonable access (24A.5(6)); delay limited to prep/avoiding disruption; unreasonable delay = denial. New: AG Public Access Counselor review (24A.40, 5/29/25), 10-business-day pre-suit notice to city and AG, SB 535 specificity + $75 advance-payment rules (11/1/25).

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Any person may request records (policy of access for "all persons," 51 O.S. § 24A.2); no residency or citizenship requirement (confirmed — RCFP Oklahoma guide). No statement of purpose is required and purpose may not be demanded, EXCEPT information reasonably needed for the public body to comply with the Act — chiefly to determine whether the request is "solely for commercial purpose" so it can apply the § 24A.5(4) search fee (1999 OK AG 55; AG one-pager, updated July 2025: a written request may be required including contact information and information to determine commercial purpose). Since Nov. 1, 2025 (SB 535), a public body "may require a requestor to complete a records request form" (§ 24A.5(7)). Practical anonymity is possible for emailed requests (an email alias works), but refusing to answer a commercial-purpose question may cause the body to treat the request as commercial for fee purposes — and a requester whose request is made for a commercial purpose is barred from the AG's Public Access Counselor review (§ 24A.40).

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** Commercial purpose changes FEES and ADMIN-REVIEW eligibility, not deadlines. Under 51 O.S. § 24A.5(4) (numbering confirmed from the SB 535 enrolled text), a search fee (staff time to locate records) may be charged ONLY if the request "is solely for commercial purpose, or would clearly cause excessive disruption of the essential functions of the public body," and even then only "a reasonable fee to recover the direct cost of record search and copying." Publication or broadcast by news media for news purposes is expressly NOT a commercial purpose/resale (§ 24A.5(4) proviso). The body may require enough information (written request/form) to determine commercial purpose. In no case may a search fee be charged when release is in the public interest (news media, scholars, authors, taxpayers). No resale prohibition (except the § 24A.5(5) county-clerk tract-index restriction) and no longer deadline for commercial requests. NEW: commercial-purpose requesters may NOT file for Public Access Counselor review (§ 24A.40, eff. May 29, 2025), though a requester wrongly classified as commercial may seek review of that classification. City practice example (confirmed on okc.gov): OKC charges $0.30 per minute of search/copy staff time for commercial/excessively-disruptive requests.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty and no acknowledgment deadline — the Act imposes only the substantive "prompt, reasonable access" standard. Acknowledgment is city practice only: Oklahoma City routes requests through its Action Center/portal and Tulsa uses an online request portal; both auto-acknowledge on submission. An emailed request to a smaller city may get no formal acknowledgment. § 24A.5(8) requires each public body to designate persons authorized to release records, at least one of whom must be available during regular business hours.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
No fixed day count. 51 O.S. § 24A.5(6) requires a public body to "provide prompt, reasonable access to its records"; a delay "shall be limited solely to the time required for preparing the requested documents and the avoidance of excessive disruptions of the public body's essential functions." The statute itself (¶6, verbatim, confirmed from the SB 535 enrolled text) bars queue abuse: "In no event may production of a current request for records be unreasonably delayed until after completion of a prior records request that will take substantially longer than the current request," and posting the requested records on the Internet satisfies the prompt-access obligation. What must happen: actual production of the records (or denial); there is no separate determination or estimate deadline and no statutory extension mechanism (no fixed clock to extend). Constructive denial: unreasonable delay is treated as a denial; since May 29, 2025 a requester whose request was not handled promptly/reasonably may also seek Public Access Counselor review under 51 O.S. § 24A.40 (nonbinding AG advisement within 60 calendar days). Before suing under § 24A.17(B), the requester must give the public body AND the Attorney General written notice 10 business days before filing (added by HB 3779, Laws 2024, c. 116, eff. Nov. 1, 2024 — confirmed from current § 24A.17 text). SB 535 (Laws 2025, c. 404, § 2, eff. Nov. 1, 2025) added request-form/specificity/clarification procedures and an advance-payment rule to § 24A.5 but did NOT add any day-count response deadline (confirmed from the enrolled text).

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

Since Nov. 1, 2025 (SB 535, Laws 2025, c. 404, § 2, adding 51 O.S. § 24A.5(7) — verbatim from enrolled text), the statute addresses vague requests: a public body may require its records request form; "if a records request does not describe the requested records with reasonable specificity, a public body may ask the requestor to clarify the request." To have reasonable specificity a request shall (a) "specify a general time frame within which the requested records would have been created or transmitted," (b) "seek identifiable records, rather than general information without any qualifiers or other specifications," and (c) "include search terms that are sufficiently specific to assist the public body in identifying the requested records." Denial for lack of specificity is allowed only "if a public body has engaged with the requestor to seek the information needed... including providing the requestor with general topics or a specific list of records related to the request" and the request "is still not reasonably specific" — a codified duty to engage/assist before denying. Before SB 535 there was no statutory clarification process. No clock effect (no statutory clock to pause), but promptness practically runs from a workable request.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** Statute is silent on a required "no responsive records" response format; common practice is a written statement that no responsive records exist. No duty to create records: 51 O.S. § 24A.18 provides the Act imposes no additional recordkeeping requirements, and the AG's one-pager (updated July 2025) states flatly that "there is no duty to create a new record to fulfill a request" (citing 2001 OK AG 46 and 2009 OK AG 12); AG opinions also confirm no duty to compile lists or answer questions. Advisable city practice is a written no-records response so the requester can test it via § 24A.40 PAC review or in district court (burden of proof stays on the public body if a denial is challenged).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Mandatory segregability: "Any reasonably segregable portion of a record containing exempt material shall be provided after deletion of the exempt portions" (51 O.S. § 24A.5(3); paragraph number confirmed from the SB 535 enrolled text; § 24A.5(2) separately permits SSN redaction). Redaction labor is NOT separately chargeable for ordinary requests — fees are capped at the reasonable direct cost of copying (max 25 cents/page); staff time (which would include redaction/review time) is recoverable only under the § 24A.5(4) commercial-purpose/excessive-disruption search-fee provision, and never when release is in the public interest.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
No statutory requirement that a denial be in writing and no denial deadline, but the public body must identify and carries the burden of proving the applicable exemption (51 O.S. § 24A.2; Citizens Against Taxpayer Abuse v. City of Oklahoma City, 2003 OK 65 — burden on the body urging exemption; 2025 OK AG 9 — exemption must "clearly apply," and blanket denials are improper where redaction is feasible). Under § 24A.5(7), a specificity-based denial is allowed only after clarification engagement fails. Charging fees "for the purpose of discouraging requests" is itself a violation exposing officials to misdemeanor liability (§ 24A.17(A) / § 24A.5(4)). No Vaughn-index requirement. Unreasonable delay is treated as constructive denial supporting PAC review (file within 30 calendar days of denial, § 24A.40) or suit after the 10-business-day pre-suit notice (§ 24A.17).

**Administrative appeal:** YES since May 29, 2025 — Public Access Counselor Unit in the Attorney General's office, 51 O.S. § 24A.40 (HB 2163, Laws 2025, c. 335, § 1, emergency; enacted over the Governor's veto). Who may file: a person denied access, a person whose request was not handled promptly/reasonably, or a person contesting a commercial-purpose classification; commercial-purpose requesters themselves may NOT file, nor may complaints against the Legislature. Deadlines: complaint within 30 calendar days after the denial; the Counselor forwards it to the public body within 7 business days; the body must respond within 7 business days; the AG issues an advisement within 60 calendar days of receipt. The advisement is NOT binding — the body must either comply or respond, and a body that discloses in accordance with AG advice is immune from liability/penalties. Free to file (AG portal launched Aug. 2025; opengov@oag.ok.gov). If the requester sues on the same denial, the PAC takes no further action. Judicial enforcement (district court after 10-business-day notice) remains the only binding remedy.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
51 O.S. § 24A.5(4): copy fees limited to recovery of "the reasonable, direct costs of record copying," and "in no instance shall the record copying fee exceed twenty-five cents ($0.25) per page for records having the dimensions of eight and one-half (8 1/2) by fourteen (14) inches or smaller, or a maximum of One Dollar ($1.00) per copied page for a certified copy" (verbatim, confirmed). Search/labor fees: only for solely-commercial or excessively-disruptive requests (§ 24A.5(4)); no statutory rate — must be reasonable direct cost (OKC uses $0.30/minute, confirmed on okc.gov). NEW (SB 535, eff. Nov. 1, 2025): "A public body may require advance payment of the estimated fees authorized under this section when the estimated cost exceeds Seventy-five Dollars ($75.00) or if the requestor has outstanding fees from previous requests," and any excess advance payment "shall be returned to the requestor." Electronic records: no per-page fee for computer-readable data; chargeable at reasonable direct costs of media, processing and staff time (2005 OK AG 21); OKC charges $4.00 per disc plus $4.00 per quarter-hour handling/mailing plus postage (confirmed). A requester using personal copying equipment may not be charged copy fees (2006 OK AG 35). Fee schedules must be posted in writing at the body's principal office and with the county clerk (§ 24A.5(4)). Fees "shall not be used for the purpose of discouraging requests."

**Labor charges:** Staff time for search, review, and redaction is chargeable ONLY when the request is solely for a commercial purpose or would clearly cause excessive disruption of essential functions, and never when release is in the public interest (news media, scholars, authors, taxpayers seeking to evaluate government) — 51 O.S. § 24A.5(4). There is no free-hours threshold because the default is zero labor charges; when authorized, the charge is limited to the reasonable direct cost of record search and copying (no statutory hourly cap; OKC: $0.30/min = $18/hr, confirmed; state AG's own schedule runs $55–$85/hr for its records).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
STATUTORY since Nov. 1, 2025 (SB 535 amendment to 51 O.S. § 24A.5(4)): a public body "may require advance payment of the estimated fees authorized under this section when the estimated cost exceeds Seventy-five Dollars ($75.00) or if the requestor has outstanding fees from previous requests." No percentage cap — the full estimate may be demanded — but "any portion of an advance payment that exceeds the costs of responding to the request shall be returned to the requestor." Below $75 (and absent outstanding fees), advance payment remains a matter of local practice (e.g., OKC: "payment in advance... may be required" by the department director). A deposit/estimate inflated to discourage a request still violates the prohibitive-fee rule.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
The statutory waiver is the public-interest SEARCH-FEE prohibition: "In no case shall a search fee be charged when the release of records is in the public interest, including, but not limited to, release to the news media, scholars, authors and taxpayers seeking to determine whether those entrusted with the affairs of the government are honestly, faithfully, and competently performing their duties" (51 O.S. § 24A.5(4)); it can be lost if the request causes substantial disruption (McVarish v. New Horizons, 1995 OK CIV APP 145). There is NO statutory waiver of the 25-cent copy fee itself and no indigency waiver — any copy-fee waiver is purely discretionary city practice.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Since Nov. 1, 2025, non-payment has a statutory hook: outstanding fees from previous requests are express grounds for requiring advance payment of estimated fees on a new request (51 O.S. § 24A.5(4), as amended by SB 535). Otherwise the statute is silent; city practice is that records are withheld until fees are paid, and where advance payment is required, non-payment halts processing and the request is administratively closed/treated as abandoned after a set period (portal-dependent, varies by city).

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** There is no statutory clock to pause. Functionally, promptness is tolled by: (1) § 24A.5(7) clarification engagement when a request lacks reasonable specificity (eff. Nov. 1, 2025); (2) awaiting statutory advance payment where the estimate exceeds $75 or prior fees are outstanding (§ 24A.5(4), eff. Nov. 1, 2025), or discretionary prepayment below that; (3) legitimate document-preparation time and avoidance of excessive disruption — the only delays § 24A.5(6) permits. No extension-notice regime exists. Filing a § 24A.40 PAC complaint does not pause anything, but a lawsuit on the same denial ends PAC review.

## REMEDIES

51 O.S. § 24A.17: (A) willful violation by a public official is a misdemeanor — fine up to $500 and/or up to 1 year in county jail (amounts stated in the section, confirmed); charging prohibitive fees can qualify. (B) Civil suit in district court for declaratory and/or injunctive relief, limited to records requested and denied before filing (Lawson v. Curnutt, 2010 OK CIV APP 78); requester must give written notice of intent to sue to the public body/official AND the Attorney General 10 business days before filing (HB 3779, Laws 2024, c. 116, eff. Nov. 1, 2024). Attorney fees: a successful requester "shall be entitled to reasonable attorney fees" (mandatory); a prevailing public body recovers fees only if the court finds the suit clearly frivolous. No damages remedy; public bodies are not civilly liable for damages for providing access, and good-faith reliance on AG advice under § 24A.40 confers immunity from penalties. Burden of proof on the public body (Citizens Against Taxpayer Abuse, 2003 OK 65). The AG/district attorneys may also prosecute violations, and the § 24A.40 PAC process provides free (nonbinding) enforcement pressure.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for an Oklahoma city: (1) Identity fields optional — statute allows anonymous requests; collect an email for delivery only, but a records request form MAY now be required (§ 24A.5(7)). (2) Purpose question must be narrow: a single commercial-purpose screen ("Is this request solely for a commercial purpose?") is defensible; a general purpose demand is not; warn requesters that a commercial answer forfeits AG Public Access Counselor review. (3) Commercial-rate module: search/staff-time billing (e.g., OKC's $0.30/min) unlocks ONLY on solely-commercial or excessive-disruption flags, and must auto-disable for news-media/scholar/author/taxpayer public-interest requests. (4) Fee engine: $0.25/page cap (≤8.5x14), $1.00/page certified, direct-cost e-media charges; display the posted fee schedule (statute requires posting at principal office and county clerk filing). (5) Deposit logic (post-11/1/2025): auto-offer advance payment of the full estimate when the estimate exceeds $75 or the requester has outstanding fees from prior requests; refund any excess; flag inflated estimates for prohibitive-fee (misdemeanor) risk. (6) Fee-waiver question: only a public-interest search-fee exemption checkbox; no indigency module needed. (7) Auto-acknowledgment: not required by statute but standard practice (OKC, Tulsa) and reduces constructive-denial and PAC-complaint exposure. (8) Clarification workflow (post-11/1/2025): prompt requester for time frame, identifiable records, and search terms; log the engagement (including topics/record lists offered) before any specificity denial. (9) Litigation/PAC-exposure flags: aging requests (unreasonable delay = constructive denial and PAC-reviewable), incoming § 24A.40 PAC complaints (7 business days to respond in writing), incoming 10-business-day intent-to-sue notices (also copied to the AG), and any fee configured above statutory caps. (10) Admin-appeal module now warranted: track PAC complaint windows (30 days from denial), the city's 7-business-day response, the AG's 60-day advisement, and document compliance with AG advice (which confers immunity).
