# Minnesota City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Minnesota city, under the Minnesota Government Data Practices Act (MGDPA) (Minn. Stat. ch. 13 (esp. secs. 13.03, 13.04, 13.08, 13.085); Minn. R. ch. 1205). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** No fixed clock for public requests: comply in an 'appropriate and prompt manner,' copies 'as soon as reasonably possible' (13.03). Hard 10-business-day clock only for data subjects (13.04). No deemed denial; unreasonable delay = de facto denial per IPAD; one late response can violate (Webster).

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Requesters of public data may be anonymous: IPAD's requester guidance states 'You do not need to identify yourself when requesting public data,' and Minn. R. 1205.0300 requires access for 'any person, without regard to the nature of that person's interest in the data' — no purpose may be demanded for public data. Contact info is practically needed for email/copy delivery. Identity verification IS required for data-subject (private data) requests under sec. 13.04 and Minn. R. 1205.0400/1205.1300. No residency requirement — 'any person' may request; the Act does not limit access to Minnesotans (confirmed in statute, rule, and IPAD guidance).

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No commercial-requester branch: the entity may not demand disclosure of purpose, and deadlines/copy fees do not change based on commercial intent. The only commercial provision is data-based, not requester-based: sec. 13.03 subd. 3(d) (confirmed current) allows a 'reasonable fee' in addition to copy costs when the data itself has commercial value and is a substantial and discrete portion of (or an entire) formula, pattern, compilation, program, device, method, technique, process, database, or system developed with a significant expenditure of public funds; the fee must relate to the actual development costs, and the entity must be able to justify it.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty and no acknowledgment deadline for public-data requests (confirmed: nothing in 13.03 or 13.025 requires acknowledgment). Sec. 13.03 subd. 2(a) only requires internal procedures for prompt compliance, and sec. 13.025 requires a written data-access policy updated by August 1 each year and made easily available to the public. Acknowledging email requests within a few days is common city practice (portal auto-replies are widespread) but purely practice-based.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
NO fixed day count for requests from the general public. Sec. 13.03 subd. 2(a) requires the responsible authority to establish procedures to 'insure' that requests are 'received and complied with in an appropriate and prompt manner'; subd. 3(c) requires copies 'as soon as reasonably possible' if they cannot be provided immediately (both confirmed against the current revisor text, July 2026). Webster v. Hennepin County, 910 N.W.2d 420 (Minn. 2018) (A16-0736, Apr. 18, 2018) affirmed an ALJ finding that a county's procedures failed to 'insure' prompt compliance — a single untimely response can violate subd. 2(a); the holding that the MGDPA contains no 'unduly burdensome' exception is from the court of appeals decision, 891 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. Ct. App. 2017), which the supreme court left undisturbed for lack of appellate jurisdiction. IPAD advisory opinions treat unreasonable delay as noncompliance/de facto denial, but there is no statutory extension mechanism and no deemed-denial rule. DIFFERENT rule for data subjects: sec. 13.04 subd. 3 requires compliance 'immediately, if possible,' otherwise within ten days of the request excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays (i.e., 10 business days). NOTE: 2025 Minn. Laws ch. 35, art. 9, sec. 1 amended 13.03 subd. 3 (adding the subd. 3(g) pickup/suspension rule) but created no response deadline. Cities commonly self-impose targets (e.g., 10-14 business days) — practice, not statute.

---

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

No statutory clarification procedure or explicit duty-to-assist clause; requests must be for existing government data (no duty to answer questions or create new data — sec. 13.03; IPAD guidance). IPAD advises entities to seek clarification of unclear requests rather than ignore them; because the deadline is a reasonableness standard, clarification effectively pauses the clock in practice. Entities cannot refuse a request as 'burdensome' — Webster v. Hennepin County, 891 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. Ct. App. 2017) (no unduly-burdensome exception; left undisturbed by the supreme court). Entity data-access policies (required by sec. 13.025) typically tell requesters to describe data with reasonable specificity — practice, not statute.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No statutory text on no-records responses, but IPAD advisory-opinion practice requires the entity to inform the requester that the data do not exist (a request only obligates production of existing data). No duty to create records, compile new data, or answer questions (sec. 13.03 subd. 3; IPAD guidance). Written 'no responsive data' replies are standard city practice.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Strong segregability duty: public data commingled with not-public data must be separated and the public portions provided. Redaction labor is explicitly NOT chargeable — sec. 13.03 subd. 3(c) (confirmed current): the authority 'may not charge for separating public from not public data.' IPAD copy-cost guidance repeats: 'Government may not charge any fee for redaction or separation of public data from not public data' — on both the 25-cent and actual-cost tracks.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Sec. 13.03 subd. 3(f) (confirmed current): if access is denied, the authority must inform the requester orally at the time of the request or in writing as soon after as possible, AND must cite the specific statutory section, temporary classification, or specific federal law relied on. Upon the requester's further demand, the authority must certify the denial in writing with the citation. No fixed denial deadline; no statutory balancing test for public data (presumption of publicness, sec. 13.01 subd. 3 / 13.03 subd. 1); no Vaughn-style index requirement.

**Administrative appeal:** No binding administrative appeal. Two non-judicial routes (both confirmed current): (1) Commissioner of Administration advisory opinion, sec. 13.072 — a person who disagrees with an entity's determination may seek one; commissioner must issue within 50 days of receipt or decline within 5 business days; no fee in the current statute; opinion is not binding but must be given deference by courts, and entity conformity with an opinion shields it from compensatory/exemplary damages, attorney fees, and penalties under 13.08/13.085/13.09. (2) Data-practices complaint to the Office of Administrative Hearings, sec. 13.085 — file within 2 years of the act or failure to act (or within 1 year of discovery if concealment/misrepresentation prevented discovery); $1,000 filing fee or bond; ALJ preliminary (probable-cause) determination within 20 business days after the response is filed or due; hearing within 30 business days after notice of hearing; ALJ may order compliance, impose a civil penalty up to $300, and award a prevailing complainant attorney fees up to $5,000 plus OAH costs up to $1,000.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Inspection: free, always (subd. 3(a)-(b) — out of scope here but anchors the copies rule). Copies, sec. 13.03 subd. 3(c) (confirmed current, July 2026): (1) 100 or fewer pages of black-and-white letter/legal paper copies — actual costs may not be used; max $0.25 per page copied (IPAD guidance treats a two-sided copy as 50 cents — the two-sided figure is guidance, not statutory text); flat rate, no add-ons for mailing or staff time (IPAD). (2) All other copies (over 100 pages, color, oversized, photos, electronic) — 'actual costs' of searching for and retrieving the data, including employee time, and of making, certifying, and electronically transmitting copies; NEVER for separating public from not-public data. (3) Electronic data kept in a computer storage medium: entity must provide an electronic copy if it can reasonably make one, and may charge actual cost (subd. 3(e)). (4) Commercial-value data developed with significant public funds: additional development-cost-based fee (subd. 3(d)). Data subjects: only actual costs of making and certifying copies — no search/retrieval charge (sec. 13.04 subd. 3; IPAD). City practice examples (e.g., Minneapolis: ~$30/hour staff time, fees collected only when total exceeds ~$30; small media charges) — practice figures, unverified this pass and varying by city.

**Labor charges:** Yes, for copy requests on the actual-cost track (over 100 pages or non-standard copies): employee time to search for, retrieve, copy, and transmit is chargeable (sec. 13.03 subd. 3(c); Demers v. City of Minneapolis, 468 N.W.2d 71 (Minn. 1991)). IPAD actual-cost guidance (confirmed): employee time must be calculated at the wages/salary (may include benefits) of the lowest-paid entity employee who could complete the task; no charge for redaction/separation time, no labor charge at all within the 25-cent flat-rate track, and no search/retrieval charge to data subjects. No statutory free-hours threshold; some cities add a de minimis collection threshold by policy.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Statute is silent on deposits; IPAD guidance and entity policies under sec. 13.025 permit requiring payment of the actual/estimated cost BEFORE making or providing copies (prepayment of the full estimate; characterization from IPAD practice — current IPAD pages fetched this pass do not expressly address prepayment). No percentage cap (no 50%-style rule). Requester should receive a cost breakdown/justification on request (IPAD practice). Practice varies by city — many bill under a threshold, larger requests require prepayment.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency fee waiver anywhere in ch. 13 (confirmed — the fee provisions are permissive maximums: 'may charge'). Waivers are wholly discretionary by entity policy. Practice: some cities do not charge media/government requesters and collect nothing below a de minimis threshold (e.g., Minneapolis ~$30) — practice, not statute, unverified this pass.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Entity may withhold copies until payment (prepayment permitted). NEW in 2025 — sec. 13.03 subd. 3(g), added by 2025 Minn. Laws ch. 35, art. 9, sec. 1 (not present in the 2024 statutes): if the requester does not inspect or retrieve the data within five business days of being notified they are available, the responsible authority 'may suspend any further response' to the request. Some cities' policies require payment of outstanding balances before processing new requests — practice, not statute.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No formal clock, so no formal tolling. In practice the reasonable-time standard flexes for: clarification exchanges, awaiting prepayment of the cost estimate, and volume/complexity (but burden alone does not excuse compliance — Webster litigation). The 10-business-day data-subject clock in sec. 13.04 subd. 3 has no statutory extension. The 5-business-day pickup rule (13.03 subd. 3(g), added 2025) lets the entity suspend response on stale requests.

## REMEDIES

Sec. 13.08 (confirmed current; last amended 2016): (subd. 1) damages plus costs and reasonable attorney fees for violations; willful violations carry exemplary damages of not less than $1,000 nor more than $15,000 per violation; (subd. 2) injunctive relief; (subd. 4) district-court action to compel compliance, with costs and attorney fees as determined by the court, and a civil penalty up to $1,000 against the entity payable to the state general fund; attorney fees are MANDATORY ('shall award') to a prevailing plaintiff where the entity received a directly related written sec. 13.072 advisory opinion and failed to follow it. Sec. 13.09 (confirmed): willful violation is a misdemeanor and constitutes just cause for suspension without pay or dismissal of the public employee.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields: make name optional and never required for public-data requests (only a delivery/contact channel) — anonymity confirmed by IPAD guidance and Minn. R. 1205.0300; add an identity-verification path ONLY for data-subject (about-me) requests, which also flips the deadline to a hard 10-business-day clock (13.04 subd. 3). Purpose question: do NOT require purpose; if asked at all, mark clearly optional. Commercial-rate module: none — no commercial-requester branch (only the rare 13.03 subd. 3(d) data-with-commercial-value fee tied to development costs). Fee logic: two tracks — flat $0.25/page for ≤100 B&W letter/legal pages (no add-ons; IPAD treats two-sided as 50 cents) vs. actual-cost (staff search/retrieval/copy/transmit time chargeable at the lowest-paid-capable-employee rate; NEVER charge redaction/separation time; electronic copies at actual cost). Fee-waiver question: no statutory waiver — model discretionary/de-minimis thresholds (e.g., bill only over ~$30, a common city practice). Deposit logic: prepayment of the estimate allowed, no percentage cap; NEW 2025 rule — entity may suspend response if requester doesn't retrieve/pay within 5 business days of the availability notice (13.03 subd. 3(g)), so build an auto-close timer keyed to that notice. Auto-acknowledgment: not legally required but standard practice — safe to send. Deadline engine: no fixed public-request clock; track 'reasonable time' with an internal SLA (10-14 business days is common city practice) and escalate aging requests as litigation-exposure flags (unreasonable delay = de facto denial per IPAD; a single untimely response can violate 13.03 subd. 2(a) per Webster; no burden-based refusals). Denials must auto-insert the specific statutory citation and offer written certification.
