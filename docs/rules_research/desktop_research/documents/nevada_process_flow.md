# Nevada City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Nevada city, under the Nevada Public Records Act (NPRA) (NRS Chapter 239 (NRS 239.001 et seq.)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** NV NPRA (NRS 239): act by end of 5th business day — produce, refer, earliest-date notice, or written denial citing authority. Fees = actual cost of copies only (labor charges abolished 2019); no admin appeal — district court with docket priority and mandatory attorney fees.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Requester may be anonymous as far as the statute is concerned: NRS 239.0107 accepts written OR oral requests from 'a person' and nowhere requires name, ID, or signature (contact info is practically needed for email delivery). Purpose may not be demanded: the Nevada Public Records Act Manual (Nev. State Library, Archives & Public Records, 2020) states, verified verbatim, that 'the requester is not required to provide a reason for seeking a record, and the agency cannot deny a request based on the requester's refusal to provide a reason' (asking a purpose to help narrow a broad request is permitted); NRS 239.001 requires liberal construction in favor of access. No residency requirement — RCFP's Nevada guide confirms any person may request regardless of status, purpose, or residence. Cities' portal forms typically ask for name/email (practice), but statute does not condition access on identity.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None. NRS Chapter 239 contains no commercial-purpose provisions: no duty to disclose a commercial purpose, no different deadline, no higher fee tier, and no resale restrictions for records obtained under the NPRA (confirmed by RCFP Nevada guide: fee and access provisions apply uniformly to all requesters). Specific record types outside the NPRA (e.g., DMV records under NRS 481.063) have their own use-based rules, but a general city records request has a single track for all requesters.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate statutory acknowledgment duty and no acknowledgment deadline — the 5-business-day substantive action under NRS 239.0107 is itself the required response. Auto-acknowledgment is common CITY PRACTICE via portals (e.g., City of Reno's GovQA 'Public Records Center'; Henderson's portal), not a statutory requirement.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Not later than the end of the FIFTH BUSINESS DAY after the date the person with legal custody or control receives a written or oral request (email qualifies), the city must do one of four things (NRS 239.0107(1), verified against official NRS text): (a) allow inspection/copying or provide the requested copy; (b) if it does not have legal custody or control, give WRITTEN notice of that fact plus the NAME AND ADDRESS of the governmental entity that does have the record, if known (239.0107(1)(b)); (c) if it cannot make the record available by day 5, give WRITTEN notice of that fact stating the EARLIEST DATE AND TIME after which it reasonably believes the record will be available — and if that date passes unmet, provide a further WRITTEN explanation of the reason plus a new date/time (a rolling-notice mechanism with no statutory outer limit) (239.0107(1)(c)(1)); or (d) deny in writing with a citation to the specific statute or other legal authority making the record confidential (239.0107(1)(d)). Records that are 'readily available' must be provided 'as expeditiously as practicable' — the entity may not sit on the full 5 days (NRS 239.0107(2)). No express deemed-denial clause, but NRS 239.011(1) makes a request that is 'denied or unreasonably delayed' (or subject to an 'excessive or improper' fee) immediately actionable in district court, so blowing day 5 or serial earliest-date extensions functions as constructive denial. Amendment history confirmed from the current (2025) codification: Added 2007; A 2013, 321; 2019, 4006 (SB 287) — no 2021-2025 amendment.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

Statutory duty to assist, with confirmed placement: within the NRS 239.0107(1)(c) earliest-date-notice path, the entity shall 'make a reasonable effort to assist the requester to focus the request in such a manner as to maximize the likelihood the requester will be able to inspect, copy or receive a copy of the public book or record as expeditiously as possible' (NRS 239.0107(1)(c)(2), added by SB 287 (2019)). There is no statutory provision pausing the 5-day clock for clarification; the compliant path for an unclear or voluminous request is the paragraph (c) written notice stating the earliest date/time of availability, combined with the assist duty. The 2020 NPRA Manual tells records officials to help narrow/clarify (including by asking — but not requiring an answer about — the purpose) rather than reject for vagueness.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** If the city does not have legal custody or control, it must, within the same 5 business days, provide WRITTEN notice of that fact and the name and address of the governmental entity that does have the record, if known (NRS 239.0107(1)(b)). 'No responsive records exist' written responses are handled the same way in practice. No duty to create a new record or compile new data — the Act covers existing public books and records (LVMPD v. Blackjack Bonding requires producing existing records, including data in electronic systems, but not creating new ones).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Strong segregability duty: a city may NOT deny a request on the basis that the record contains confidential information if it 'can redact, delete, conceal or separate, including, without limitation, electronically,' the confidential portion; the remainder must be produced (NRS 239.010(3), verified verbatim; electronic-redaction language added by SB 287 (2019)). Redaction LABOR IS NOT CHARGEABLE: 'actual cost' (NRS 239.005) is limited to direct costs (ink, toner, paper, media, postage) and expressly excludes any cost the entity incurs regardless of the request — i.e., staff time for search, review, and redaction — and the old extraordinary-use fee statute (NRS 239.055) was repealed by SB 287 (ch. 612, Stats. 2019, p. 4008). Police body-worn-camera records are governed by NRS 289.830 (per-incident requests; on-site inspection where confidential content cannot be redacted); that statute contains NO separate fee authorization, and agencies' hourly video-redaction fees (e.g., LVMPD) have drawn litigation as excessive/improper under NRS 239.011.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing, issued by the 5-business-day deadline, and must include 'a citation to the specific statute or other legal authority' that makes the record, or a part thereof, confidential (NRS 239.0107(1)(d), verified verbatim). Blanket or unexplained withholding is barred: exemptions are construed narrowly (NRS 239.001), the entity bears the burden of proving confidentiality by a preponderance of the evidence (NRS 239.0113, subject to the NRS 239.0115 exception for records confidential for a period of time), and for non-statutory confidentiality claims Reno Newspapers v. Gibbons, 127 Nev. 873, 266 P.3d 623 (2011) requires the entity to show, with particularity (balancing test), that nondisclosure interests clearly outweigh the strong presumption of access. No formal Vaughn-index requirement in the statute, but Gibbons requires more than conclusory citation.

**Administrative appeal:** None — judicial only. Nevada has no public access counselor, records ombudsman, or FOI commission with jurisdiction over city denials, and the NPRA makes no provision for administrative review (confirmed by RCFP Nevada guide); the Attorney General has no NPRA enforcement/appeal role over local governments (the 2020 State Library/Archives manual is executive-branch guidance). The sole remedy is an application to district court under NRS 239.011. Informal escalation to the city clerk/city attorney is practice, not an appeal of right.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
No statutory per-page rate for cities. Rule: fee must not exceed the ACTUAL COST to provide the copy unless another specific statute sets a fee (NRS 239.052(1)); 'actual cost' means the direct cost incurred in providing the record — 'ink, toner, paper, media and postage' — and excludes any cost incurred regardless of whether a copy is requested (NRS 239.005, verified verbatim; narrowed by SB 287 (2019)). Electronic records: if requested, the entity shall provide the copy in electronic format by electronic medium (NRS 239.010(4)), with narrow exceptions (record neither created nor available in electronic format; production would expose proprietary software or unredactable confidential information); an emailed PDF should therefore cost at or near $0. Search/review/staff-time charges: prohibited (excluded from actual cost; extraordinary-use fee statute NRS 239.055 REPEALED by SB 287, eff. 10/1/2019 — repeal verified). Statutory numbers that do exist: 50 cents/page cap for records in the custody of a government-operated LAW LIBRARY (NRS 239.052(4), verified); posted-fee transcripts of administrative proceedings (NRS 239.053); separate authorized GIS fee (NRS 239.054). A fee list must be prepared, maintained, and posted or referenced by conspicuous notice (NRS 239.052(3)). NRS 239.052 last amended 2013 (2025 codification) — the 2019 overhaul ran through NRS 239.005/239.055 instead. CITY PRACTICE varies: the Nevada Open Government Coalition's statewide fee audit found some local agencies still charging staff-time and repealed extraordinary-use fees years after 2019.

**Labor charges:** Not chargeable, with no threshold needed: since SB 287 (2019), staff time for determining whether a record is public, searching, retrieving, reviewing, and redacting is categorically excluded from 'actual cost' (NRS 239.005; 2020 NPRA Manual: no charge for 'regular staff time for complying with a public records request'; NAC 239.860-239.869 fee regulations for executive-branch agencies likewise authorize only reproduction/postage charges). The former extraordinary-use-of-personnel/technology surcharge (NRS 239.055) was repealed effective 10/1/2019 (ch. 612, Stats. 2019, p. 4008). Practice caveat: some city/county fee schedules continued to list labor/research fees after repeal — charging them is an 'excessive or improper fee' challengeable under NRS 239.011.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
NRS 239 itself is silent on deposits — no authorization, prohibition, or percentage cap in the statute. But NAC 239.864 (State Library/Archives regulation binding executive-branch state agencies, persuasive for cities) authorizes a written fee estimate when estimated cost exceeds $25, a DEPOSIT of up to the estimated actual cost, and payment of all fees before the copy is released; RCFP's Nevada guide reports the same deposit-up-to-estimated-actual-cost practice. Because labor is not chargeable, a lawful deposit for a city request is limited to estimated reproduction/media/postage costs; a deposit demand based on staff hours is improper under NRS 239.005/239.052.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary only — no statutory public-interest or indigency waiver right. NRS 239.052(2) (verified): a governmental entity MAY waive all or part of its fee, but only if it (a) adopts a written waiver policy and (b) posts, in a conspicuous place in each office, a legible sign or notice stating the policy's terms; waivers must then be applied per the posted policy. Practically, because an emailed electronic copy has near-zero actual cost, many Nevada requests carry no fee at all.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
NRS 239 is silent. NAC 239.864 (executive branch) requires payment of all fees before the copy is provided, and cities follow the same copies-on-payment practice; unpaid or unclaimed requests are administratively closed after a stated period under the city's own policy — a practice matter, not an NRS 239 rule. Conversely, a requester who believes a demanded fee is excessive or improper need not pay and may go straight to district court under NRS 239.011(1).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No statutory tolling. The 5-business-day clock runs from the custodian's receipt and is not paused by clarification dialogue, fee estimates, or deposit demands. The only statutory relief valve is the NRS 239.0107(1)(c) written notice of the earliest date/time the record will reasonably be available — which extends production but not the day-5 duty to respond; if the promised date slips, a further written explanation and new date are required (239.0107(1)(c)(1)), and an unreasonably distant or repeatedly-slipped date is actionable as 'unreasonable delay' under NRS 239.011.

## REMEDIES

Application to the district court of the county where the record is located if the request is denied or unreasonably delayed, or if the fee is excessive or improper (NRS 239.011(1), verified; delay and fee grounds added by SB 287 (2019)); the court MUST give the matter priority over other civil matters not given priority by other statutes (NRS 239.011(2)); no statutory limitations period for filing. Burden is on the city to prove confidentiality by a preponderance (NRS 239.0113). Attorney fees: MANDATORY — a prevailing requester 'is entitled to recover' costs and reasonable attorney's fees (NRS 239.011(2)), and again on an appeal affirmed in whole or in part (NRS 239.011(3)). Civil penalties: if the court finds the entity WILLFULLY failed to comply, it imposes $1,000 (first violation in 10 years), $5,000 (second), $10,000 (third+), deposited in the State General Fund and accounted for separately to support the Division of State Library, Archives and Public Records in improving access — not paid to the requester (NRS 239.340, verified; added by SB 287 (2019)). Public officers/employees acting in good faith in disclosing or refusing to disclose, and their employers, are immune from DAMAGES liability (NRS 239.012, verified) — this does not bar the fee/cost award against the entity. Criminal: willful and unlawful removal, alteration, mutilation, destruction, concealment, or obliteration of a filed public record — by ANY person, not just officers — is a category C felony (NRS 239.310, verified); related offenses at NRS 239.300, 239.320, 239.330.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for a Nevada city: (1) Identity fields optional — statute permits oral/anonymous requests; require only a delivery email. (2) NO purpose question and NO commercial-use module — the NPRA has no purpose or commercial provisions; an optional 'anything to help us narrow this?' field is fine, but denial for refusing to give a reason is expressly barred by state guidance. (3) Auto-acknowledgment is safe, standard practice (Reno GovQA; Henderson portal), but the real compliance event is a substantive action by end of business day 5 — build a 5-business-day timer with four exit paths: produce / not-custodian referral with the holding entity's name and address / earliest-date-available written notice / written denial with specific statutory citation. If an earliest-date promise slips, the system must generate a further written explanation plus a new date (NRS 239.0107(1)(c)(1)). (4) 'Readily available' records should ship immediately, not at day 5. (5) Fee engine: actual cost only (media + postage; per-page paper at documented direct cost); hard-block staff-time, search, review, redaction, and 'extraordinary use' line items — NRS 239.055 was repealed in 2019 and NOGC audits show lingering illegal fees; charging them is directly actionable under NRS 239.011 and risks NRS 239.340 willfulness penalties plus mandatory attorney fees. Electronic delivery of native/PDF records should default to $0. (6) Deposit logic: prepayment/deposit up to ESTIMATED ACTUAL COST only (mirroring NAC 239.864); no percentage cap exists because labor is excluded entirely. (7) Fee-waiver question only if the city has adopted AND conspicuously posted a written waiver policy (NRS 239.052(2)); otherwise omit. (8) Litigation-exposure flags: missed day-5 action, repeated slips of the 'earliest date,' denials lacking a specific citation, any labor-based fee, and failure to redact-and-release rather than withhold whole documents.
