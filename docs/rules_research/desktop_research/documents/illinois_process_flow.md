# Illinois City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Illinois city, under the Illinois Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) (5 ILCS 140/1 et seq.). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** IL FOIA: respond in 5 business days (day after receipt = day 1); one 5-day extension with notice. Commercial: 21 working days. Recurrent requester: 21 business days. Silence = denial, forfeits fees and undue-burden defense. New 2026: request must be in email body; personhood check tolls clock.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
No residency requirement — records must be made available to "any person" (5 ILCS 140/3(a)); non-residents and out-of-state entities have full rights. A public body may not require a standard form and may not require the requester to specify the purpose of a request, EXCEPT to determine (i) whether records are requested for a commercial purpose or (ii) whether to grant a fee waiver (3(c), verified verbatim). It is a violation of the Act to knowingly obtain records for a commercial purpose without disclosing that purpose when asked (3.1(c)). Anonymity narrowed by P.A. 104-438 (eff. 1-1-2026), new 3(j): a public body with a reasonable belief that a request was NOT submitted by a person (anti-bot/AI provision) may, within 5 business days of receipt, require verification — oral or written — that the requester is an actual person; the clock tolls during verification and the request may be denied if verification is not provided within 30 days. The body may NOT require personal, private, or identifying information (such as a photo ID) as part of verification. Relatedly, the 2(b) definition of "person" now includes individuals acting as agents of entities. Pseudonymous requests were previously tolerated in practice; 3(j) now gives cities a tool to challenge suspected automated requests, but not to force identity disclosure.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** Extensive commercial regime, all verified against ILGA text. "Commercial purpose" defined at 5 ILCS 140/2(c-10) (use for sale, resale, solicitation or advertisement for sales or services); news-media and nonprofit/scientific/academic uses for news dissemination or academic/scientific/public research or education are excluded. Handling changes in three ways: (1) Deadline — 21 WORKING days (not 5 business days) to respond with (i) a time/fee estimate, (ii) a denial under Section 7, (iii) an unduly-burdensome declaration, or (iv) the records (3.1(a)). (2) Disclosure — requesters must disclose a commercial purpose when asked; knowing failure violates the Act (3.1(c)). (3) Fees — in addition to copy fees, up to $10 per hour for search and exemption-review personnel time after the first 8 hours free, plus the actual cost of retrieving records from a third-party off-site storage facility (6(f)). Commercial requesters may NOT file a PAC Request for Review except for the limited purpose of contesting whether the body properly categorized the request as commercial (9.5(a), verified).

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No general statutory acknowledgment duty — the 5-business-day substantive response IS the required act. Narrow statutory notice duties: (1) 3(c) as amended by P.A. 104-438 (eff. 1-1-2026) requires notice within 5 business days if an electronic request is improperly formatted (content in attachment/hyperlink rather than the body of the submission); (2) a body invoking the recurrent-requester (3.2) or voluminous (3.6) track must notify the requester within 5 business days of receipt; (3) a 3(e) extension requires notice within 5 business days of receipt with reasons and the response date. Auto-acknowledgment is common city portal practice (e.g., Chicago's portal confirms receipt), not a statutory mandate.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Standard: the public body shall promptly either comply with or deny the request within 5 business days after receipt (5 ILCS 140/3(d), verified against ILGA text); per AG/PAC guidance the day of receipt is not counted — day 1 is the next business day, and a request emailed on a weekend/holiday is treated as received the next business day. The deadline requires an actual response (production, denial, or partial denial), not merely a determination. Extension: one extension of not more than 5 business days from the original due date for the enumerated 3(e) reasons (records at other locations; substantial number of records; categorical search requiring extensive effort; not yet located; need for exemption review; undue operational burden; need to consult another body), with notice to the requester within 5 business days of receipt stating the reasons and the date a response will be made (3(e)); longer periods only by written agreement with the requester (3(e)). Special tracks: commercial-purpose requests — 21 working days (3.1(a)); recurrent requesters — 21 business days after receipt (3.2(a)); voluminous requests — separate Section 3.6 timeline (see vagueRequest). New for 2026 (P.A. 104-438, SB 243, signed 11-21-2025, eff. 1-1-2026, verified): electronic requests must appear in their entirety within the body of the electronic submission — bodies need not open attachments or hyperlinks — and the body must notify the requester of this requirement within 5 business days of receipt; if the requester does not resubmit a compliant request, the body need not respond further (3(c)). Also new 3(j): a body with a reasonable belief that a request was not submitted by a person may, within 5 business days of receipt, require the requester to verify orally or in writing that he or she is a person; the response deadline is tolled until verification, and the body may deny if no verification within 30 days. Constructive denial: failure to respond within the statutory period is a denial and exhausts administrative remedies (9(c)); a late-responding body forfeits the right to charge fees for copies thereafter provided and may not treat the request as unduly burdensome (3(d), verified verbatim).

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

Requests "calling for all records falling within a category" must be complied with unless compliance would be unduly burdensome and the burden outweighs the public interest (3(g), verified). Before denying as unduly burdensome, the body MUST first extend the requester an opportunity to confer in an attempt to reduce the request to manageable proportions — a statutory duty to assist/narrow; if it remains burdensome the body may deny in writing specifying the reasons (3(g)). Repeated requests from the same person for the same records already provided or properly denied are deemed unduly burdensome per se (3(g), verified). Voluminous-request track (2(h), 3.6, added by P.A. 98-1129; procedure verified against ILGA text): more than 5 individual requests for more than 5 different categories of records within 20 business days, or compilation exceeding 500 letter/legal pages (unless a single record exceeds 500 pages); news-media and nonprofit/scientific/academic requesters are excluded by the 2(h) definition. Body notifies within 5 business days of receipt; requester then has 10 business days from the body's response to amend to a non-voluminous request; the body must then respond by the earlier of 5 business days after the requester's response or 5 business days after the final day to respond, extendable up to 10 business days for 3(e) reasons or longer by written agreement; tiered electronic fees under 6(a-5) apply. A body that misses its deadline loses the unduly-burdensome defense (3(d)).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No express "no records" section; the Act imposes no duty to create new records or answer questions — it covers existing records only (2(c); PAC guidance). Standard practice per AG/PAC: respond in writing within 5 business days stating that a reasonable search located no responsive records. The PAC treats a no-records response as reviewable under 9.5, where the body must show the adequacy of its search. Directing requesters to records posted online can satisfy the Act (8.5), but the body must otherwise produce the record if the requester cannot reasonably access it electronically. P.A. 104-438 also excludes unsolicited "junk mail"/commercial solicitations the body never responded to from the definition of public records (2(c), 2(j)).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Strong segregability duty: when a record contains both exempt and non-exempt material, the body shall redact the exempt portions and make the remaining information available for inspection and copying (5 ILCS 140/8; see also 7(1)). Redaction/review labor is NOT chargeable for ordinary requests — Section 6(a) bars charges for search, retrieval, review or personnel time except the commercial-request hourly fee (6(f), $10/hr after 8 free hours) and voluminous-request tiers (6(a-5)). Records are presumed open and exemptions are construed narrowly, with the body bearing the burden of proving an exemption by clear and convincing evidence (1.2, 11(f), verified).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing and must state: the decision and reasons including a detailed factual basis, the names and titles of each person responsible for the denial, notice of the right to review by the Public Access Counselor (with the PAC's address and phone number), and the right to judicial review under Section 11 (9(a)). If an exemption is claimed, the notice must specify the Section 7 exemption and give a detailed factual basis plus citation to supporting legal authority (9(b)). The body must keep an indexed central file of all denial letters, by exemption and record type (9(b)). Failure to respond within the Section 3 periods is deemed a denial and exhausts administrative remedies (9(c)). Charging a fee inconsistent with Section 6 constitutes a denial of access for judicial-review purposes (6, verified). No balancing test in the denial letter itself, but 7(1)(c) privacy and 3(g) undue-burden denials embed balancing standards.

**Administrative appeal:** Yes — the Public Access Counselor (PAC) in the Attorney General's office (5 ILCS 140/9.5). A person whose request is denied (including constructive denial) may file a Request for Review with the PAC not later than 60 days after the date of the final denial; it must be in writing, signed by the requester, and include a copy of the request and any responses (9.5(a), verified). The PAC forwards to the public body within 7 business days; the body has 7 business days to answer and produce records for confidential review (9.5(c)-(d), verified as business days). The AG may issue a binding opinion within 60 days of receipt, extendable once by no more than 30 business days on written notice (9.5(f), verified), may mediate, or may resolve by non-binding determination (the most common outcome, with no statutory deadline). Binding opinions are reviewable under the Administrative Review Law (11.5); P.A. 104-438 adds express immunity for individual officers/employees for disclosures made pursuant to AG opinions. Limits: commercial-purpose requesters may only ask the PAC to review the commercial categorization (9.5(a)); voluminous requesters likewise may only contest the voluminous categorization (9.5(b-5), verified — note subsection, not (a)). PAC review is optional — requesters may go straight to circuit court.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Copies (5 ILCS 140/6(a), all figures verified against ILGA text): first 50 pages of black-and-white letter/legal copies FREE; thereafter a maximum of 15 cents per page (statutory cap — cities may charge less). Color or nonstandard-size copies: actual reproduction cost. Certification: shall not exceed $1. Electronic records: only the actual cost of the recording medium (disc/drive) — no charge for search, retrieval, review, or personnel time on ordinary requests; records must be furnished in the requested electronic format if feasible. Commercial requests (6(f)): plus up to $10/hour for search and exemption review after the first 8 hours free, plus actual cost of retrieval from third-party off-site storage. Voluminous requests, electronic data (6(a-5), verified): non-PDF — up to $20 (not more than 2MB), $40 (2-4MB), $100 (over 4MB); PDF — up to $20 (not more than 80MB), $40 (80-160MB), $100 (over 160MB). Some record types (e.g., traffic accident reports) are priced under separate statutes, not FOIA. A fee inconsistent with Section 6 constitutes a denial of access (6); a body that misses its deadline may not charge copy fees at all (3(d)).

**Labor charges:** Not chargeable for ordinary requests: Section 6(a) forbids charging for search, retrieval, review, or personnel time. Two exceptions: (1) commercial-purpose requests — up to $10/hour for searching and exemption review, with the first 8 hours free (6(f), verified); (2) voluminous requests — flat tiered fees for electronic data under 6(a-5) rather than hourly labor. No programming-charge provision; furnishing records in a requested electronic format is required if feasible (6(a), 8.5) at medium cost only.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
No statutory deposit or prepayment scheme and no percentage cap — the statute is silent. For commercial and recurrent-requester tracks the required response includes an estimate of time and fees (3.1(a)(i), 3.2), and PAC guidance accepts requiring payment of properly computed copy fees before releasing copies; many cities require payment on or before pickup/mailing (practice, not statute). A demand for fees exceeding Section 6 constitutes a denial (6), so inflated "deposits" are challengeable via PAC review or suit.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Statutory public-interest waiver, mandatory in terms: documents shall be furnished without charge or at a reduced charge where the requester states a specific purpose and waiver/reduction is in the public interest — i.e., the principal purpose of the request is to access and disseminate information regarding the health, safety and welfare or the legal rights of the general public, and not for personal or commercial benefit (6(c), verified). No indigency waiver in the statute. First 50 b/w pages are free to everyone regardless (6(a)). Cities apply the 6(c) waiver case-by-case; refusal is reviewable as a fee dispute/denial.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute is silent on nonpayment. Practice: cities condition delivery of copies on payment of lawful fees and administratively close requests as abandoned if the requester does not pay or retrieve records (hold periods vary by city — practice, not statute). Some cities cite unpaid balances from prior requests to require prepayment on later requests — a practice not grounded in the statute and contestable. Charging beyond Section 6 rates converts the fee demand itself into a denial (6).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** (1) Personhood verification (P.A. 104-438, eff. 1-1-2026, 3(j), verified verbatim): the response deadline "shall be tolled until the requester verifies that he or she is a person"; denial permitted if no oral or written verification within 30 days after the body's request. (2) Written agreement: requester and body may agree in writing to extend beyond the 5+5 business days (3(e)); same for the voluminous track (3.6). (3) Unduly-burdensome conferral (3(g)) and the voluminous-request amendment window (3.6: 10 business days to amend, then response due the earlier of 5 business days after the requester's response or after the amendment window closes) redefine the response period. (4) Improperly formatted electronic requests (content in attachment/hyperlink): the body must notify of the defect within 5 business days, and if the requester does not resubmit compliantly the body need not respond further (3(c), P.A. 104-438). NOT a pause: a fee dispute does not toll anything, and a clarification inquiry outside 3(g) has no statutory tolling effect.

## REMEDIES

Circuit court suit for injunctive or declaratory relief (5 ILCS 140/11(a)); de novo review with in camera inspection available (11(e)-(f)); the public body bears the burden of proving an exemption by clear and convincing evidence (1.2, 11(f), verified). Attorney fees: MANDATORY — the court "shall award such person reasonable attorney's fees and costs" to a prevailing requester (11(i), verified; the degree to which relief obtained relates to relief sought informs the amount). Civil penalties: $2,500 to $5,000 for each occurrence where the body willfully and intentionally failed to comply or otherwise acted in bad faith (11(j)); Lucy Parsons Labs v. Chicago Police Dept., 2026 IL App (1st) 250205 (verified — citation and holding), holds that a willful and intentional violation alone triggers penalties, "or otherwise acted in bad faith" being a catch-all rather than an additional element, and reversed a penalty denial as against the manifest weight of the evidence. Additional penalty of up to $1,000 per day where the body fails to comply with a court order for more than 30 days without appeal, stay, or court-granted extension (11(j), verified). No criminal enforcement provision against public bodies (requester-side commercial nondisclosure is an Act violation under 3.1(c)).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Critical 2026 rule for portals/email intake: the request text must appear ENTIRELY in the body of the email/submission — attachments and hyperlinked request letters need not be opened, the city must flag the defect within 5 business days, and a never-corrected request requires no further response (3(c), P.A. 104-438); a portal should force request text into a body field. Identity fields: name/contact for delivery, but no purpose field may be required EXCEPT (a) a commercial-purpose question — answering yes routes to the 21-working-day track and enables the $10/hr-after-8-hours fee module (6(f)) — and (b) an optional fee-waiver purpose question keyed to 6(c) (health/safety/welfare/legal rights, non-commercial/personal). Personhood-verification module (3(j)): usable only on reasonable belief the request was not submitted by a person, must be triggered within 5 business days, tolls the clock, accepts oral or written verification, may NOT demand ID/personal info, and permits denial after 30 days of silence. Auto-acknowledgment is good practice but not statutory. Deadline engine: 5 business days starting the day after receipt (PAC counting rule), one 5-business-day extension with notice stating reasons and date, agreed extensions in writing; track recurrent-requester counters (50/12mo, 15/30d, 7/7d per requester per body) and voluminous triggers (>5 requests/>5 categories in 20 business days; >500 pages) with their notice/amendment sub-deadlines. Fee module: 50 free b/w pages then up to 15 cents; $1 certification cap; e-records at medium cost only; tiered voluminous e-fees (6(a-5)); NO labor charges outside commercial/voluminous; hard-stop warnings that any nonconforming fee is itself a denial and a missed deadline forfeits all copy fees and the undue-burden defense (6, 3(d)) — litigation-exposure flags, since attorney fees are mandatory (11(i)) and penalties run $2,500-$5,000 per occurrence with willful/intentional violation alone sufficing (Lucy Parsons Labs, 2026).

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) Prepayment/abandonment: the statute is silent; the claim that cities may condition copy delivery on payment and close requests for nonpayment rests on PAC guidance summaries and observed city practice — hold periods and closure practices vary by city and were not re-verified against any specific city's current policy this session. (2) The commercial-track '21 working days' term is not defined in the Act; it is treated in practice like business days but no authority construing it was located. (3) The 3(e) extension notice: the statute requires notice within 5 business days with reasons and date; whether the text itself says 'in writing' was not re-verified verbatim (written notice is universal practice and PAC expectation). (4) The P.A. 104-438 immunity provision (individual officers/employees immune for disclosures made pursuant to AG opinions) is confirmed from two law-firm summaries of the Act; the PAC's own newsletter did not describe it, and its exact scope (binding opinions only vs. all AG opinions) was not verified against session-law text. (5) Specific city fee schedules below the statutory caps (e.g., Naperville's lower b/w rate) were dropped as unverified this session; accident-report fees arise from separate statutes, not FOIA. (6) No amendment after P.A. 104-438 affecting municipal email requests surfaced in searches through July 2026, but spring-2026 session acts may not yet appear in secondary sources; ILGA's codified text as fetched already integrates the 1-1-2026 changes. (7) The PAC receipt-day counting rule (day of receipt excluded) is AG FAQ guidance, not statutory text.
