# Ohio City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Ohio city, under the Ohio Public Records Act (Sunshine Laws) (Ohio Rev. Code sec. 149.43 (current version eff. Sept. 30, 2025, HB 96, 136th G.A.; major amendments eff. Apr. 9, 2025 via HB 265, HB 315, SB 29, SB 109); enforcement also under R.C. 2743.75 (eff. Apr. 9, 2025)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** Ohio R.C. 149.43: NO fixed deadline — inspection 'promptly,' copies in a 'reasonable period.' Fees = actual cost, never labor (sole exception: police video, $75/hr of video, $750 cap). No admin appeal: 3-business-day cure notice, then $25 Court of Claims or mandamus ($100/day damages, $1,000 cap). Version eff. 9/30/2025.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Strongly protective. R.C. 149.43(B)(4) (verified): a public office may not "limit or condition the availability of public records by requiring disclosure of the requester's identity or the intended use," and may not require the request to be in writing. R.C. 149.43(B)(5): the office MAY ask for a written request, identity, or intended use only after disclosing that a written request is not mandatory, that the requester may decline to reveal identity/use, and that the information is sought only when it would enhance the office's ability to identify, locate, or deliver the records. Anonymous email requests are valid. No residency requirement — anyone may request. Exceptions: (1) incarcerated persons need a finding from their sentencing judge (or successor) that the information is "necessary to support what appears to be a justiciable claim" for records concerning their criminal case (R.C. 149.43(B)(8), verified); (2) since HB 265 (eff. 4/9/2025), declared vexatious litigators need leave of the common pleas court and a court order specifying the records before requesting, and an office with reasonable cause to believe an ANONYMOUS requester is a vexatious litigator may require acceptable identification (R.C. 2323.52(J)(1)-(2), verified via CCAO Bulletin 2025-04); the Supreme Court of Ohio maintains the vexatious-litigator list online.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** Narrow. R.C. 149.43(B)(7)(c)(i) (verified): the office may limit records it will PHYSICALLY deliver by U.S. mail or another delivery service to ten per month UNLESS the requester certifies in writing that the records (or the information in them) will not be used for commercial purposes; (B)(7)(c)(iii): "commercial" is narrowly construed and excludes reporting or gathering news, gathering information to assist citizen oversight or understanding of government, and nonprofit educational research. This certification mechanism does not apply to email/digital delivery or in-person pickup, and there are no different deadlines or higher fees for commercial requesters generally. (Separate regime: bureau of motor vehicles records requested in bulk for commercial purposes may bear a surcharge under a distinct division of 149.43 — inapplicable to typical city requests; not re-verified verbatim this pass.)

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty or deadline. The only timing command is "promptly"/"reasonable period" in (B)(1). Prompt acknowledgment is universal best practice (AG model policy and city policies acknowledge and confirm scope), and a timely response bears on later attorney-fee exposure under (C)(4)(b), but it is practice, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
NO fixed day count. R.C. 149.43(B)(1) (verified verbatim): records must be "promptly prepared and made available for inspection," and copies must be provided "within a reasonable period of time." What is "prompt"/"reasonable" is fact-specific (volume, need for redaction, legal review); AG guidance stresses overall office workload is not an excuse. For law-enforcement VIDEO records, (B)(1) as amended in 2025 expressly directs courts evaluating reasonableness to consider the time required to "retrieve, download, review, redact, seek legal advice regarding, and produce" the video. There is no extension mechanism because there is no base deadline. No deemed-denial rule as such, but unreasonable delay is treated as a denial actionable in mandamus or the Court of Claims, and attorney fees are available under (C)(4)(b) where the office failed to respond affirmatively or negatively within the time allowed or missed a promised production date. A city's adopted policy may NOT establish a fixed waiting period before responding unless that period is less than 8 hours, and may not cap the number of records available to one person or in a fixed period except as the statute itself authorizes (R.C. 149.43(E)(2), verified).

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

R.C. 149.43(B)(2) (verified): if a request is ambiguous or overly broad, or the office cannot reasonably identify what public records are being requested, the office MAY deny the request but must then provide the requester an opportunity to revise the request by informing the requester of the manner in which the office's records are maintained and accessed in the ordinary course of business. Case law (e.g., State ex rel. Zidonis v. Columbus State Community College, 2012-Ohio-6113; State ex rel. Glasgow v. Jones, 2008-Ohio-4788) upholds denial of "all records containing X"-type requests. Clarification effectively restarts the "reasonable period" for the revised request; the statute has no formal tolling language.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No duty to create records that do not exist or to compile new records from data (settled case law, e.g., State ex rel. White v. Goldsberry, 85 Ohio St.3d 153 (1999); State ex rel. Morgan v. New Lexington, 112 Ohio St.3d 33, 2006-Ohio-6365). If no responsive records exist, the proper response is a statement that none exist; because that operates as a denial, the (B)(3) explanation duty applies (written if the request was written). The office need only search where records are maintained in the ordinary course; the requester bears the burden in litigation to prove responsive records exist.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Full segregability duty: R.C. 149.43(B)(1) (verified) requires that where a record contains both exempt and non-exempt material, the office make available all non-exempt information and "notify the requester of any redaction or make the redaction plainly visible"; a redaction is deemed a denial of the redacted information (triggering the (B)(3) explanation duty) except where federal or state law authorizes or requires the redaction. Redaction LABOR IS NOT CHARGEABLE for ordinary records — "at cost" means actual material cost only (State ex rel. Warren Newspapers, Inc. v. Hutson, 70 Ohio St.3d 619 (1994)). Statutory exception (eff. 4/9/2025, HB 315; carried forward in the 9/30/2025 version): law-enforcement VIDEO records — the office may charge "actual cost" of reviewing, blurring/obscuring, redacting, uploading, or producing the video (including storage media and staff time), capped at $75 PER HOUR OF VIDEO PRODUCED and $750 total per request (R.C. 149.43(B)(1), verified verbatim).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
R.C. 149.43(B)(3) (verified verbatim): any denial, in whole or part, must be accompanied by "an explanation, including legal authority, setting forth why the request was denied"; if the initial request was in writing (email qualifies), the explanation must also be in writing. No fixed deadline for the denial beyond the general promptness standard. Exemptions are strictly construed against the custodian, with the burden on the office (e.g., State ex rel. Cincinnati Enquirer v. Jones-Kelley, 118 Ohio St.3d 81, 2008-Ohio-1770). No Vaughn-index requirement, though courts conduct in camera review in litigation. Note: (B)(3) expressly does NOT preclude the office from relying on ADDITIONAL reasons or legal authority in later litigation (verified — the draft's contrary statement was wrong).

**Administrative appeal:** None — no administrative appeal body (the AG publishes guidance but does not adjudicate; "self-help statute"). NEW mandatory pre-suit step (HB 265, eff. 4/9/2025, verified in current text): before filing ANY court action, the requester must transmit a written complaint to the public office, which then "has three business days to cure or otherwise address the failure alleged in the complaint" (R.C. 149.43(C)(1)); the requester may not file within that 3-business-day window and must file a written affirmation (per (C)(2)) that the complaint was properly transmitted at least three business days before filing and was not satisfactorily resolved — failure to file the affirmation requires dismissal. The quasi-administrative low-cost route is the Court of Claims special-master process under R.C. 2743.75 (all timelines verified): $25 filing fee, prescribed form with the request/response attached, immediate referral to mediation (unless the special master determines otherwise), office response and any motion to dismiss due 10 business days after mediation terminates (or notice of non-referral), special-master report within 7 business days of the response (one 7-business-day extension for good cause), objections within 7 business days of the report (response to objections within 7 more; final order within 7 after that), appeal to the court of appeals of the district where the office's principal place of business sits (2743.75(G)(1)). Filing in the Court of Claims bars mandamus on the same request and vice versa (149.43(C)(1)(a)-(b); 2743.75(C)(1)).

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Copies must be provided "at cost" — meaning ACTUAL cost of the medium only (paper, toner, CD), never employee time (Warren Newspapers v. Hutson, 70 Ohio St.3d 619 (1994)). No statutory per-page rate; 10 cents/page was accepted as a reasonable measure of actual cost in State ex rel. Zauderer v. Joseph, 62 Ohio App.3d 752, 577 N.E.2d 444 (10th Dist. 1989), and typical city schedules run about 5 cents/page B&W, 10 cents color, ~$1 per CD (city policy, e.g., Cleveland Heights — practice varies). Emailed/portal-delivered electronic copies are commonly free because marginal cost is zero. Postage and mailing supplies are chargeable and payable in advance (B)(7)). No search, retrieval, review, or programming charges permitted for ordinary records. Sole labor exception (verified): law-enforcement video preparation at actual cost capped at $75 per hour of video produced and $750 total per request; the office may require the ESTIMATED actual cost to be paid before processing, and if actual cost exceeds the estimate the office may charge the difference only up to 20% above the estimate and only if the requester was notified in advance of that possibility (R.C. 149.43(B)(1)). Crime victims (and their counsel with a signed retention agreement, per (B)(11)) cannot be charged video fees for video related to the act that caused the victim's harm. Inspection is always free.

**Labor charges:** Not chargeable, with no threshold to worry about: staff time for search, review, and redaction can never be billed for standard records — "cost" excludes labor entirely (Warren Newspapers v. Hutson, 1994). Only exception is the 2025 law-enforcement video provision: actual cost of reviewing/blurring/redacting/uploading/producing (expressly including staff time), capped at $75 per hour of video produced, $750 total per request.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Full prepayment allowed, no percentage cap: R.C. 149.43(B)(6) (verified) lets the office "require the requester to pay in advance the cost involved in providing the copy," and (B)(7) allows advance payment of postage/mailing supplies. State ex rel. Dehler v. Spatny, 127 Ohio St.3d 312, 2010-Ohio-5711, confirms the office may await advance payment before producing. For police video, the estimated preparation fee may be demanded before work begins, with any overage capped at 20% of the estimate (advance notice required). Since amounts are pennies per page, deposits matter mainly for large paper or video requests.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency fee waiver. Waivers are purely discretionary; in practice cities routinely waive trivial amounts (many policies deliver electronically free and only invoice paper/media). The one statutory waiver (verified, R.C. 149.43(B)(11)): a crime victim who reasonably asserts the video relates to the act or omission that caused the victim's harm — or the victim's legal counsel (with signed retention agreement) or insurer — cannot be charged the video-record fee.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute is silent on closure; because prepayment may be required (B)(6)), the office simply withholds copies until payment, and the request sits unfulfilled. Common city practice is to treat a request as abandoned/closed after a stated period of non-payment or non-response to a fee estimate (policy-based, not statutory). Withholding for non-prepayment is not actionable delay (Dehler, 2010-Ohio-5711).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** There is no statutory clock to pause. Practically, the "reasonable period" analysis accommodates: (1) awaiting revision of an ambiguous/overbroad request under (B)(2); (2) awaiting advance payment under (B)(6)-(7) (Dehler); (3) redaction and legal review time (expressly listed as reasonableness factors for video in (B)(1)). None of these is framed as tolling; each simply informs what a court later deems "prompt"/"reasonable."

## REMEDIES

(1) Mandamus in the court of common pleas, court of appeals, or Supreme Court of Ohio (R.C. 149.43(C)(1)(b)), after the 3-business-day cure notice. (2) Statutory damages (R.C. 149.43(C)(3), verified): $100 per business day of non-compliance "beginning with the day on which the requester files a mandamus action," capped at $1,000; injury conclusively presumed; available only if the written request was transmitted by hand delivery, electronic submission (email qualifies), or certified mail; reduced/denied if the office reasonably believed its conduct lawful under existing law; NOT available to persons committed to DRC/federal BOP custody or children committed to DYS (HB 265); NOT available in the Court of Claims forum. (3) Court costs: mandatory ("shall determine and award") for prevailing relators in mandamus (C)(4)(a)). (4) Attorney fees in mandamus: DISCRETIONARY ("may award," (C)(4)(b)) where the court orders compliance or finds the office failed to respond affirmatively or negatively within the time allowed, broke a promised production date, or acted in bad faith by releasing records only after suit; subject to a reasonable-belief limitation ((C)(4)(c)) and capped at fees incurred before the record was produced plus fees for proving fee reasonableness ((C)(5)(b)). Frivolous mandamus exposes the REQUESTER to the office's costs and fees ((C)(6)). Court of Claims route: recovery limited to the $25 filing fee and other costs (2743.75(F)(3)(b)); attorney fees only if the office appeals with intent to delay or harass (2743.75(G)(2)). Related: R.C. 149.351 civil forfeiture of $1,000 per violation (aggregate cap $10,000 plus limited fees, per 2016 amendments — cap not re-verified verbatim this pass) for unlawful destruction of records. No criminal penalties in 149.43 itself.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity and purpose fields MUST be optional, with the (B)(5) disclaimer (written request not mandatory; may decline to identify; info only used to enhance ability to identify, locate, or deliver records) — a mandatory login field is common in portals but the city must still accept anonymous email requests outside the portal. One narrow identity carve-out: if an anonymous request gives reasonable cause to believe the requester is a listed vexatious litigator, ID may be required (R.C. 2323.52(J)(2)) — build as an exception flag, not a default field. No purpose question needed; no commercial-rate module needed for digital delivery (the non-commercial certification only gates the 10-per-month U.S.-MAIL cap — build it into the mail-delivery branch only). No fee-waiver question needed (no statutory waiver), but include a crime-victim flag that zeroes video fees ((B)(11)). Auto-acknowledgment is safe and recommended but set no promised date the city cannot keep — a missed promised date is an attorney-fee trigger under (C)(4)(b). Fee module: per-page/media rates from the city's policy schedule (e.g., 5-10 cents/page), zero for email delivery, full prepayment invoice allowed (B)(6), video-prep fee estimator capped at $75 per hour OF VIDEO PRODUCED / $750 total, with mandatory advance notice if actual cost may run up to 20% over the estimate. Litigation-exposure flags: (1) every emailed written request qualifies for statutory damages ($100/business day from mandamus filing, $1,000 cap) — track age of open requests; (2) inbound written 'cure notice' complaints start a 3-BUSINESS-DAY window before suit (149.43(C)(1)) — immediate escalation queue; (3) denials must auto-attach legal-authority text when the request was written (B)(3).

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) Per-page rates are city practice, not statute — 5 cents B&W / $1 CD figures come from city policy (Cleveland Heights) and were not re-verified this pass; Zauderer's 10 cents/page is the case-law benchmark. (2) The BMV/motor-vehicle bulk commercial-records surcharge division of 149.43 was not re-verified verbatim (inapplicable to city portals). (3) The R.C. 149.351 forfeiture aggregate cap ($10,000, 2016 amendments) was not re-verified verbatim. Re-check before launch after mid-2026 budget/session activity. (5) Exact romanette-level subdivision letters within (C)(4)(b) and (B)(7)(c) were confirmed via structured fetches of codes.ohio.gov rather than a full line-by-line read. (6) Non-payment closure timelines are pure city practice with no statutory basis. Everything else in this file — no fixed deadline, (B)(1)-(B)(11) content, 8-hour policy limit in (E)(2), cure-notice mechanics, damages accrual/exclusions, fee/costs standards, video-fee caps and 20% overage rule, Court of Claims timelines, vexatious-litigator rules — was verified against the current statute text or the CCAO Bulletin 2025-04 in July 2026.
