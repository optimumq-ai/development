# New York City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a New York city, under the Freedom of Information Law (FOIL) (N.Y. Public Officers Law art. 6, secs. 84-90). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** NY FOIL: within 5 business days agency must grant, deny, or acknowledge with an approximate date (ordinarily within 20 business days); beyond that needs written reason + date certain. Hard-deadline bill S2520B vetoed Oct 9, 2025 — 'reasonable date' regime still governs (July 2026).

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
No residency requirement — FOIL rights extend to "all persons"; the requester's status or need is generally of no consequence (Matter of Data Tree, LLC v. Romaine, 9 N.Y.3d 454 (2007)). Agencies may not demand a reason or intended use, with one purpose-based exception: for LISTS OF NAMES AND ADDRESSES, the agency may require a written certification that the list will not be used (or passed to others for use) for solicitation or fund-raising purposes (sec. 89(3)(a)); the corresponding privacy exemption for sale/release of such lists if they would be so used is sec. 89(2)(b)(iii). Requests must be written but the statute does not require proof of identity; practically, a name/email is needed for delivery, and city portals collect contact info. Home addresses of present/former public officers, employees, and retirees of public retirement systems are shielded from disclosure (sec. 89(7)).

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** FOIL has NO general commercial-purpose branch: no commercial-purpose disclosure duty, no different deadline, and no higher fee schedule for commercial requesters — the sec. 87(1) fee caps apply to everyone (commercial requester prevailed in Data Tree). The single purpose-based rule: an agency may treat as an unwarranted invasion of privacy — and thus deny — LISTS OF NAMES AND ADDRESSES that would be used for solicitation or fund-raising (sec. 89(2)(b)(iii)), and may require a written no-solicitation certification (sec. 89(3)(a)); this affects disclosability of that one record type, not deadlines or fees. Otherwise commercial resale of FOIL records is unrestricted.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
Yes, statutory. If the agency cannot grant or deny within 5 business days, it MUST furnish a written acknowledgment within those 5 business days that includes a statement of the approximate date, reasonable under the circumstances, when the request will be granted or denied (POL sec. 89(3)(a)). Entities with reasonable means available must accept emailed requests and respond by email (sec. 89(3)(b)). Failure to acknowledge within 5 business days is itself a constructive denial (COOG time-limits guidance). City practice (e.g., NYC OpenRecords portal, NYC Office of the Actuary FOIL Policy 2026) is automatic written/portal acknowledgment with an estimated completion date.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
No fixed production deadline. Within 5 business days of receipt of a written (including emailed) request for records "reasonably described," the agency must do one of three things: (1) make the record available, (2) deny in writing, or (3) acknowledge receipt in writing with an approximate date — "reasonable under the circumstances" — when the request will be granted or denied, which ordinarily should fall within 20 business days of the acknowledgment (POL sec. 89(3)(a); COOG time-limits guidance). If disclosure within those 20 business days is not possible, the agency must state in writing the reason AND "a date certain within a reasonable period, depending on the circumstances," when the request will be granted in whole or in part. So the 5-day deadline requires a determination or acknowledgment, not production; production timing is governed by the reasonableness standard. Failure to respond in 5 business days, failure to grant/deny by the stated approximate/certain date, or giving an unreasonable date = constructive denial, immediately appealable under sec. 89(4)(a). VERIFIED: S2520B (2025), which would have imposed hard fulfillment deadlines (180 days for requests before Dec. 31, 2026; 90 days in 2027; 60 days from Jan. 1, 2028), was VETOED by Gov. Hochul on Oct. 9, 2025 (veto memo 29) — the open-ended "date certain" regime remains current law as of July 2026; no signed successor found.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

Records must be "reasonably described" (sec. 89(3)(a)); the test is whether the agency can locate the records with its filing/retrieval systems — descriptions need not be exact (Konigsberg v. Coughlin, 68 N.Y.2d 245 (1986)). Affirmative duty to assist by regulation: the records access officer must ensure staff "assist persons seeking records to identify the records sought, if necessary," indicate how records are filed, retrieved or generated, and "contact persons seeking records when a request is voluminous or when locating the records sought involves substantial effort" to narrow scope (21 NYCRR 1401.2(b), text verified). An agency may reject a request that is not reasonably described, but the 5-business-day response duty applies to telling the requester so; a clarified/amended request is generally treated as newly received, starting a fresh clock (practice/COOG guidance, not express statute). Volume alone is not a basis for denial where records are retrievable with reasonable effort (Data Tree).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** On request, the agency must certify that it does not have possession of the record or that the record cannot be found after diligent search (sec. 89(3)(a)). FOIL imposes no duty to create a record: "Nothing in this article shall be construed to require any entity to prepare any record not possessed or maintained" (sec. 89(3)(a)), but retrieving/extracting electronic data with reasonable effort — including programming — is NOT creating a new record (sec. 87; Data Tree). A "no records" response should issue within the same 5-business-day/acknowledged-date framework and is appealable.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Strong segregability duty: if a record contains both exempt and non-exempt material, the agency must redact the exempt portions and disclose the remainder; blanket exemptions for particular types of documents are inimical to FOIL's policy (Gould v. NYPD; reaffirmed by the Feb. 20, 2025 Court of Appeals decisions requiring individualized privacy assessment with redactions "when feasible without unreasonable difficulty"). Chargeability: per COOG advisory opinions (e.g., FOIL-AO-19261 (Mar. 26, 2015), text verified: "There is no authority in the law to charge for time spent reviewing records or redacting records prior to disclosure, whether they be paper or electronic"), redaction and search time are NOT chargeable; only "preparing a copy" of electronic records exceeding 2 hours is chargeable, and the 25-cent page rate may be charged for redacted photocopies. Some agencies dispute this in practice for electronic extractions — see uncertainties.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial must be in writing (sec. 89(3)(a)) within the statutory timeframe, and on administrative appeal the agency must "fully explain in writing" the reasons for further denial (sec. 89(4)(a)). Exemptions (sec. 87(2)) are permissive and narrowly construed; the agency bears the burden of proving each record falls squarely within an exemption (Capital Newspapers v. Burns, 67 N.Y.2d 562 (1986); Gould v. NYPD, 89 N.Y.2d 267 (1996); burden codified for litigation in sec. 89(4)(b)). No Vaughn-index requirement at the administrative stage; courts may require particularized justification and conduct in camera review in Article 78 litigation. Blanket/categorical denials are prohibited — agencies must give "a particularized and specific justification" per record, including for unsubstantiated police disciplinary complaints (Matter of NYCLU v. City of Rochester, N.Y. Court of Appeals, Feb. 20, 2025). Failure to respond on time = constructive denial, appealable.

**Administrative appeal:** Yes — internal, not to an external body. Appeal in writing within 30 DAYS of denial (express or constructive) to the head, chief executive, or governing body of the entity, or their designated appeals officer (sec. 89(4)(a)). The appeals officer has 10 BUSINESS DAYS from receipt to fully explain the denial in writing or provide the records; failure to decide within 10 business days is deemed a denial of the appeal, exhausting remedies. The agency must immediately forward the appeal (and later its determination) to the Committee on Open Government (COOG). COOG issues advisory opinions (persuasive, non-binding) but is not an adjudicative appeal body. The administrative appeal must be exhausted before suing.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
POL sec. 87(1)(b)(iii)(1): paper copies capped at 25 cents per photocopy not in excess of 9x14 inches; larger/other formats at actual cost of reproduction. Sec. 87(1)(c) (actual cost of reproducing other records, incl. electronic) may include ONLY: (i) an amount equal to the hourly salary of the lowest-paid agency employee with the necessary skill to prepare the copy — but NO fee unless at least 2 hours of agency employee time is needed; (ii) actual cost of storage devices/media provided; (iii) actual cost of an outside professional service to prepare the copy, only when the agency's IT equipment is inadequate. "Preparing a copy shall not include search time or administrative costs." The requester must be informed of the estimated cost when more than 2 hours of employee time or an outside service is needed. Free-repeat rule (sec. 87(1)(b)(iii)(2), added by L.2022 ch.745 with chapter amendment S2220 signed Mar. 3, 2023): where an identical record was prepared for a previous request within the past 6 months and an electronic copy is available, NO fee may be charged except actual cost of a storage device/media if one is provided. Records must be provided in the medium requested if the agency can reasonably do so. Inspection is free; emailing existing electronic records is effectively free. City practice: NYC agencies charge 25 cents/page for hard copies and typically deliver electronic records via the OpenRecords portal at no charge.

**Labor charges:** Search time: never chargeable, paper or electronic (sec. 87(1)(c): preparing a copy excludes "search time or administrative costs"). Review/redaction time: not chargeable per COOG advisory opinions (FOIL-AO-19261, verified). Copy-preparation time for ELECTRONIC records: chargeable only when preparation requires at least 2 hours of agency employee time, at the hourly salary of the lowest-paid employee capable of the task; under 2 hours is free. Outside-vendor/programming charges only when agency IT equipment is inadequate for the job. For paper copies, labor is not separately chargeable — the 25 cents/page cap is all-inclusive.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Statute is silent on deposits. Sec. 87(1)(c) requires the agency to inform the requester of the ESTIMATED cost of preparing a copy whenever more than 2 hours of employee time or an outside professional service is needed. COOG advisory opinions permit agencies to require payment of the (properly computed) fee in advance of reproduction; there is no statutory percentage cap or deposit formula (contrast Michigan's 50%). City practice: NYC agency FOIL policies (e.g., Office of the Actuary FOIL Policy 2026) state the agency may require the copying/reproduction fee to be paid in advance.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency fee waiver. The sec. 87(1) fees are ceilings ("shall not exceed"), so agencies retain discretion to charge less or nothing; many cities waive fees for small or electronic productions as a matter of practice (NYC OpenRecords deliveries are typically free). The only statutory free-copy entitlement is the 6-month identical-record rule in sec. 87(1)(b)(iii)(2).

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
No statutory provision. Practice: agencies condition release of copies on payment (advance payment permitted per COOG opinions) and administratively close requests when estimated fees go unpaid after notice; some agencies treat unpaid balances as grounds to require prepayment on future requests. None of this is codified — closure/abandonment timelines vary by city and should be stated in the agency's own FOIL rules (required by sec. 87(1)(b) and 21 NYCRR Part 1401).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No statutory tolling scheme. Practical pauses: (1) a determination that the request is not "reasonably described" (communicated within the 5-day window) stops processing until the requester clarifies — the clarified request is generally treated as newly received (practice/COOG guidance); (2) where advance payment is properly demanded, production awaits payment; (3) the sec. 89(3)(a) mechanism itself is the extension tool — the agency extends its own time by stating a reason and a "date certain" beyond the 20-business-day window, but an unreasonable date is a constructive denial. COOG advisory guidance treats emailed requests received outside business hours as received the next business day, which sets the clock start (advisory-opinion level, not statute).

## REMEDIES

CPLR Article 78 proceeding in Supreme Court after exhausting the administrative appeal (4-month statute of limitations, CPLR 217(1), verified: "within four months after the determination to be reviewed becomes final and binding"); the agency bears the burden of proving an exemption applies (sec. 89(4)(b)); courts may review in camera. An agency's APPEAL from a court order requiring disclosure must be given calendar preference and perfected on an expedited basis (sec. 89(4)(d)(ii)) — there is no general trial-level calendar preference for FOIL Article 78 proceedings. Attorney fees, sec. 89(4)(c) (2017 amendment, A.2750-A/S.2392-A, signed Dec. 13, 2017): the court MAY award reasonable attorney's fees and litigation costs when the requester substantially prevailed and the agency failed to respond to the request or appeal within the statutory time; the court SHALL award them when the requester substantially prevailed and the agency "had no reasonable basis for denying access." No civil penalties against agencies. Criminal: willfully concealing or destroying a record with intent to prevent public inspection is a violation (sec. 89(8)).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields: collect name + email for delivery only — do not require ID, and never ask "purpose" EXCEPT auto-insert the no-solicitation certification checkbox when a request seeks a list of names and addresses (certification authority sec. 89(3)(a); related privacy exemption sec. 89(2)(b)(iii)). No residency gate. No commercial-rate module needed (commercialBranch=false). No fee-waiver question needed (no statutory waiver; optionally note agency discretion). Auto-acknowledgment is load-bearing: the portal must generate a written acknowledgment WITH an approximate grant/deny date within 5 business days, then track the 20-business-day marker and any "date certain" extension (each extension needs a stated reason and a specific reasonable date). Deadline-miss flags = constructive denial: missed 5-day response, missed approximate/certain date, or unreasonable date — each triggers the requester's 30-day appeal window and litigation exposure, including MANDATORY attorney fees if a denial lacked reasonable basis and discretionary fees for untimeliness alone (sec. 89(4)(c)). Fee module: 25 cents/page hard copy; electronic = actual cost with a 2-hour free labor threshold at lowest-paid-capable rate; block search/review/redaction charges; auto-check whether an identical record was produced in the last 6 months with an electronic copy available (must be free except media cost); generate cost estimate + advance-payment request when prep exceeds 2 hours or outside services are needed. Appeal routing: to agency-head/appeals officer with a 10-business-day decision timer and auto-copy to COOG. Delivery: honor requested format (email/electronic) when reasonably possible.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) Redaction-labor chargeability for electronic records: COOG advisory opinions (FOIL-AO-19261, verified this pass) say review/redaction time is never chargeable, but some agencies fold redaction into chargeable '>2 hour preparation' of electronic copies; appellate law is not fully settled — COOG's view treated as the rule. (2) Deposits/non-payment: advance-payment authority rests on COOG opinions and agency policies, not statute; closure-after-nonpayment timelines are pure city practice and vary. (3) Clock effect of a clarification request (restart vs. toll) and next-business-day receipt of after-hours emails are practice/COOG advisory guidance, not express statute; the specific advisory-opinion citation for after-hours receipt was not re-verified this pass. The nysenate.gov sec. 87 revision dated May 29, 2026 appears to reflect sunset/repeal dates of traffic-camera-image exemption provisions (per FindLaw amendment notes), not fee or deadline changes — fee text was verified unchanged in the current version. (5) S2520B veto confirmed from the Senate bill page (vetoed Oct. 9, 2025, veto memo 29); no signed successor found as of this pass, but a 2026-session re-passage could still be awaiting gubernatorial action — re-check before launch.
