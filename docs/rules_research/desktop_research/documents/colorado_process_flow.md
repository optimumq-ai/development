# Colorado City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Colorado city, under the Colorado Open Records Act (CORA) (C.R.S. § 24-72-200.1 et seq. (Title 24, Art. 72, Part 2; core operative sections §§ 24-72-201 to 24-72-205; former penalty § 24-72-206 repealed 2017)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** CORA: produce records in 3 working days (presumed reasonable); +7 working days max via written extenuating-circumstances finding — never for a single identified document. No admin appeal; district court after 14-day notice; costs and attorney fees mandatory for prevailing requester.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
All public records are open for inspection "by any person" (C.R.S. § 24-72-203(1)(a)) — no Colorado residency requirement. Purpose may not be demanded and is legally irrelevant under CORA for ordinary records. Since SB23-286 (eff. Aug. 7, 2023), § 24-72-203(1)(a) expressly provides that a custodian "shall not require a requester to provide the custodian with any form of identification," with a narrow exception for records restricted by law to a person in interest (verified in current statute text). Practical anonymity is limited only by needing a delivery email/address. Contrast: the separate Colorado Criminal Justice Records Act requires a signed statement that records of official actions will not be used for direct solicitation of business for pecuniary gain (§ 24-72-305.5) — relevant to city police-records paths but outside core CORA.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No commercial-purpose branch in current CORA: no required disclosure of commercial purpose, no different deadline, and no separate commercial fee tier for ordinary records. Bills to create tiered treatment all failed: HB24-1296 (2024, died); SB25-077 (2025) would have created separate timelines for media, general, and "financial gain" requesters — VETOED by Gov. Polis (verified objections: custodians would have "far too much power to define who is and is not a member of the media," and identical requests would "get access... on different timelines"; override abandoned); SB26-107 (2026, similar) postponed indefinitely Mar. 5, 2026. Two adjacent provisions: (1) for computer output other than word processing (databases/GIS), § 24-72-205(4) lets custodians recover actual incremental costs plus a reasonable portion of system build/maintenance costs, and authorizes fee REDUCTIONS/WAIVERS when the use is a public purpose (agency program support, nonprofit activities, journalism, academic research), uniformly applied — so commercial data users effectively pay more; (2) criminal-justice records carry the § 24-72-305.5 anti-solicitation affirmation.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty. The only obligations inside the 3-day window are production, the § 24-72-203(3)(a) date-and-hour-certain notice (records not readily available), the § 24-72-205(1)(b) copy-available/payment notice, and the written extenuating-circumstances finding under § 203(3)(b). Auto-acknowledgment is common city PRACTICE via portals (e.g., Boulder, Fort Collins request portals confirm receipt), not a statutory requirement. Some agencies by policy treat requests received after a cutoff or on closure days as received the next working day (practice, not statute).

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Records must be made available within a "reasonable time"; a reasonable time is presumed to be THREE WORKING DAYS or less (C.R.S. § 24-72-203(3)(b), verified current through Fall 2025). Within that window the custodian must make the records available, or — if the records are in active use, in storage, or otherwise not readily available — forthwith notify the requester and set a date and hour certain when they will be available (§ 24-72-203(3)(a)). For COPY requests (the email use case), § 24-72-205(1)(b) adds a statutory production mechanic: within the § 203(3)(a) period the custodian must notify the requester that the copy is available but will be sent only once payment (or a payment arrangement) is received for transmission costs and all other lawful fees; after payment/arrangement, the custodian must send the record as soon as practicable and no more than 3 business days later. Extension: the 3-day period may be extended by up to SEVEN additional working days if "extenuating circumstances" exist, with a written finding of those circumstances provided to the requester (§ 24-72-203(3)(b); the statute requires the finding within the three-day period). Extenuating circumstances are statutorily defined: (I) a broadly stated request encompassing all or substantially all of a large category of records without sufficient specificity; (II) a broadly stated request arriving when the agency must devote all/substantially all resources to an impending deadline or peak-demand period (for legislative-branch entities, General Assembly in session); (III) volume so large that 3-day compliance would substantially interfere with other duties. By express command, extenuating circumstances can NEVER apply to a request for a single, specifically identified document (§ 24-72-203(3)(b), verified). No statutory deemed-denial provision; a blown deadline is treated as a denial enforced via the § 24-72-204(5) show-cause procedure after the required pre-suit notice. Reform bills to lengthen deadlines all failed: HB24-1296 (2024) died; SB25-077 (2025) — which would have created tiered timelines (journalists keeping ~3 days, other requesters longer, "financial gain" requesters longest) — was VETOED by Gov. Polis in April 2025 (veto letter dated Apr. 17-18, 2025; override attempt abandoned May 2, 2025); SB26-107 (2026, similar tiered/three-week approach) was postponed indefinitely by the Senate State, Veterans, & Military Affairs Committee on March 5, 2026 (3-2). The 3-day rule remains current law as of July 2026.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

No express statutory duty to assist or to seek clarification. The statute's lever for vague requests is the extension: a request "broadly stated" so as to encompass all or substantially all of a large category of records without sufficient specificity is an "extenuating circumstance" supporting up to 7 extra working days with a written finding (§ 24-72-203(3)(b), verified) — but never for a single, specifically identified document. City PRACTICE is to email for clarification and treat the clarified request as restarting the 3-day clock; the statute does not expressly authorize that tolling. No duty to create a record that does not exist or to answer questions (Colorado case law no-duty-to-create doctrine), though digital records must be provided in existing searchable/sortable formats (§ 24-72-203(3.5)).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** If requested records are not in the custodian's custody or control (including nonexistence or destruction under a retention schedule), the custodian must "forthwith" notify the requester of that fact — in writing if requested — stating in detail, to the best of the custodian's knowledge and belief, the reason for the records' absence, their location, and what person then has custody or control (C.R.S. § 24-72-203(2)(a), verified). No duty to create a new record or compile data into a format not already maintained (case law), except that digitally stored records must be provided in searchable/sortable formats matching how they are kept (§ 24-72-203(3.5)).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Segregability is enforced by courts rather than spelled out in one clean subsection: exempt portions are redacted and the non-exempt remainder disclosed (specific provisions in § 24-72-204(2)-(3) protect discrete data elements, implying redaction rather than blanket denial). Since SB23-286, a custodian may not convert a searchable/sortable digital record into a non-searchable format before transmission (§ 24-72-203(3.5)) — redaction cannot be an excuse to flatten data. Redaction labor: the statute is silent; standard practice treats staff time reviewing and redacting as "research and retrieval" chargeable under § 24-72-205(6) (first hour free, then up to $41.37/hour) if the custodian has a pre-published fee policy — practice, not express statutory text.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denial grounds are in § 24-72-204: (1) mandatory withholding where disclosure would be contrary to state statute, federal statute/regulation, or court order; (2) discretionary grounds (investigatory records, test questions, real-estate appraisals, security/specialized details, etc.); (3) mandatory-confidential categories (personnel files, trade secrets, sexual-harassment investigation records, etc.). On the requester's request, the custodian must provide a WRITTEN statement of the grounds for denial that "shall cite the law or regulation under which access is denied" (§ 24-72-204(4), verified). For deliberative-process withholding, the custodian must provide a sworn statement specifically describing each document withheld and explaining why each is privileged (§ 24-72-204(3)(a)(XIII), verified). A custodian who believes disclosure of an otherwise-open record would do substantial injury to the public interest must itself apply to district court for permission to withhold and bears the burden of proof (§ 24-72-204(6)(a), verified). No denial deadline separate from the production deadline; no exemption-index (Vaughn) requirement beyond the deliberative-process sworn statement.

**Administrative appeal:** None — judicial only. Colorado has no Public Access Counselor, records ombudsman, or AG appeal for CORA disputes (the AG publishes guidance only). The mandatory pre-suit step is quasi-administrative: written notice to the custodian at least 14 DAYS before filing suit, during which the custodian must meet in person or communicate by telephone with the requester to determine if the dispute may be resolved (verified); if the requester states an expedited need in writing, notice at least 3 BUSINESS DAYS before filing suffices (§ 24-72-204(5)(a)). Any common expense necessary to resolve the dispute is apportioned equally unless the parties agree otherwise (verified).

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Copies: max $0.25 per standard page (§ 24-72-205(5)(a), verified: "a fee not to exceed twenty-five cents per standard page"); actual cost for non-standard formats. NO per-page fee for records provided in a digital or electronic format (§ 24-72-205(5)(a), as amended by SB23-286 — verified location; NOT (1)(b) as sometimes cited). NO transmission fee for records sent by email (§ 24-72-205(1)(b), verified). Research and retrieval (search, retrieval, and in practice review/redaction): first HOUR FREE, then a capped hourly rate — the statutory $30/hour cap in § 24-72-205(6)(a) is adjusted every 5 years by the Legislative Council Staff director of research per the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood CPI (§ 24-72-205(6)(b), verified); the current maximum is $41.37/hour effective July 1, 2024 through June 30, 2029 (a 23% increase from the 2019-2024 rate of $33.58 — CFOIC, verified). The hourly fee may be charged ONLY if the custodian, before receiving the request, posted on its website or otherwise published a written fee policy (§ 24-72-205(6)(a), verified). Computer output other than word processing (databases/GIS): fees may recover actual incremental costs plus a reasonable portion of information-system build/maintenance costs (§ 24-72-205(4)); data manipulation to generate a record in a form not used by the agency is chargeable at a reasonable fee not exceeding actual cost (§ 24-72-205(3), verified). If the custodian accepts credit-card/electronic payment for any other service, it MUST accept it for any CORA "fee or deposit" (§ 24-72-205(7), verified). City practice example (Boulder): $0.25/page paper, hourly rate near the cap after the first free hour, records emailed at no charge (verify each city's posted policy).

**Labor charges:** Yes — staff time for locating and retrieving records (and, per standard practice, reviewing and redacting) is chargeable as "research and retrieval" under § 24-72-205(6): first hour free per request, then a maximum of $41.37/hour (7/1/2024-6/30/2029 rate; CPI-adjusted every 5 years under § 24-72-205(6)(b)), and only if a written fee policy was posted/published before the request. The rate is a cap — agencies may charge less, though CFOIC reporting shows many governments adopted the maximum immediately. Data-manipulation/programming time is separately recoverable at actual cost (§ 24-72-205(3)-(4)).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Prepayment before delivery of copies is STATUTORY: § 24-72-205(1)(b) (verified full text) directs the custodian, within the § 203(3)(a) period, to notify the requester that the copy is available but "will only be sent... once the custodian either receives payment or makes arrangements for receiving payment" for transmission costs and all other lawful fees (unless waived); after payment/arrangement the custodian must send the record within 3 business days. Deposits are recognized: § 24-72-205(7) refers to paying "any fee or deposit," and the Legislative Council Staff CORA summary (last reviewed 9/18/2024) states the custodian may require a deposit equal to estimated staff time before completing a request expected to exceed one hour of research-and-retrieval work. No statutory percentage cap on deposits (unlike Michigan's 50% rule). Common practice: written cost estimate within the 3-day window; work begins/completes after deposit or full prepayment.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No general statutory public-interest or indigency waiver. The one statutory waiver is § 24-72-205(4): for computer-output records (databases/GIS), fees "may be reduced or waived" when the use serves a PUBLIC PURPOSE — public agency program support, nonprofit activities, JOURNALISM, and ACADEMIC RESEARCH — and reductions/waivers "shall be uniformly applied among persons who are similarly situated" (verified). § 24-72-205(1)(b) also contemplates discretionary waiver of transmission costs/fees. Everything else (waiving hourly or page fees) is discretionary agency/city policy; the first free hour and the free-email-transmission rule function as built-in universal waivers.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Delivery of copies is statutorily conditioned on payment: no payment or payment arrangement, no transmission (§ 24-72-205(1)(b)); once payment is received the custodian owes delivery within 3 business days. The statute is otherwise silent on abandonment: custodians close or deem abandoned requests whose estimates go unpaid after a stated period (commonly 30 days, per agency/city policy — practice, not statute). Because delivery may be conditioned on prepayment, non-payment ends the request without penalty to the requester; sending unpaid balances for completed work to collections is general municipal fiscal practice, not CORA.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** The statutory clock modifiers are: (1) the written extenuating-circumstances extension (max +7 working days, written finding required, § 24-72-203(3)(b)); and (2) for copies, the § 24-72-205(1)(b) payment gate — the 3-day duty is discharged by the copy-available/payment notice, and the delivery clock (3 business days) restarts only upon payment or a payment arrangement. In PRACTICE, cities also pause while awaiting clarification of a vague request — CORA neither authorizes nor forbids that. Agency policies define receipt (after-hours/weekend requests treated as received next working day). No third-party-notice tolling exists in CORA.

## REMEDIES

District court show-cause application in the district where the record is found (§ 24-72-204(5)(a)), after the 14-day (or expedited 3-business-day) notice; hearing "at the earliest practical time" (§ 24-72-204(5)(b), verified). Unless the court finds the denial proper, it SHALL order inspection and SHALL award court costs and reasonable ATTORNEY FEES to the prevailing applicant — mandatory — with exactly two verified exceptions in (5)(b): (1) no fee award to a person who has sued the public body where the court finds the records sought are related to the pending litigation and discoverable under C.R.C.P. ch. 4; (2) if the denial was proper, the custodian recovers costs/fees only if the action was frivolous, vexatious, or groundless. A custodian fearing substantial injury to the public interest may itself seek court permission to withhold, bearing the burden (§ 24-72-204(6)(a)). No daily civil penalty under CORA (the $25/day penalty is CCJRA-only, § 24-72-305(7)). The former § 24-72-206 misdemeanor (fine/imprisonment for willful violation) was REPEALED in 2017 (verified via Legislative Council Staff summary; the section no longer appears in current codifications) — no criminal penalty remains.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields: keep name/ID optional — § 24-72-203(1)(a) (SB23-286) bars demanding identification (a delivery email is enough); no residency gate. Purpose question: must be optional for CORA records; add a required anti-solicitation affirmation ONLY on criminal-justice-records (police) request paths (§ 24-72-305.5). Commercial-rate module: not needed for CORA today (HB24-1296, SB25-077 (vetoed), and SB26-107 all failed), except a separate cost path for database/GIS extracts under § 24-72-205(4) with a public-purpose (journalism/nonprofit/academic/agency) discount checkbox applied uniformly. Fee engine: $0.25/page paper only; $0 per-page for digital (§ 205(5)(a)) and $0 email transmission (§ 205(1)(b)); hourly meter starting after 1 free hour at the city's adopted rate (≤$41.37 through 6/30/2029; auto-update 7/1/2029) — and BLOCK hourly charges unless the city's written fee policy was posted before the request (surface the policy link). Payment gate: build the § 205(1)(b) flow — copy-available/estimate notice generated within the 3-working-day window, delivery held for payment or arrangement, then a 3-business-day delivery SLA timer after payment; accept credit/e-payment if the city takes it elsewhere (§ 205(7)). Deadline logic: 3-working-day timer from receipt; extension workflow must generate the written extenuating-circumstances finding within the initial 3 days, cap at +7 working days, and refuse extension for a single identified document. Deposit logic: deposit for jobs estimated over 1 hour (Leg. Council summary), no percentage cap; auto-close unpaid requests per policy (e.g., 30 days) with notice. Auto-acknowledgment: send it (good practice, not required). Litigation-exposure flags: inbound 14-day § 24-72-204(5)(a) notices (and 3-business-day expedited notices) trigger escalation — attorney fees to a prevailing requester are mandatory; also flag any denial lacking a written citation to specific law and any deliberative-process denial missing the § 204(3)(a)(XIII) sworn statement.
