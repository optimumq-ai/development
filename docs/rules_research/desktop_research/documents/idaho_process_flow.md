# Idaho City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Idaho city, under the Idaho Public Records Act (Idaho Code §§ 74-101 through 74-127 (Title 74, ch. 1)). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** Grant or deny in 3 working days (residents) or 21 days (nonresidents). Written-notice extension to 10 working days / 35 days only if more time is needed to locate or retrieve. Silence = deemed denial. No admin appeal — district court within 180 days.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Not anonymous, and residency now matters. I.C. § 74-102(4) (verified verbatim, as amended 2025 ch. 298): a public agency MAY require that a request be submitted in a writing that specifically describes the subject matter and records sought, including a specific date range; the request "shall also provide the requester's name, mailing address, email address, telephone number, and a written declaration by the requesting party attesting or affirming under oath whether such requester is a resident." Residency is not a bar to access, but nonresidents face longer deadlines (21/35 days vs 3/10 working days) and discretionary fees. Purpose: § 74-102(5) — "The custodian shall make no inquiry of any person who requests a public record," EXCEPT (a) to verify identity per § 74-113, (b) to ensure the record will not be used for a mailing or telephone list prohibited by § 74-120 (or as otherwise provided by law), or (c) as required to protect personal information under I.C. title 49, ch. 2 (motor vehicle records) and federal law. Email is expressly authorized by the statute itself: § 74-102(4) — "A request for public records and delivery of the public records may be made by electronic mail."

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No general commercial-purpose branch: no commercial-purpose disclosure requirement, no different deadline, and no separate commercial fee tier in the statute (verified against § 74-102(10) and § 74-103 as amended through 2025). The only purpose-based rule is I.C. § 74-120: an agency may not "distribute or sell for use as a mailing list or a telephone number list any list of persons without first securing the permission of those on the list" (with enumerated exceptions, e.g., registered electors, licensees); the custodian may ask a requester only enough to ensure the records will not be used for such a prohibited list (§ 74-102(5)(b)), and a court may assess a civil penalty up to $1,000 for deliberate bad-faith violations. The 2025 amendments differentiate by RESIDENCY (deadlines and fees), not by commercial purpose.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty, and none appears in the AG's Idaho Public Records Law Manual (July 2025 ed., AG Labrador). The 3-working-day (resident) / 21-day (nonresident) grant-or-deny response is itself the required action. Cities using portals (e.g., Boise's online system) commonly send automatic receipt confirmations, but that is practice only, not statute.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
I.C. § 74-103(2) (verified against current statute text, as amended 2025 ch. 298): a public agency "shall either grant or deny" a request within 3 WORKING DAYS of receipt for an Idaho RESIDENT, and within 21 DAYS of receipt for a NONRESIDENT (statute says "twenty-one (21) days," unqualified; the AG's July 2025 Public Records Law Manual treats these as calendar days). The deadline requires an actual determination and, if granted, production of the records — not a mere acknowledgment. Extension: "If it is determined by employees of the public agency that a longer period of time is needed to locate or retrieve the public records," the agency must notify the requester IN WRITING and provide the records no later than 10 working days after the request (residents) or 35 days (nonresidents). The nonresident tier was created by 2025 H.B. 253 (ch. 298, eff. 7/1/2025). Special rule: if an existing electronic record must first be converted to another electronic format and conversion cannot be completed within the allotted time, the agency notifies in writing and provides the converted record at a mutually agreed time. Constructive denial: § 74-103(3) — if the agency "fails to respond within the time allotted for the response, the request shall be deemed to be denied," starting the 180-day court clock. The 10-day/35-day period is not an automatic grace period — by its terms it requires a determination that more time is needed to locate or retrieve, though agencies commonly invoke it as a matter of practice.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

I.C. § 74-102(4): the requesting party "shall be as specific as possible" and a request "shall describe records sought in sufficient detail to enable the public body to locate such records with reasonable effort"; the agency may require a writing that specifically describes the subject matter and includes a specific date range. § 74-103(1) (verified verbatim; amended 2024, ch. 107): requests must be made to the designated custodian (defined in § 74-101(3)), "a public records request must clearly indicate that it is a public records request," and the agency "shall have no obligation and shall not be deemed to have assumed any obligation to respond" to a request that fails to comply with the subsection. Duty to assist is permissive, not mandatory: AG Manual (July 2025) — "The public agency may provide the requester information to help the requester narrow the scope of the request." There is no express tolling provision for clarification — an insufficiently specific or non-compliant request can simply be treated as triggering no response obligation.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No duty to create records: AG Manual (July 2025) — "nothing in the public records law requires the creation of a record in order to provide it." The Act provides access to existing records only. A no-records response should still be made within the statutory window; because failure to respond is a deemed denial (§ 74-103(3)), cities in practice send a written no-responsive-records reply within the 3/10-working-day (resident) window. The statute does not prescribe a specific no-records notice format.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
I.C. § 74-112 (verified): on receipt of a request, the agency "shall ... separate the exempt and nonexempt material and make the nonexempt material available"; commingling is no basis for denial. Redaction labor IS chargeable: § 74-102(10)(b) permits fees when nonpublic information must be deleted from a record (one of the three statutory charging conditions, applicable even under 100 pages), and § 74-102(10)(e) prices labor at the per-hour rate of the lowest-paid administrative staff employee capable of the task, or the lowest-paid attorney capable when redaction/legal review requires an attorney. The first 2 hours of labor are free for RESIDENTS only (§ 74-102(10)(a)).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
I.C. § 74-103(4)-(5): for any denial or partial denial, the person legally responsible for administering the agency (or designee) must notify the requester IN WRITING, within the same response deadline. The notice must state whether "the attorney for the public agency has reviewed the request" or that the agency has chosen not to consult counsel; must cite "the statutory authority for the denial"; and must state "the person's right to appeal the denial or partial denial and the time periods for doing so" (180 days to district court under § 74-115). Silence within the deadline = deemed denial (§ 74-103(3)). Presumption of openness: § 74-102(1) — "there is a presumption that all public records in Idaho are open at all reasonable times for inspection except as otherwise expressly provided by statute"; exemptions construed narrowly with the burden on the party claiming exemption (Cowles Publ'g Co. v. Kootenai Cnty. Bd. of Cnty. Comm'rs, 144 Idaho 259, 159 P.3d 896 (2007)). No Vaughn-type index requirement.

**Administrative appeal:** None — judicial only. Idaho has no Public Access Counselor, records ombudsman, or AG appeal jurisdiction; the AG's Public Records Law Manual is guidance only. § 74-115 (verified) makes the district-court petition "the sole remedy for a person aggrieved by the denial of a request for disclosure."

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
I.C. § 74-102(10) (subsection letters verified against current text). Residents: NO fee for the first 2 hours of labor or for copying the first 100 pages of paper records (§ 74-102(10)(a), resident-only after 2025 ch. 298). Charging conditions (§ 74-102(10)(b)): fees only when (i) the request exceeds 100 paper pages, (ii) nonpublic information must be deleted/redacted, or (iii) labor exceeds 2 person-hours. Resident copying fees "may not exceed the actual cost to the agency of copying the record" (§ 74-102(10)(c)). Labor rates: per-hour pay rate of the lowest-paid administrative staff employee — or lowest-paid capable attorney for redaction/legal review — capable of the work (§ 74-102(10)(e)). Electronic records (§ 74-102(10)(d)): fee may include the direct cost of copying, the standard cost if sold as a publication, and conversion/programming costs when converting formats; § 74-102(4) requires that requests and delivery be permitted by email. Nonresidents (§ 74-102(10)(g), added 2025 ch. 298): fees for labor and copying at the agency's discretion, capped at actual costs — the 2-hour/100-page free allowances do not apply. Itemization (§ 74-102(10)(h)): fee statements "shall be itemized to show the per page costs" and hourly rates of employees and attorneys; "No lump sum costs shall be assigned." No statutory per-page cent rate exists; city schedules vary (Boise quotes actual costs case by case). Inspection itself is free (out of scope here).

**Labor charges:** Yes, chargeable — for residents only past the first 2 person-hours per request, and only in the three § 74-102(10)(b) situations (over 100 paper pages, deletion of nonpublic information required, or labor over 2 hours). Rate cap (§ 74-102(10)(e)): per-hour pay rate of the lowest-paid administrative staff employee capable of the task; attorney time (redaction/legal review) at the lowest-paid capable attorney's rate. For nonresidents, labor charges are discretionary up to actual cost with no free threshold (§ 74-102(10)(g)).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
I.C. § 74-102(12) (verified — advance payment sits in subsection (12), not (10)): "The custodian may require advance payment of fees authorized by this section," and "[a]ny portion of an advance payment in excess of the actual costs of labor and copying incurred by the agency in responding to the request shall be returned to the requester." No statutory percentage cap and no formal estimate procedure — full prepayment of the estimated/itemized fee may be demanded before work or release, with mandatory refund of any excess. City practice (e.g., Boise): costs must be paid before records are distributed; clerks typically send an itemized estimate and await payment.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Statutory MANDATORY waiver, RESIDENT-ONLY after 2025 ch. 298 — § 74-102(10)(f) (verified verbatim opening): "The public agency shall not charge any cost or fee for copies or labor when the requester is a resident" and demonstrates the examination/copying (i) is likely to contribute significantly to the public's understanding of government operations or activities, (ii) is not primarily in the requester's individual interest (including but not limited to litigation interest), and (iii) will not occur if fees are charged because the requester has insufficient financial resources. All prongs required — a combined public-interest + inability-to-pay test, narrower than the federal FOIA waiver, and unavailable to nonresidents. No standalone indigency waiver. Agencies may also voluntarily waive fees.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute authorizes advance payment (§ 74-102(12)), so the agency may withhold copies until fees are paid; nothing in the Act sets an abandonment timeline. Common city practice is to hold the request open for a stated period after the fee estimate/invoice and then close it as abandoned if unpaid — practice, not statute. Nonpayment of a lawful fee is not a "denial" the requester can appeal as such, though a dispute over fee lawfulness could be litigated under § 74-115.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No general tolling provisions. Three practical pauses: (1) a non-compliant request (not clearly indicated as a public records request, not directed to the designated custodian, or lacking § 74-102(4) contents) never starts the clock — the agency has no obligation to respond (§ 74-103(1), verified verbatim); (2) electronic format conversion — if conversion "cannot be completed within the time allotted for the response," the agency notifies in writing and delivers at a mutually agreed time (§ 74-103(2)); (3) advance-payment demands halt production (not the determination) until paid (§ 74-102(12)). Clarification requests do NOT toll the statutory deadline; there is no statutory clarification-tolling clause.

## REMEDIES

I.C. § 74-115 (verified verbatim): petition the district court of the county where the records or some part thereof are located within 180 CALENDAR DAYS "from the date of mailing of the notice of denial or partial denial" (deemed denials included); the court must set responsive pleadings and hearing "at the earliest possible time, or in no event beyond twenty-eight (28) calendar days from the date of filing"; the agency must keep all disputed records until the end of the appeal period or decision, whichever is longer. I.C. § 74-116(2) (verified): "the court shall award reasonable costs and attorney fees to the prevailing party or parties, if it finds that the request or refusal to provide records was frivolously pursued" — mandatory upon a frivolousness finding, but frivolousness is the gate (works both ways; a good-faith agency loss yields no fee award). I.C. § 74-117 (verified): civil penalty up to $1,000 against a public official who "deliberately and in bad faith improperly refused a legitimate request," paid into the state general fund. § 74-120: court-assessed civil penalty up to $1,000 for deliberate bad-faith list-use violations. I.C. § 74-118 (verified): no liability for good-faith RELEASE of a public record in attempted compliance with the chapter. No criminal penalties in the Act.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal implications: (1) MUST collect name, mailing address, email, phone, and a sworn residency declaration (attesting/affirming under oath whether the requester is a resident, § 74-102(4)) — residency drives both the deadline engine (3/10 working days vs 21/35 days) and the fee engine (free allowances AND the mandatory fee waiver are resident-only). (2) The confirmation email should expressly label the submission "a public records request" and route to the designated custodian, since unlabeled/misdirected requests carry no response obligation (§ 74-103(1)). (3) Do NOT include a general purpose field — purpose inquiry is prohibited except identity verification and a targeted certification that records will not be used for a prohibited mailing/telephone list (§§ 74-102(5), 74-120); include that certification instead of a commercial-rate module (none exists in Idaho). (4) Require a specific description + date range field to satisfy § 74-102(4) specificity. (5) Fee module: resident logic = first 2 labor hours and first 100 paper pages free, then itemized actual cost at lowest-paid-capable-employee rate (attorney rate for redaction review); nonresident logic = discretionary up to actual cost; itemization is mandatory with per-page costs and hourly rates shown — never lump sums (§ 74-102(10)(h)). (6) Deposit logic: full advance payment permitted (§ 74-102(12)), no percentage cap; hold release until paid; refund any excess over actual costs. (7) Fee-waiver question should first gate on residency, then track the § 74-102(10)(f) prongs (public understanding + not primarily individual interest + inability to pay). (8) Auto-acknowledgment is fine but has no legal effect; the deadline countdown should target grant/deny at day 3 (working days, residents) or day 21 (nonresidents), with an extension-notice generator requiring a stated locate/retrieve justification — the statute conditions the 10/35-day extension on an actual determination that more time is needed. (9) Deemed-denial flag: any request with no response by the applicable deadline is legally denied (§ 74-103(3)) and starts the 180-day court clock — treat as high litigation exposure, since denial-notice defects (missing attorney-review statement or statutory citation) are facially checkable. (10) Denial template must include: attorney-reviewed (or chose-not-to-consult) statement, statutory authority, and 180-day district-court appeal notice (§ 74-103(4)-(5), § 74-115).

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** After verification against the current Idaho Legislature statute text (§§ 74-101, 74-102, 74-103, 74-112, 74-115, 74-116, 74-117, 74-118, 74-120), the H.B. 253 (2025) bill page and text, the AG's July 2025 Public Records Law Manual, and the Cowles opinion summary, the following remain unconfirmed or noted: (1) Day-type for nonresident deadlines — the statute says "twenty-one (21) days" / "thirty-five (35) days" without specifying calendar or working; the AG Manual reads them as calendar days, which is the safest operational assumption, but no case law confirms it. (2) The internal roman-numeral ordering of § 74-102(10)(b)'s three charging conditions ((i) >100 pages, (ii) deletion of nonpublic information, (iii) >2 hours labor) is inferred from the paragraph's order as summarized, not read verbatim. (3) The precise allocation of denial-notice elements between § 74-103(4) and (5) was confirmed as a set but not subsection-by-subsection. (4) Whether the 2-hour/100-page free allowances may be aggregated or reset across serial requests from one requester is not addressed in statute and no authority was found. (5) City fee-schedule practice varies and some published schedules (e.g., small cities charging per-page fees within the first 100 pages) appear in tension with § 74-102(10)(a) for residents; the earlier draft's City of Dover rates ($0.10/$0.15 per page) were not re-verified and are omitted from the fee narrative. (6) No authority found on abandonment/closure timelines for non-payment — practice only. (7) The 2024 session law (ch. 107) amending § 74-103(1) was confirmed via the statute's history line and current text, but the session law itself was not read. (8) A prior draft attribution to an 'Open Idaho, May 2026' commentary on reflexive extensions could not be verified and was removed; the underlying point stands on the statute's own conditional extension language.
