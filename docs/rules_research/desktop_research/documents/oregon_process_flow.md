# Oregon City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Oregon city, under the Oregon Public Records Law (ORS 192.311 to 192.478). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** ORS 192.324/.329: acknowledge in 5 business days; complete ASAP and by 15 business days total either produce or send status letter with estimated date. Clock suspends for unpaid fees and clarification (60-day auto-close). Free DA petition decided in 7 days; court attorney fees mandatory if requester fully prevails.

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Any person may request — 'every person' has the inspection right, with no residency, citizenship, or in-state requirement (ORS 192.314). The AG's Public Records and Meetings Manual and the League of Oregon Cities handbook state records are available 'regardless of the person's identity, motive, or need'; purpose matters only when the requester seeks the public-interest fee waiver (ORS 192.324(5)) or in narrow exemption balancing. The statute requires a written request but does not require legal name or purpose; effectively pseudonymous email requests are accepted in practice, though contact info is needed for fee notices and delivery. No statute authorizes demanding purpose as a condition of processing.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None. Oregon's statute contains no commercial-purpose disclosure duty, no separate deadline, and no higher fee tier for commercial requesters; requester motive is irrelevant to the disclosure decision (AG Manual). Commercial purpose is relevant only negatively to the discretionary public-interest fee waiver (a commercial requester is unlikely to show disclosure 'primarily benefits the general public,' ORS 192.324(5)). Separate resale restrictions exist only for specific record sets outside the general law (e.g., DMV records under driver-privacy statutes), not for ordinary city records.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
Statutory, not just practice: within 5 business days after receiving a written request, the city must acknowledge receipt or complete the response (ORS 192.324(2)). The acknowledgment must do one of three things: (a) confirm the city is custodian, (b) state it is not custodian, or (c) state it is uncertain whether it is custodian (ORS 192.324(2)(a)-(c)). Nuance: ORS 192.324(2)'s acknowledgment clock runs from 'receiving the request' with no designated-person qualifier, while ORS 192.329(1)'s completion duty is keyed to receipt 'by an individual identified in the public body's procedure' — the League of Oregon Cities advises routing all requests, however received, to the records custodian. Cities (e.g., Portland, McMinnville) build the 5-day acknowledgment into portal auto-replies.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Two-step statutory scheme (2017 SB 481, Or. Laws 2017 ch. 456). Step 1: within 5 business days after receiving the request, the city must acknowledge the request or complete its response (ORS 192.324(2)). Step 2: the city must complete its response 'as soon as practicable and without unreasonable delay' (ORS 192.329(1) — note this completion duty is expressly keyed to a request 'received by an individual identified in the public body's procedure described in ORS 192.324'), and 'as soon as reasonably possible but not later than 10 business days after the date by which a public body is required to acknowledge receipt' (i.e., 15 business days from receipt) must either complete the response or provide a written statement that it is still processing plus 'a reasonable estimated date' for completion (ORS 192.329(5)) — the 10-day clock anchors to the acknowledgment DEADLINE, not the actual acknowledgment date. 'Complete response' (ORS 192.329(2)(a)-(f)) means producing all nonexempt records (or telling requester where publicly available), asserting exemptions with citations, segregating exempt material per ORS 192.338, written statements re non-custodianship or Glomar-type situations, and notice of petition/review rights. Escape hatch: the 5/15-day periods 'do not apply' if compliance would be impracticable due to staff/volunteer unavailability (staff on leave count as unavailable, ORS 192.329(7)), impairment of other essential functions, or volume of concurrent requests (ORS 192.329(6)) — but the practicable/no-unreasonable-delay duty remains. Enforcement: failure to provide the ORS 192.329 response within the prescribed period, and unreasonably long estimated dates, are petitionable grounds (ORS 192.407(1)(a)-(c)); DA/AG failure to order within 7 days of a petition is treated as an order denying the petition, permitting suit (ORS 192.418(1)); an elected official's failure to respond within 7 days is treated as a denial for purposes of court proceedings (ORS 192.418(2)).

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

The city may in good faith request additional information or clarification; its obligation to further complete the response is suspended until the requester answers, and if the requester does not respond within 60 days the city 'shall close the request' (ORS 192.329(4)). No express statutory duty to assist in narrowing, but the AG Manual instructs bodies to work with requesters (offer date ranges, search terms, cheaper alternatives), and DA/AG petition review of noncompliance with ORS 192.329 (ORS 192.407(1)(c)) polices bad-faith clarification games. Portland's rule ARA-8.03 asks requesters to identify records 'with as much specificity as possible, including a date range.'

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** No duty to create a record, answer questions, analyze records, or perform legal research (AG Manual), though bodies must retrieve pre-existing electronic data from databases. If the city is not custodian or has no responsive records, the complete response must include a written statement to that effect (ORS 192.329(2)(d)); the 5-day acknowledgment likewise can state non-custodianship (ORS 192.324(2)(b)). 'No records' statements are reviewable via DA petition as an alleged failure to comply with ORS 192.329 (ORS 192.407(1)(c)).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Mandatory segregability: if a record contains both exempt and nonexempt material, the city must separate and disclose the nonexempt material (ORS 192.338); AG Manual describes redaction and furnishing redacted copies. Redaction labor IS chargeable: fees may include 'the cost of time spent by an attorney ... in reviewing the public records, redacting material ... or segregating the public records into exempt and nonexempt records,' but may NOT include attorney time spent 'determining the application of the provisions of ORS 192.311 to 192.478' (i.e., exemption analysis) (ORS 192.324(4)(b)). Non-attorney staff redaction time is chargeable as actual cost.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
A complete response asserting withholding must be in writing, assert each exemption and cite its legal authority — for exemptions outside ORS 192.345/192.355 the specific state or federal statute must be cited (ORS 192.329(2)(b); AG Manual) — and must include notice that the requester may seek review via the petition process, citing ORS 192.401, 192.411, 192.415, 192.418, 192.422, 192.427 and 192.431 (ORS 192.329(2)(f)). Conditional (ORS 192.345) exemptions carry a public-interest balancing test; burden of proving any exemption is always on the public body (ORS 192.431(1)). No Vaughn-index requirement, but AG recommends briefly describing withheld material. Denial timing follows the general 5/15-business-day structure; failure to respond in time is a petitionable ground (ORS 192.407(1)(a)).

**Administrative appeal:** Yes — for city (local) records, petition the district attorney of the county where the city is located (if multi-county, where its administrative offices are), who 'shall carry out the functions of the Attorney General' using the ORS 192.401/192.411 procedures (ORS 192.415). Grounds: denial of inspection, failure to provide the ORS 192.329 response within the prescribed period, unreasonably long estimated dates, other ORS 192.329 noncompliance (ORS 192.407(1)(a)-(c)), and unreasonable fee-waiver denial (ORS 192.324(6)). The DA must issue an order granting/denying within 7 days of receiving the petition; failure to do so 'shall be treated as an order denying the petition,' permitting suit (ORS 192.418(1)). If the DA orders disclosure, the city must comply in full within 7 days of the order unless within that period it issues notice of intent to institute proceedings, and it must file suit within 7 days after issuing that notice (ORS 192.411(2) via 192.415). On an ORS 192.407 petition the DA may order disclosure within 7 days (or another appropriate period), impose a $200 penalty payable to the requester for undue delay or failure to respond, and order fee waiver/reduction (ORS 192.407(3), as amended by Or. Laws 2019 ch. 205). Exception: where an ELECTED official (e.g., mayor, councilors) denies or claims the right to deny, no DA/AG petition lies (or if filed, it is not considered) — the requester goes straight to circuit court (ORS 192.427); the elected official's non-response for 7 days is treated as a denial (ORS 192.418(2)). Optional: Public Records Advocate facilitated dispute resolution is available to city disputes only if requester, city, and Advocate all agree; must be completed within 21 days of the Advocate's receipt of the request unless all parties extend (ORS 192.464). No filing fee for DA petitions.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
Statute sets no rates — only a standard: fees must be 'reasonably calculated to reimburse the public body for the public body's actual cost of making public records available,' including costs for summarizing, compiling or tailoring (ORS 192.324(4)(a)); no profit permitted. Search/locate staff time (chargeable even if no records are found — LOC handbook), copying, mailing, and attorney redaction/segregation time are chargeable; attorney exemption-analysis time is not (ORS 192.324(4)(b)). Estimate gate: no fee greater than $25 may be charged unless the city first gives written notice of the estimated fee and the requester confirms it wants the city to proceed (ORS 192.324(4)(c)). Electronic records: must be provided in the requested electronic form if available, else in the form maintained (ORS 192.324(3)); charging is at actual cost (media + staff time). 2025 amendment (Or. Laws 2025 ch. 30, HB 3385): county clerk record-copy fees are set by ORS 205.320 instead — a carve-out irrelevant to city records. Typical city practice (Portland fee schedule): $0.25/side B&W letter/legal, $0.50/side 11x17, $1.50/side color, CD/DVD $5.00; staff time billed at hourly wage of the lowest-paid capable employee plus overhead. No statewide free-page or free-hour threshold exists in statute.

**Labor charges:** Yes — staff time for searching, retrieving, reviewing, redacting, compiling, and supervising inspection is chargeable as part of 'actual cost' (ORS 192.324(4)(a); AG Manual; League of Oregon Cities handbook, which confirms locate-time is chargeable 'regardless if the requested records are located'). Attorney time is chargeable only for review/redaction/segregation, never for determining application of exemptions (ORS 192.324(4)(b)). No statutory free-hours threshold or hourly cap; the only universal gate is the $25 written-estimate-plus-confirmation rule (ORS 192.324(4)(c)). City practice caps rates at the lowest-paid employee capable of the task (Portland ARA-8.03), consistent with the 'reasonably calculated' standard.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Statute is silent on deposits but the AG Manual confirms a body may require prepayment of its estimated charges before compiling records; once the city informs the requester of a fee (or denies a waiver), its duty to complete the response is suspended until the fee is paid or waived (ORS 192.329(3)(a)). No statutory percentage cap. City practice varies: Portland requires a 50% deposit when estimated costs exceed $100 and full payment before release; many cities require full prepayment of any estimate over $25 (LOC model practice: request not fulfilled 'until the requester makes a deposit in an amount of the estimated fee'). Overpayments are refunded.

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
Discretionary public-interest waiver only: the custodian 'may furnish copies without charge or at a substantially reduced fee' if waiver/reduction 'is in the public interest because making the record available primarily benefits the general public' (ORS 192.324(5)). No statutory indigency waiver (ability to pay is one AG-Manual factor). Discretion must be objectively reasonable under the totality of circumstances (In Defense of Animals v. OHSU, 199 Or App 160, 112 P3d 336 (2005)); AG factors include requester identity/purpose, dissemination capacity, burden on the body, and whether partial reduction suffices. An 'unreasonable denial of a fee waiver or fee reduction' is independently petitionable to the district attorney (ORS 192.324(6)), and a DA/AG/court finding undue delay or failure to respond may itself order a fee waiver or reduction (ORS 192.407(3)(c)).

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Response obligation is suspended from the date of the fee notice until the fee is paid or waived (ORS 192.329(3)(a)). If the requester fails to pay within 60 days after the fee notice (or after waiver denial), the city 'shall close the request' (ORS 192.329(3)(b)) — mandatory closure; the requester must start over. Records are not released before full payment in common city practice (Portland ARA-8.03; LOC handbook).

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** Two statutory suspensions with mandatory 60-day closure: (1) fee notice — duty to complete suspended until fee paid or waived, closure at 60 days of nonpayment (ORS 192.329(3)); (2) good-faith clarification request — duty suspended until requester responds, closure at 60 days of silence (ORS 192.329(4)). Additionally the 5- and 15-business-day periods 'do not apply' while compliance is impracticable due to unavailable staff/volunteers (leave counts), impairment of other essential functions, or request volume (ORS 192.329(6)-(7)) — a tolling-like out that does not excuse the overarching 'without unreasonable delay' duty (ORS 192.329(1)) and can be tested by ORS 192.407 petition.

## REMEDIES

Suit for injunctive or declaratory relief in circuit court of the county where the city sits (after DA denial/deemed denial, or directly for elected officials). Review is de novo; burden on the public body to sustain withholding; in camera inspection allowed on the court's own motion; noncompliance with a court order is punishable as contempt (ORS 192.431(1)-(2); 192.415). Attorney fees: MANDATORY award of costs, disbursements and reasonable attorney fees at trial and on appeal to a requester who prevails; discretionary (full or partial award) if the requester prevails in part (ORS 192.431(3)). If a body neither complies with a disclosure order nor timely institutes proceedings, the requester gets trial-level costs and reasonable attorney fees regardless of which party sued or prevailed (ORS 192.431(3)). Civil penalty: $200 to the requester for undue delay or failure to respond, orderable by DA, AG, or court (ORS 192.407(3)(b)). No criminal penalties in the Public Records Law itself.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Identity fields: name optional as a legal matter — collect email for fee notices/delivery; never require purpose except an optional fee-waiver justification box. No commercial-rate module and no commercial-purpose question (no statutory basis). Auto-acknowledgment: send within 5 business days and include the required custodian-status statement (is / is not / uncertain custodian) — build the three-option template into the auto-reply, and route every inbound request (however received) to the designated ORS 192.324(7) contact, since ORS 192.329(1) keys the completion duty to receipt by that individual. Timers: 15-business-day master timer (10 business days after the 5-day acknowledgment deadline); if not complete, auto-generate the ORS 192.329(5) status letter with a specific reasonable estimated completion date (an unreasonably long estimate is petitionable under ORS 192.407(1)(b)). Fee logic: hard stop at $25 — no fee above $25 without written estimate plus recorded requester confirmation (ORS 192.324(4)(c)); itemize staff time at lowest-capable-employee rate; flag attorney line-items as redaction/segregation only (exemption-analysis time is non-billable). Deposit module: prepayment permitted, no statutory cap (Portland practice: 50% over $100); suspend the clock at fee-notice date; auto-close at 60 days nonpayment and 60 days clarification silence (statutorily mandatory 'shall close'). Fee-waiver question: offer public-interest waiver request with factor prompts (dissemination plans, benefit to public, ability to pay); waiver denials are DA-petitionable under ORS 192.324(6), so log reasoning. Litigation-exposure flags: missed 5/15-day deadlines (petitionable; DA silence for 7 days = deemed denial), undue delay ($200 penalty), any DA disclosure order (7-day comply-or-notice, then 7 days to sue), elected-official custodians (different appeal path — direct to circuit court), and mandatory attorney fees if the requester fully prevails in court.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) RESOLVED: HB 3385 (Or. Laws 2025 ch. 30) is a narrow amendment adding a county-clerk carve-out to ORS 192.324(4)(d) (clerk copy fees set by ORS 205.320); the introduced-bill markup confirms no other change — irrelevant to city requests. The enrolled bill has no emergency/effective-date clause (default effective date applies; in force well before July 2026). (2) The $200 penalty and 2019 c.205 history in ORS 192.407(3)(b) were verified verbatim from full-text statute publishers (oregon.public.law) consistent with the Legislature's chapter 192 page, though the exact subsection was not re-pulled from the .gov page itself. (3) Per-page rates ($0.25/side etc.) are Portland practice (schedule possibly dated), not statute — rates vary by city; the '30 minutes included' convention is local. (4) No statutory deadline for a requester to file suit after a DA denial/deemed denial was found (general limitations principles apply). (5) CONFIRMED dead: 2023 SB 417 (least-expensive-manner, media waiver presumption, rate caps) died in Senate Rules and was never enacted — do not rely on it. (6) Receipt ambiguity is real but asymmetrical: ORS 192.324(2)'s 5-day acknowledgment clock runs from 'receiving the request' with no designated-person qualifier, while ORS 192.329(1)'s completion duty applies to requests 'received by an individual identified in the public body's procedure' — email to the wrong address arguably starts the acknowledgment clock but not the completion clock; cities handle this differently (LOC advises routing all requests to the custodian). (7) AG Manual propositions (prepayment allowed; identity/motive irrelevant) were verified via the DOJ manual's indexed text and the League of Oregon Cities 2024 handbook, not re-quoted page-by-page from the current DOJ edition.
