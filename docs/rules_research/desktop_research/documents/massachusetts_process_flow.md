# Massachusetts City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Massachusetts city, under the Massachusetts Public Records Law (M.G.L. c. 66, §§ 6A, 10 & 10A; definition at M.G.L. c. 4, § 7, cl. 26; regulations at 950 CMR 32.00). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** MA Public Records Law: email to city RAO OK. Written response in 10 business days; municipal production by 25 business days; Supervisor of Records can add up to 30 more (single extension). Miss the 10-day response = appealable non-response AND all fees forfeited (s10(e)).

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Any person may request; no residency requirement and no resident/non-resident or press/public distinction (c. 4, § 7(26); RCFP guide). The Secretary's Guide (updated Dec. 2022) states 'the requestor may not be required to identify himself or herself as a condition of obtaining access to the requested records.' Purpose may not be demanded: 950 CMR 32.06 provides a records access officer may not require the requester to specify the purpose of a request EXCEPT (1) to determine whether the records are requested for a commercial purpose or (2) to determine whether to grant a fee-waiver request; identity may likewise be probed only for those two determinations. Practical near-anonymity: an email request needs only a delivery address, though fee estimates/payment make full anonymity awkward.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** No separate deadline track, but commercial purpose changes FEES and appeal posture: (1) under § 10(d)(iv), a records access officer may petition the Supervisor of Records to charge labor above the $25/hour cap, and the Supervisor may approve where the request is for a commercial purpose (or the higher fee is a good-faith necessary actual cost not designed to limit access); (2) commercial purpose — defined in 950 CMR 32.03 and the Secretary's Guide as sale/resale of the record or use to advance the requester's strategic business interests with expectation of profit, expressly excluding news gathering, citizen-oversight, academic/scientific/journalistic/public research — is a § 10A(d)(2) ground for a court to deny the attorney-fee presumption where the request was commercial and not in the public interest; and (3) the Supervisor may decline to accept an appeal made solely for a commercial purpose (950 CMR 32.08). Custodians may ask about purpose solely to make the commercial-purpose determination (950 CMR 32.06).

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No separate acknowledgment duty. The statutory 10-business-day written response is itself the required action; if production is delayed, the § 10(b)(i) written response must 'confirm receipt of the request.' Email requests to the posted RAO address are valid delivery (§ 10(a): hand, first-class mail, or electronic mail). City practice (e.g., Boston's portal) is auto-acknowledgment, but the statute does not require any acknowledgment earlier than the 10-business-day response.

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
10 business days from receipt to permit inspection or furnish the copy (M.G.L. c. 66, § 10(a); the duty attaches if the request reasonably describes the record, the record is in the RAO's possession/custody/control, and the fee is paid). Requests may be delivered in hand, by first-class mail, or by email to the posted RAO address (§ 10(a)). If the municipality cannot produce within 10 business days, § 10(b) requires a written response within those 10 days that (verbatim clause numbers verified): (i) confirms receipt; (ii) identifies records/categories not within possession, custody or control; (iii) identifies another custodian if known; (iv) identifies records to be withheld with the specific exemption(s) and reasons; (v) identifies records to be produced with a detailed statement of why the magnitude or difficulty of the request unduly burdens; (vi) states a reasonable production timeframe — for a MUNICIPALITY not later than 25 business days after initial receipt (15 for state agencies), longer only if the requester voluntarily agrees; (vii) suggests a reasonable modification of scope or offers to assist in narrowing; (viii) includes an itemized good-faith fee estimate; and (ix) states appeal rights (Supervisor of Records and superior court). For requests too voluminous even for 25 days, the RAO may petition the Supervisor of Records under § 10(c) for a SINGLE extension not to exceed 30 additional business days for a municipality (20 for an agency); the Supervisor may grant a longer extension or relieve the RAO of the obligation to comply where the request unduly burdens operations or is frivolous or designed to intimidate/harass and not intended for broad public dissemination. Constructive denial: failure to respond within 10 business days is appealable to the Supervisor within 90 calendar days of the REQUEST (950 CMR 32.08(1)), and § 10(e) bars charging ANY fee unless the RAO responded within 10 business days.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

The 10-day production duty applies only to a request that 'reasonably describes' the record (§ 10(a)). The RAO has an affirmative duty to assist: c. 66, § 6A(b)(i) requires RAOs to 'assist persons seeking public records to identify the records sought,' § 10(b)(vii) requires the delayed-response letter to suggest a reasonable modification of scope or offer assistance narrowing a burdensome request, and 950 CMR 32.06 directs the RAO to suggest modifications 'when appropriate.' Common city practice: send a clarification email; the Supervisor treats the clock as effectively restarting/pausing pending clarification of a request that does not reasonably describe records (Supervisor practice under 950 CMR 32.00, not an express statutory tolling clause).

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** If no responsive records exist, the RAO must say so in the written 10-business-day response — § 10(b)(ii) requires identifying records/categories not within the possession, custody or control of the municipality after a reasonable search. No duty to create a new record, honor prospective requests, or answer questions (Secretary's Guide: 'there is no obligation to create a record for a requestor or to honor prospective requests'). A 'no records' response is appealable to the Supervisor of Records, who can order a description of the search or a further search.

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Segregability is mandatory: any 'segregable portion' of a public record is itself a public record and non-exempt portions must be released with redactions (c. 4, § 7(26); c. 66, § 10(a)). Redaction/segregation LABOR is NOT chargeable for either agencies or municipalities — §§ 10(d)(ii) (agencies) and 10(d)(iii) (municipalities) both provide, in materially identical language, that fees 'shall not be assessed for time spent segregating or redacting records unless such segregation or redaction is required by law or approved by the supervisor of records' under clause (iv) (e.g., CORI, student records, other statutorily mandated redactions).

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denials must be in writing within the 10-business-day window, must identify the specific record(s) or portions withheld, cite the specific exemption(s) in c. 4, § 7(26) with the reasons for withholding, and include notice of appeal rights to the Supervisor of Records and superior court (§ 10(b)(iv), (ix); 950 CMR 32.00). Exemptions are strictly and narrowly construed, records are presumed public, and the custodian must 'prove with specificity the exemption which applies' (Attorney Gen. v. Assistant Comm'r of the Real Prop. Dep't of Boston, 380 Mass. 623, 625 (1980)); in court the presumption that each record is public and the municipality's preponderance-of-the-evidence burden are codified at § 10A(d). No Vaughn-index statute, but the Supervisor requires exemption-by-exemption justification on appeal.

**Administrative appeal:** Yes — petition the Supervisor of Records (Secretary of the Commonwealth, Public Records Division) under M.G.L. c. 66, § 10A(a) and 950 CMR 32.08: within 90 CALENDAR DAYS of the date of the RAO's response (or, for a failure to respond, within 90 calendar days of the request), attaching complete copies of the request and all correspondence including email headers (950 CMR 32.08). The Supervisor must issue a written determination within 10 business days of receipt and may order compliance; noncompliance may be referred to the Attorney General, who may take whatever measures necessary to ensure compliance (§ 10A(b)). The Supervisor may decline an appeal where the records are the subject of active litigation/administrative hearings/mediation, the request is designed to harass or intimidate or to assist in the commission of a crime, is made solely for a commercial purpose, or the petition requirements were not met (950 CMR 32.08). Appeal is optional — a requester may go straight to superior court.

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
M.G.L. c. 66, § 10(d): clause (i) — actual cost of any storage device or material may be charged, but standard black-and-white paper copies/printouts capped at $0.05/page (single or double-sided); actual cost for special media. No per-page copy charge for records delivered electronically; § 6A(d) makes electronic delivery the default — records go out by electronic means unless unavailable in electronic form or the requester cannot receive them, in the requester's preferred format or, absent a preference, 'searchable, machine readable' form. Labor: clause (iii) (municipalities) — employee time to search for, compile, segregate, redact or reproduce chargeable at up to $25/hour; a municipality with a population OVER 20,000 may not charge the first 2 hours; municipalities of 20,000 or fewer may charge from the first hour (agencies, clause (ii): first 4 hours free). Above-$25/hour rates only with Supervisor approval under clause (iv) (commercial purpose or good-faith necessary actual cost). § 10(f): 'employee time' includes necessary vendors — outside legal counsel, technology and payroll consultants. Postage at actual cost (Guide/practice). No statutory programming-fee schedule; extraordinary IT retrieval goes through the actual-cost/Supervisor-petition route. § 10(e): NO fee may be charged unless the RAO responded within 10 business days.

**Labor charges:** Chargeable: search, compilation, and reproduction time at up to $25/hour, after 2 free hours for municipalities over 20,000 population (no free hours required for municipalities of 20,000 or fewer) — § 10(d)(iii). NOT chargeable: segregation/redaction time, unless the redaction is required by law or the Supervisor of Records approves it under § 10(d)(iv); this bar applies to municipalities as well as agencies. Above-$25/hour rates require Supervisor approval (§ 10(d)(iv)). 'Employee time' includes necessary vendors including outside counsel and IT consultants (§ 10(f)). Any labor fee must appear in the itemized good-faith written estimate in the 10-day letter (§ 10(b)(viii)) before work is billed.

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
Production may be conditioned on payment of a reasonable fee (§ 10(a)), and the RAO may in practice require payment of the itemized good-faith estimated fee before beginning reproduction work (Secretary's Guide; Supervisor practice — no express statutory prepayment clause was verified); there is no statutory percentage cap on the deposit. The estimate must be itemized (§ 10(b)(viii)). Fee disputes are appealable to the Supervisor as part of an appeal of the response within 90 calendar days (950 CMR 32.08(1); the regulation does not separately enumerate fee appeals, but Supervisor determinations routinely review fee estimates).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No mandatory statutory waiver. Section 10(d)(v) authorizes (does not require) the RAO to waive or reduce any fee upon a showing that disclosure is in the public interest AND not primarily in the commercial interest of the requester, or that the requester lacks the financial ability to pay. The Secretary's Guide states: 'All agencies and municipalities are strongly urged to waive the fees associated with access to public records, but are not required to do so under the law.' A court that awards attorney fees SHALL order the record fee waived, and may waive it even when fees are not awarded (§ 10A(d)(3)). All fees are automatically barred when the 10-business-day response was missed (§ 10(e)). Many cities waive fees for electronic records as a matter of practice.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
Statute is silent on closure; because production is conditioned on 'payment of a reasonable fee' (§ 10(a)) and estimated payment is required up front in practice, cities hold production until payment and administratively close requests as abandoned after a stated period (commonly 30 days) — practice, not statute. A requester who thinks the fee is unlawful should appeal to the Supervisor within 90 days of the response rather than not pay. Whether past unpaid balances from earlier requests can be demanded before honoring a new request is not addressed in the statute.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** No express statutory tolling clause. Functionally the clock pauses: (1) production is contingent on payment of the fee, so time awaiting payment of the estimate does not count against the production date (§ 10(a)-(b); Supervisor practice); (2) a request that does not 'reasonably describe' records does not trigger the production duty until clarified; (3) a timely § 10(b)(vi) letter lawfully extends municipal production to 25 business days (more if the requester voluntarily agrees), and a granted § 10(c) Supervisor petition adds a single extension of up to 30 more business days for a municipality. The 10-business-day first-response deadline itself is never tolled — missing it forfeits all fees (§ 10(e)) and is appealable as a non-response.

## REMEDIES

Superior court action under § 10A(c)-(d); de novo-style review with a codified presumption that each record sought is public and the burden on the municipality to prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the record may be withheld (§ 10A(d)). Attorney fees and costs: statutory PRESUMPTION in favor of award to a prevailing requester (§ 10A(d)(2)), overcome only if: the Supervisor found no violation; the municipality reasonably relied on a published appellate-court opinion on materially similar facts; it reasonably relied on a published Attorney General opinion on materially similar facts; the request was designed to harass or intimidate; or the request was for a commercial purpose not in the public interest. When fees/costs are awarded the court SHALL order the record fee waived; otherwise it may (§ 10A(d)(3)). Punitive damages of $1,000–$5,000 for bad-faith violations (withholding or unreasonable fees), deposited into the Public Records Assistance Fund established in c. 10, § 35DDD (§ 10A(d)(4)). No criminal penalty in § 10A; a legacy fine provision for custodial violations exists elsewhere in c. 66 (rarely used; not re-verified).

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for a Massachusetts city: (1) Identity fields optional — collect only a delivery email; NO residency field and no mandatory purpose field (950 CMR 32.06; Guide). (2) Include an optional purpose question only as a conditional module framed for the two lawful uses: commercial-purpose screening (feeds a possible § 10(d)(iv) Supervisor petition for >$25/hr rates) and fee-waiver requests (public interest + non-commercial, or inability to pay — § 10(d)(v)). (3) Auto-acknowledgment is fine but the compliance-critical timer is the 10-BUSINESS-DAY written response — build a hard deadline flag, because missing it forfeits ALL fees (§ 10(e)) and is appealable as a non-response within 90 days of the request. (4) Fee engine: $0.05/page paper; no per-page charge for electronic delivery (default per § 6A(d), searchable/machine-readable); labor at ≤$25/hr with the first 2 hours free (municipality >20,000 pop.; municipalities ≤20,000 configurable to charge from hour one); block any segregation/redaction-time line item unless tagged 'required by law' or 'Supervisor-approved' (§ 10(d)(ii)-(iii)); allow vendor/outside-counsel time under the § 10(f) employee-time definition. (5) Itemized good-faith estimate generator required in the 10-day letter (§ 10(b)(viii)); support prepayment holds and a pause-pending-payment state, plus an abandonment/closure timer (disclose the period in the letter — practice, not statute). (6) Denial letters must cite specific c. 4, § 7(26) exemptions with reasons and include Supervisor-of-Records and superior-court appeal language (§ 10(b)(iv), (ix)). (7) Litigation-exposure flags: late response (fee forfeiture + appealable non-response), attorney-fee presumption for prevailing requesters, mandatory record-fee waiver on fee award, $1,000–$5,000 bad-faith punitive damages. (8) Extension logic: self-extension to 25 business days via the 10-day letter (§ 10(b)(vi), longer if requester agrees); beyond that, a § 10(c) Supervisor petition (single extension, max 30 more business days for a municipality).
