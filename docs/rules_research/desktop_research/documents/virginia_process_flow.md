# Virginia City Public Records Request — Process Flow (Email-Based)

**Scope:** Requests for *copies* of records handled by email with a Virginia city, under the Virginia Freedom of Information Act (VFOIA) (Va. Code Ann. §§ 2.2-3700 through 2.2-3715). Inspection-of-records rules excluded per scope.

**Governing clock:** Virginia FOIA: substantive response due 5 working days after receipt; +7 work days by one written notice; silence = deemed denial. Virginia citizens & VA media only; fees = actual cost with no free hours; no admin appeal — court hearing within 7 days and near-mandatory attorney fees. (No 2024-2026 changes to deadlines or fees.)

---

## PHASE 1 — RECEIPT & INTAKE

**1.1 Request received by email.**
Residency-restricted: rights run to "citizens of the Commonwealth," representatives of newspapers/magazines with circulation in the Commonwealth, and radio/TV stations broadcasting in or into the Commonwealth (§ 2.2-3704(A)); the citizens-only limit was upheld in McBurney v. Young, 569 U.S. 221 (2013). VFOIA affords no rights to persons incarcerated in state, local, or federal correctional facilities or civilly committed under the Sexually Violent Predators Act (§ 2.2-3703(C); section most recently amended 2024 c. 366, 2025 c. 337). The custodian MAY require the requester's name and legal address (§ 2.2-3704(A)), so true anonymity is not guaranteed (though many cities don't verify). Purpose may not be demanded — FOIA Council guidance and RCFP: the purpose or motivation behind a request is irrelevant and cannot be required. Requests may be oral or written (email valid) and need not reference FOIA (§ 2.2-3704(B)); cities may voluntarily honor out-of-state requests.

**Commercial-purpose treatment:** None. VFOIA has no commercial-purpose disclosure requirement, no different deadline, and no different fee schedule for commercial requesters; commercial use is permitted without restriction (RCFP Va. guide). The only eligibility line is the Virginia-citizen/Virginia-media rule of § 2.2-3704(A), which is independent of commercial purpose.

**1.2 Log and acknowledge.**
No statutory acknowledgment duty. The only statutory obligation is the substantive 5-working-day response under § 2.2-3704(B). City practice: portal-based cities (e.g., Alexandria's GovQA system) auto-acknowledge receipt by email, but that is convenience, not law. One quasi-acknowledgment duty exists on the fee side: before conducting a search, the custodian must notify the requester that charges up to actual cost may apply and that the requester may ask for a cost estimate in advance (§ 2.2-3704(F), added by 2022 c. 756).

**1.3 Response deadline framework.**
Five working days from receipt (§ 2.2-3704(B), verified current through July 2026 — last amended 2023 c. 534): within 5 working days the city must either provide the requested records or make one of four written responses: (B)(1) records entirely withheld, citing with reasonable particularity the volume/subject matter and the specific Code section authorizing withholding; (B)(2) records provided in part and withheld in part, with the same citations; (B)(3) records could not be found or do not exist (with contact info for another public body known to have them); or (B)(4) it is not practically possible to respond within 5 working days — this written invocation, made within the initial 5 days and specifying the conditions, buys 7 additional work days (12 working days total without court involvement). For criminal investigative files under § 2.2-3706.1, the (B)(4) extension is 60 work days instead of 7 — rarely relevant to routine city requests. For extraordinary volume/scope where response would prevent the body from meeting its operational responsibilities, the city may petition the court for additional time, but must first make reasonable efforts to reach agreement with the requester (§ 2.2-3704(C)). "Failure to respond to a request for records shall be deemed a denial of the request and shall constitute a violation of this chapter" (§ 2.2-3704(E)). The deadline requires an actual determination/production (or a statutory written response), not merely an acknowledgment or estimate.

## PHASE 2 — PATH: VAGUE / OVERBROAD REQUEST

A request must "identify the requested records with reasonable specificity" (§ 2.2-3704(B), verified verbatim); a request lacking it does not trigger the 5-day clock, and bodies may decline overbroad requests on that ground. No express statutory duty to assist, but FOIA Council guidance and the policy of liberal construction (§ 2.2-3700(B)) push custodians to work with requesters to narrow scope. There is NO statutory tolling for clarification (only cost-estimate/deposit tolling under §§ 2.2-3704(F) and (H)), so common city practice of restarting the clock upon clarification rests on the "reasonable specificity" trigger, not an express tolling clause.

**No response from requester:** after a policy waiting period, closure notice ("administratively closed; may resubmit"). **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 3 — SEARCH & LOCATE

Reasonable, good-faith search; route to custodian department(s).

**PATH: NO RESPONSIVE RECORDS —** If records cannot be found or do not exist, the city must say so in writing within the deadline; if the city knows another public body has the records, the response must include contact information for that body (§ 2.2-3704(B)(3)). No duty to create a new record that does not exist; the body may abstract or summarize by agreement (§ 2.2-3704(D)). Database excerpts in formats used in the regular course of business must be produced (§ 2.2-3704(G)).

**PATH: Another agency's records —** courtesy referral. **[Practice]**

---

## PHASE 4 — REVIEW, REDACTION & THE DENIAL PATH

**4.1 Review & redaction.**
Strong segregability duty: § 2.2-3704.01 (enacted 2016 cc. 620, 716, overriding Dep't of Corrections v. Surovell, 290 Va. 255 (2015)) — a record may be withheld in its entirety only if an exclusion applies to its entire content; otherwise exempt portions must be redacted and the rest released. Redaction/review labor IS chargeable as part of "actual cost": FOIA Council opinions (e.g., AO-01-22) confirm time reviewing records for responsiveness and exclusion review is chargeable, at a rate reasonable for the level of work — higher-level (e.g., attorney) rates only where the request specifically requires that person; no overhead or surplus fees.

**4.2 PATH: DENIAL — required communications.**
Denials (full or partial) must be in writing within the 5/12-working-day window, must identify with reasonable particularity the volume and subject matter of withheld records, and must cite the specific Code section that authorizes withholding (§ 2.2-3704(B)(1)-(2)). Exemptions are discretionary and narrowly construed (§ 2.2-3700(B)). Silence = deemed denial and a per se violation (§ 2.2-3704(E)). No Vaughn-index requirement, but in litigation the public body bears the burden of establishing an exclusion by a preponderance of the evidence (§ 2.2-3713(E), verified).

**Administrative appeal:** None — judicial only. The Virginia Freedom of Information Advisory Council (Va. Code §§ 30-178 through 30-181) issues free, nonbinding advisory opinions (phone/email/written) and trains FOIA officers, but it cannot order release, has no appeal deadlines, and is optional; exhaustion is not required before suit. Good-faith reliance on a Council advisory opinion is a defense to willful-violation penalties (§ 2.2-3715). The Attorney General has no enforcement/appeal role for requesters.

---

## PHASE 5 — FEES, DEPOSITS & PAYMENT (summary level)

**5.1 Fee structure.**
No statutory per-page rate. § 2.2-3704(F) (verified verbatim): "reasonable charges not to exceed its actual cost incurred in accessing, duplicating, supplying, or searching for the requested records"; no "extraneous, intermediary, or surplus fees" to recoup general/overhead costs; cost recovery only, never revenue. Before searching, the custodian must notify the requester of possible charges and that an advance cost estimate is available on request (2022 c. 756). Electronic records: actual cost only, in any format used in the regular course of business (§ 2.2-3704(G)); FOIA Council guidance stresses electronic delivery cost is often nominal and cannot be billed at paper rates. Cities must post FOIA rights-and-responsibilities including a general description of charges (§ 2.2-3704.1). Typical city practice: roughly $0.05-$0.25/page copying plus pro-rated hourly salary+benefits of processing staff — rates vary by city and are policy, not statute. NOTE: SB 56 (2026, Roem), which would have capped labor charges at the median employee rate, passed the Senate unanimously in Feb. 2026 but was NOT enacted — § 2.2-3704 is unchanged (last amendment 2023 c. 534) as of July 2026.

**Labor charges:** Yes — staff time for search, access, responsiveness review, and exclusion/redaction review is chargeable from the first minute; Virginia has NO statutory free hours or free pages. Rate = actual pro-rated hourly cost (salary/benefits) of the employee doing the work; per FOIA Council AO-01-22, charging a higher-level employee's rate is reasonable only if there is a specific reason the request must be handled by that person — otherwise a lower rate should apply — and overhead may never be charged. Some cities voluntarily waive small bills (practice only).

**5.2 Deposits / prepayment.**
If the city determines in advance that charges are likely to exceed $200, it may, before continuing to process the request, require a deposit not to exceed the amount of the advance determination, credited to the final cost (§ 2.2-3704(H), verified verbatim). The response period is tolled for the time between the notice of the advance determination (or cost estimate, § 2.2-3704(F)) and the requester's response. If the requester does not respond within 30 days of the cost estimate being sent, the request is deemed withdrawn (§ 2.2-3704(F), 2022 c. 756).

**5.3 Fee waiver.**
No statutory public-interest or indigency waiver of any kind. Charging is permissive ("may make reasonable charges," § 2.2-3704(F)), so waivers are purely discretionary; in practice many Virginia cities do not bill small requests (RCFP: fees frequently are not imposed for small requests), and some post de minimis thresholds in their fee policies.

**5.4 PATH: NO PAYMENT.**
If a requester owes money on a prior request that has remained unpaid 30 days or more after billing, the city may require payment of the overdue bill before processing any new request (§ 2.2-3704(I), verified). Separately, failure to respond to a cost estimate within 30 days = request deemed withdrawn (§ 2.2-3704(F)); failure to pay a required >$200 deposit leaves the clock tolled indefinitely, effectively closing the request.

---

## PHASE 6 — FULFILLMENT & CLOSE

Deliver records by email or download link, with exemption citations for anything redacted or withheld. Closure email; retain the request file (the request and responses are themselves public records).

---

## DEADLINE & CLOCK SUMMARY

**Clock pauses / tolling:** (1) Advance cost estimate: the response period is tolled for the time between notice of the cost estimate and the requester's response (§ 2.2-3704(F), verified verbatim); 30 days of requester silence after the estimate = deemed withdrawal. (2) Deposit: for likely charges over $200, tolled between notice of the advance determination and the requester's response (§ 2.2-3704(H), verified verbatim). (3) Court-granted additional time for extraordinary volume, after reasonable efforts to agree with the requester (§ 2.2-3704(C)). No statutory tolling for clarification of vague requests — a request without reasonable specificity simply hasn't started the clock.

## REMEDIES

Petition for mandamus or injunction, supported by affidavit showing good cause, in the general district court or circuit court of the city (§ 2.2-3713(A)); expedited: the petition "shall be heard within seven days of the date when the same is made," and the petitioner must provide the public body a copy of the petition at least 3 working days before filing (§ 2.2-3713(C), verified — the 3-day notice precedes filing, not the hearing). Public body bears the burden of establishing any exclusion by a preponderance of the evidence (§ 2.2-3713(E)). Attorney fees, costs, and reasonable expert-witness fees are near-mandatory: awarded if the petitioner "substantially prevails on the merits of the case, unless special circumstances would make an award unjust" (§ 2.2-3713(D), verified verbatim). Civil penalties against individual officers/employees for willful and knowing violations: $500-$2,000 first offense, $2,000-$5,000 subsequent (§ 2.2-3714(A), verified); up to $100 per record for altering/destroying records with intent to evade FOIA (§ 2.2-3714(B)); penalties paid into the Literary Fund. No criminal provisions.

## PORTAL DESIGN NOTES

Portal design for Virginia cities: (1) Identity — collect name and legal (physical) address; § 2.2-3704(A) lets the city require both, and eligibility screening (Virginia citizen or Virginia media) may be needed; consider a citizenship attestation checkbox rather than proof. (2) Do NOT ask purpose — purpose is legally irrelevant and demanding it invites complaints; no commercial-rate module needed (no commercial branch in VFOIA). (3) Auto-acknowledgment is fine but the SLA timer must target a substantive determination at 5 working days, with a one-click written +7-work-day extension notice (§ 2.2-3704(B)(4)) generated inside the initial window. (4) Fee module: actual-cost line items only (no overhead), mandatory pre-search charge notice, estimate-on-request workflow, $200 threshold gating an up-to-estimate deposit, clock-tolling while estimate/deposit response is pending, 30-day auto-withdraw on estimate silence, and a prior-unpaid-balance (30+ days) block on new requests. (5) Fee-waiver question is optional/discretionary — no statutory waiver to administer. (6) Litigation-exposure flags: missed day-5 = deemed denial (per se violation), 7-day court hearing pace, near-mandatory attorney fees, and $500-$2,000 personal civil penalties for willful violations — escalate any request approaching day 5 without a determination. (7) Publish the city's FOIA rights-and-responsibilities statement, fee description, and FOIA officer contact (§§ 2.2-3704.1, 2.2-3704.2). (8) Monitor the 2027 session: SB 56-style labor-rate caps passed the Senate unanimously in 2026 and will likely return.

**Uncertainties / verify locally:** (1) 2026 legislation: the FOIA Council's 2026 legislative update lists 13 enacted FOIA bills (all exemption/meetings-related, none amending § 2.2-3704 or § 2.2-3713 procedure/fees), and the current code text of § 2.2-3704 shows no amendment after 2023 c. 534 — so SB 56 (Roem, fee caps), which passed the Senate unanimously in Feb. 2026, evidently died in the House or on the Governor's desk; I could not retrieve the bill's final disposition record directly (LIS/LegiScan/Richmond Sunlight blocked), so the precise failure point is unconfirmed, but its non-enactment is corroborated by two primary sources. (2) Per-page copy rates are pure city practice; the $0.05-$0.25/page range is an informed practice estimate, not a citation. (3) The claim that 'secondary legal review' is never chargeable could not be confirmed from a retrievable FOIA Council document; AO-01-22 instead frames it as: exclusion review is chargeable, but higher-level/attorney rates are reasonable only when that person's involvement is specifically required — treat the flat 'never chargeable' version as unverified. (4) Whether clarification of a vague request formally restarts vs. never-started the 5-day clock is FOIA Council interpretation/practice, not express statute. (5) The exact substance of the 2024 c. 366 and 2025 c. 337 amendments to § 2.2-3703 (requester-eligibility section) was not retrievable; the incarcerated-person and SVP-committee exclusions in § 2.2-3703(C) are confirmed from current code text. (6) Whether the 30-day deemed-withdrawal rule of subsection (F) also applies to unpaid (H) deposits (vs. indefinite tolling) is not explicit in the retrieved text.
